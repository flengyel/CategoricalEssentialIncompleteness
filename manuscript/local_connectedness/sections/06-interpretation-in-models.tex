\section{Interpretation and soundness}\label{sec:models}

In $\Pfn$, restriction products are Cartesian products and coproducts
are tagged disjoint unions.

Fix a model $M\models T$. Assign a set $X_M$ to each term
$X\in\mathsf O$ by
\[
 X_M=|M|\quad(X=\omega),\qquad
 (X\times Y)_M=X_M\times Y_M,\qquad
 (X+Y)_M=X_M\amalg Y_M.
\]
The recursive coding for each $X\in\mathsf O$ induces a bijection
$\gamma_X^M:X_M\to|M|$: use the identity for $\omega$, the
interpreted pairing for products, and the interpreted even and odd
codings for the two coproduct summands. The PA-provable coding
identities give the inverses. For $e\in\mathcal I(X,Y)$
and elements $x\in X_M$, $y\in Y_M$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model-functor}
 \begin{aligned}
 F_M(X)&=X_M,\\
 F_M([e]_T)(x)=y
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 M\models G_e\bigl(\gamma_X^M(x),\gamma_Y^M(y)\bigr).
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The standard index $e$ in $G_e$ is interpreted as its numeral in $M$.
All quantified traces and intermediate values in this formula range over
the whole model, including its nonstandard elements.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:model}
For every $M\models T$, \eqref{eq:model-functor} defines a covariant
functor $F_M:S_T\to\Pfn$ preserving restrictions, zeros, binary
restriction products and coproducts, ranges, and finite joins of
restriction idempotents.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Functionality is provable in $\PA$, so the interpreted graph is a
partial function. Equation \eqref{eq:uniform} makes the assignment
independent of the representative. Equation \eqref{eq:composition},
interpreted in $M$, quantifies over an intermediate arithmetic code.
The bijection $\gamma_Y^M$ converts that code into the intermediate
element of $Y_M$, proving preservation of composition. The identity
graph gives the identity function on $X_M$.

The definedness predicate $\exists y\,G_e(x,y)$ gives the interpreted
function's domain. Similarly,
\[
 \{y\in Y_M:M\models R_e(\gamma_Y^M(y))\}
 =\operatorname{im}(F_M[e]_T),
\]
so ranges are preserved. False predicates, conjunction, and disjunction
give zeros, intersections, and unions. The PA-provable product and
tagged-coproduct graph equations give the corresponding operations on
partial functions.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Lifting complementary conditionals}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:switch}
Let $c,c'$ be complementary restriction idempotents on $X$ in $S_T$.
There is a total morphism $s:X\to X+X$ with
\[
 sc=i_1c,\qquad sc'=i_0c'.
\]
For every $M\models T$, the morphism $\chi_{X,X}^{-1}F_Ms$ is the
unique section of $\nabla_{F_MX}(F_Mc'+F_Mc)$, where
$\chi_{X,X}:F_MX+F_MX\to F_M(X+X)$ is the coproduct isomorphism.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose $\Sigma_1$ representatives $C(x)=\exists u\,R(x,u)$ and
$C'(x)=\exists v\,R'(x,v)$ with primitive-recursive witness tests.
The theory $T$ proves universally their disjointness and coverage.
For $j=0,1$, put $\iota_j(x)=2x+j$. The graph
$z=\iota_j(x)$ represents the coproduct injection
$i_j:X\to X+X$ on numerical codes.
Search increasing finite bounds for a witness to either predicate,
returning $\iota_1(x)$ on finding a $C$-witness and $\iota_0(x)$
on finding a $C'$-witness. Fix a priority for simultaneous discoveries.
Let $e_s$ be an index of this program. The finite-search verification
in $\PA$ proves that the program halts
exactly when $C(x)\lor C'(x)$. Under the disjointness assumption it also
proves the exact graph equation
\[
 G_{e_s}(x,z)\leftrightarrow
 (C(x)\land z=\iota_1(x))\lor(C'(x)\land z=\iota_0(x)).
\]
Thus $T$ proves this equation and universal halting. The equations for
the restrictions follow, giving the total morphism $s=[e_s]_T$ of $S_T$.

Interpret this proved graph identity in $M\models T$.
Under the canonical coproduct identification, $F_M(s)$ sends
$x$ to its second tagged copy when
$M\models C(\gamma_X^M(x))$ and to its first tagged copy when
$M\models C'(\gamma_X^M(x))$.
These conditions partition $X_M$, so
$\chi_{X,X}^{-1}F_M(s)$ is the partial inverse of
$\nabla_{X_M}(F_M(c')+F_M(c))$. The latter partial function is injective
on its domain and has range $X_M$, so its partial inverse is its
unique section.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:model-pair}
For every $M\models T$, the functor $F_M$ satisfies the hypotheses of
Corollary~\ref{cor:source-conditionals}. Thus, for a Turing morphism
$\tau:\omega\times\omega\rightharpoonup\omega$ and an isomorphism
$b:\omega\to\omega+\omega$ in $S_T$, the restriction idempotents
\[
 (b\tau\Delta_\omega)^*(1_\omega+0_\omega),\qquad
 (b\tau\Delta_\omega)^*(0_\omega+1_\omega)
\]
are inseparable in $S_T$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
In $\Pfn$, total inverse images preserve binary joins of restriction
idempotents. Proposition~\ref{prop:model} supplies the range functor,
Lemma~\ref{lem:switch} supplies the total source conditionals, and
$F_M(1_\omega)\ne0$ because $|M|$ is nonempty.
Corollary~\ref{cor:source-conditionals} applies.
\end{proof}


\subsection{The total interpretation functor}

For a model $M\models T$, split each interpreted restriction
idempotent in $\Pfn$ by its domain inclusion. Restricting the resulting
functor $\widehat F_M:\Split(S_T)\to\Pfn$ to total morphisms gives
\[
 G_M:\mathcal B_T\longrightarrow\mathbf{Set},
 \qquad \mathcal B_T=\Tot(\Split(S_T)).
\]
Let $J_T:\mathcal P_T\to\mathcal B_T$ denote the equivalence
constructed in Lemma~\ref{lem:syntactic-total-category}. Thus
$J_T(C)=(\omega,[C]_T)$. With the indicated splittings,
\[
 G_MJ_T(C)=C(M):=\{a\in |M|:M\models C(a)\},
\]
and $G_MJ_T(\Gamma):C(M)\to D(M)$ is the function whose graph
is the interpretation of $\Gamma$ in $M$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:model-total-regular}
For every $M\models T$, the functor $G_M$ preserves finite limits,
finite coproducts, and regular images.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
It suffices to work along the equivalence $J_T$.
The terminal predicate $x=0$ is interpreted as a singleton.
The numerical pairing bijection identifies the interpretation of
the product predicate with $C(M)\times D(M)$. For functional graphs
$\Gamma,\Delta:C\to D$, the equalizer predicate
\[
 C(x)\land\exists y\,
             \bigl(\Gamma(x,y)\land\Delta(x,y)\bigr)
\]
is interpreted as the equalizer of the two functions. Hence $G_M$
preserves finite limits. The even and odd coding bijections identify
the interpreted coproduct predicate with the tagged disjoint union;
the false predicate is interpreted as the empty set. Finally,
the regular image of $\Gamma:C\to D$ has predicate
$\exists x\,\Gamma(x,y)$. Its interpretation is exactly the image
of the interpreted function, since the quantified variable ranges
over all of $|M|$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Soundness on standard codes}

Fix the arithmetic signature and the variable object
$V=(\omega,1_\omega)\in\mathcal B_{\PA}$, so that
$G_{\N}V=\N$. Use the syntax and derivation objects
constructed in Section~\ref{sec:st-parameterized-syntax}. Let
$S\hookrightarrow\Fm$ be the sentence object. Let $\Gamma$ be
a computably enumerable set of arithmetic sentences, choose a
$\Sigma_1$ predicate $\operatorname{Ax}_\Gamma(z)$ enumerating
their codes, and let
\[
 i_A:A=A_\Gamma\hookrightarrow S
\]
be the subobject with carrier predicate
$\operatorname{Sent}(z)\land\operatorname{Ax}_\Gamma(z)$. Write
\[
 c_A:P_A\longrightarrow\Fm,\qquad
 c_{A,S}:P_{A,S}\longrightarrow S
\]
for the full conclusion morphism and its pullback to sentences.
The standard interpretation of $i_A$ is the inclusion of the
codes of the sentences in $\Gamma$.

For $s\in G_{\N}S$, let $\varphi_s$ be the finite sentence with
code $s$. Let $K\models\Gamma$ be a nonempty first-order
structure for the arithmetic signature, with equality interpreted
as identity. Define a subobject in $\mathbf{Set}$ by
\[
 v_K:V_K\hookrightarrow G_{\N}S,
 \qquad
 V_K=\{s\in G_{\N}S:K\models\varphi_s\}.
\]
The arithmetic model $\N$ interprets the code objects and their
morphisms; the structure $K$ interprets the decoded arithmetic
formulas.

Let $j_{\Adm}:\Adm\hookrightarrow\Fm\times V\times\Tm$
be the admissibility inclusion in $\mathcal B_{\PA}$.
Composing $G_{\N}(j_{\Adm})$ with the canonical product
comparison gives an injection of sets
\[
 a_{\N}:G_{\N}\Adm\hookrightarrow
             G_{\N}\Fm\times\N\times G_{\N}\Tm.
\]
For a finite term $t$ and formula $\varphi$, their codes belong
to $G_{\N}\Tm$ and $G_{\N}\Fm$, respectively.

\begin{lemma}[Semantic substitution]\label{lem:semantic-substitution}
Let $\sigma:\N\to |K|$ be an assignment of values to variables.
For $x\in\N$ and $a\in|K|$, write
$\sigma[x\mapsto a]:\N\to|K|$ for the assignment obtained
by changing the value of $x$ to $a$. For finite terms $r,t$,
\[
 \llbracket r[x:=t]\rrbracket^K_\sigma
 =\llbracket r\rrbracket^K_{
          \sigma[x\mapsto\llbracket t\rrbracket^K_\sigma]}.
\]
For a finite formula $\varphi$, if
$(\ulcorner\varphi\urcorner,x,\ulcorner t\urcorner)$ lies in
the image of $a_{\N}$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:semantic-substitution}
 K,\sigma\models\varphi[x:=t]
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 K,\sigma[x\mapsto\llbracket t\rrbracket^K_\sigma]
             \models\varphi.
\end{equation}
Here the expressions and substitutions are the finite expressions
decoded from the realized syntax objects.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First, term values and formula truth depend only on the values
assigned to their free variables. For a variable this is the
definition of its value. The induction steps for function and
relation symbols, equality, negation, and implication follow from
their semantic clauses. For $\forall y\,\psi$, assignments agreeing
on its free variables yield, after changing $y$ to the same
$b\in|K|$, assignments agreeing on the free variables of $\psi$.
The induction hypothesis for $\psi$ therefore proves the assertion
for the quantified formula. The free-variable lists realized in
Proposition~\ref{prop:st-substitution-realization} have precisely
these constructor equations.

Induction on $r$ proves the term identity. At a variable it is the
two-case definition of substitution; at a function application it
follows by applying the interpretation of the function symbol to
the child identities. The atomic cases of
\eqref{eq:semantic-substitution} follow from this term identity.
Negation and implication follow from their truth clauses and the
induction hypotheses.

Consider $\varphi=\forall y\,\psi$ and put
$a=\llbracket t\rrbracket^K_\sigma$. If $y=x$, substitution leaves
$\varphi$ unchanged. The values of
$\sigma[x\mapsto b]$ and
$\sigma[x\mapsto a][x\mapsto b]$ agree for every $b$, proving
the required equivalence. Suppose $y\ne x$. If $x$ is not free
in $\psi$, substitution leaves $\psi$ unchanged, by
Proposition~\ref{prop:st-substitution-realization}; the free-variable
assertion just proved gives the equivalence. Otherwise admissibility
requires that $y$ is not free in $t$ and that substitution into
$\psi$ is admissible. For every $b\in|K|$, the free-variable
assertion gives
$\llbracket t\rrbracket^K_{\sigma[y\mapsto b]}=a$, and hence
\[
 \sigma[y\mapsto b][x\mapsto a]
   =\sigma[x\mapsto a][y\mapsto b].
\]
Apply the induction hypothesis to $\psi$ at
$\sigma[y\mapsto b]$ and then quantify over $b$. These are all
the admissible cases in the quantified-constructor equation.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Soundness on standard codes]
\label{prop:standard-code-soundness}
Let $\Gamma$ be a computably enumerable set of arithmetic sentences,
with axiom subobject $i_A:A\hookrightarrow S$ in
$\mathcal B_{\PA}$, and let $K\models\Gamma$.
There is a unique function
\[
 e_K:G_{\N}P_{A,S}\longrightarrow V_K
 \quad\text{such that}\quad
 v_Ke_K=G_{\N}c_{A,S}.
\]
If $\nu:S\to S$ is sentence negation, then the subobjects
$v_K$ and $(G_{\N}\nu)^*v_K$ of $G_{\N}S$ have empty
intersection.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Call a finite formula valid in $K$ if it holds under every
assignment $\N\to|K|$. We check validity for the seven logical
axiom schemes of Appendix~\ref{app:first-order-derivations}.
The first three are the displayed classical propositional
tautologies: the first retains its antecedent under a further
implication, the second composes two implications with the same
antecedent, and the third expresses contraposition. Their truth
follows in each assignment from the two-valued clauses for
negation and implication.

For universal instantiation, if $\forall x\,\varphi$ holds at
$\sigma$, then $\varphi$ holds at
$\sigma[x\mapsto\llbracket t\rrbracket^K_\sigma]$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:semantic-substitution} gives
$\varphi[x:=t]$ at $\sigma$, since its parameter object imposes
admissibility. For universal distribution, suppose
$\forall x(\varphi\Rightarrow\psi)$ and $\varphi$ hold at
$\sigma$. The side condition says that $x$ is not free in
$\varphi$. Thus $\varphi$ holds at $\sigma[x\mapsto b]$ for
every $b\in|K|$, and the universally quantified implication gives
$\psi$ there. This proves $\forall x\,\psi$ at $\sigma$.
Reflexivity holds because the two occurrences of a term have the
same value. Finally, if $u=t$ holds at $\sigma$, the assignments
obtained by assigning $x$ the values of $u$ and $t$ are equal.
Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:semantic-substitution} to both admissible
substitutions proves the equality-substitution axiom.

Every nonlogical axiom is a sentence satisfied by $K$, and hence
is valid under every assignment. Modus ponens preserves validity
by the truth clause for implication. Generalization preserves
validity because validity of $\varphi$ includes its truth at
$\sigma[x\mapsto b]$ for every assignment $\sigma$ and every
$b\in|K|$.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:syntactic-derivation-initiality}, each element
of $G_{\N}P_A$ is the code of a finite derivation tree. Its
parameter at each node satisfies the indicated rule predicate;
the child conclusions are the premises of that rule. Induction
on the tree, using the preceding checks, shows that its root
formula is valid in $K$. Restriction to the pullback
$G_{\N}P_{A,S}$ gives a sentence satisfied by $K$.
Consequently $G_{\N}c_{A,S}$ takes values in $V_K$; its
corestriction is $e_K$. The inclusion $v_K$ is monic, proving
uniqueness.

By Proposition~\ref{prop:st-substitution-realization} and the
constructor realization, $G_{\N}\nu$ sends a sentence code to
the code of its negation. A sentence and its negation cannot
both hold in $K$. This proves the empty-intersection assertion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Ultrapowers of the code interpretation}

Work in the usual set-theoretic metatheory with choice. Fix a
nonempty set $I$ and a proper ultrafilter $\mathcal U$ on $I$.
For a set $X$, let
\[
 \mathscr U(X)=X^I/{\sim_{\mathcal U}},
 \qquad
 (x_i)\sim_{\mathcal U}(y_i)
 \Longleftrightarrow\{i:x_i=y_i\}\in\mathcal U.
\]
Here $X^I$ is the set of functions from $I$ to $X$. For a
function $f:X\to Y$, put
$\mathscr U(f)[x_i]=[f(x_i)]$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:ultrapower-regular}
The functor $\mathscr U:\mathbf{Set}\to\mathbf{Set}$ preserves
finite limits, finite coproducts, and regular images.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The empty set and singleton are preserved. Coordinatewise pairing
gives a bijection
$\mathscr U(X\times Y)\cong\mathscr U(X)\times\mathscr U(Y)$.
Let $E\hookrightarrow X$ be the equalizer of $f,g:X\to Y$.
An element $[x_i]\in\mathscr U(X)$ equalizes
$\mathscr U(f)$ and $\mathscr U(g)$ precisely when
$J=\{i:x_i\in E\}$ belongs to $\mathcal U$. In that case
$J$ is nonempty, so choose $e\in E$ and replace $x_i$ by $e$
outside $J$. This gives a representative in $E^I$. Conversely,
every representative in $E^I$ equalizes the two functions.
The induced map from $\mathscr U(E)$ is injective by the
definition of $\sim_{\mathcal U}$. Hence equalizers, and
therefore finite limits, are preserved.

For a family in $X\amalg Y$, exactly one of the sets of indices
carrying the two tags belongs to $\mathcal U$. Retain that
summand on this set and fill the other coordinates with one
element of the same summand. The summand is nonempty because
the retained set is nonempty. This proves surjectivity of
\[
 \mathscr U(X)\amalg\mathscr U(Y)
       \longrightarrow\mathscr U(X\amalg Y).
\]
The two tags cannot agree on a member of $\mathcal U$, and
equality within a tag is exactly $\sim_{\mathcal U}$. Thus this
map is bijective. Finally, every surjection of sets has a section,
so its image under $\mathscr U$ is surjective. Finite-limit
preservation preserves monomorphisms; applying $\mathscr U$ to
the surjection--inclusion factorization of a function therefore
preserves its regular image.
\end{proof}

Let $M=\N^I/\mathcal U$ be the arithmetic ultrapower.
\L o\'s's theorem~\cite[Theorem~3.1]{Keisler}
gives $M\models\PA$.

\begin{lemma}[Comparison with the arithmetic ultrapower]
\label{lem:ultrapower-comparison}
There is a natural isomorphism of functors
\[
 \eta:\mathscr U G_{\N}\xRightarrow{\ \cong\ }G_M:
             \mathcal B_{\PA}\longrightarrow\mathbf{Set}.
\]
On a predicate object $J_{\PA}(C)$ it is the map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ultrapower-predicate-comparison}
 \eta_{J_{\PA}(C)}:
 \mathscr U(C(\N))\longrightarrow C(M),
 \qquad [c_i]\longmapsto[c_i].
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By \L o\'s's theorem, the displayed map is well defined and
injective. If $a=[a_i]\in C(M)$, then
$J=\{i:\N\models C(a_i)\}$ belongs to $\mathcal U$.
Choose $c\in C(\N)$ from one coordinate in $J$, and replace
$a_i$ by $c$ outside $J$. The resulting family belongs to
$C(\N)^I$ and represents $a$, proving surjectivity. If
$C(\N)$ is empty, \L o\'s's theorem gives $C(M)=\varnothing$,
so the same conclusion holds.

For a morphism represented by a functional graph
$\Gamma:C\to D$, let $d_i$ be its value at $c_i$ in $\N$.
Then $\N\models\Gamma(c_i,d_i)$ for every $i$.
\L o\'s's theorem and functionality in $M$ show that the value
at $[c_i]$ in $M$ is $[d_i]$. Thus the displayed maps are
natural on $\mathcal P_{\PA}$.

To define the comparison on $\mathcal B_{\PA}$, for each
object $B$ choose an isomorphism
$u_B:J_{\PA}(C_B)\xrightarrow{\cong}B$, as supplied by
Lemma~\ref{lem:syntactic-total-category}, and set
\[
 \eta_B=G_M(u_B)\,
       \eta_{J_{\PA}(C_B)}\,
       \mathscr U(G_{\N}(u_B^{-1})):
       \mathscr U(G_{\N}B)\longrightarrow G_MB.
\]
Fullness of $J_{\PA}$ and the naturality already proved imply
naturality for every $f:B\to B'$ in $\mathcal B_{\PA}$:
\[
 G_M(f)\eta_B=\eta_{B'}\mathscr U(G_{\N}(f)):
       \mathscr U(G_{\N}B)\longrightarrow G_MB'.
\]
They also
show that changing $u_B$ does not change $\eta_B$: the transition
isomorphism between two predicate presentations is a morphism in
the image of $J_{\PA}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Soundness in the arithmetic ultrapower]
\label{thm:ultrapower-soundness}
Let $i_A:A\hookrightarrow S$ and $K$ satisfy the hypotheses of
Proposition~\ref{prop:standard-code-soundness}, and let
$\nu:S\to S$ be syntactic negation in $\mathcal B_{\PA}$.
Let $I$ be nonempty,
let $\mathcal U$ be a proper ultrafilter on $I$, and put
$M=\N^I/\mathcal U$. Define
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 V_{K,\mathcal U}&=\mathscr U(V_K),\\
 v_{K,\mathcal U}
   &=\eta_S\mathscr U(v_K):V_{K,\mathcal U}\hookrightarrow G_MS,\\
 e_{K,\mathcal U}
   &=\mathscr U(e_K)\eta_{P_{A,S}}^{-1}:
       G_MP_{A,S}\longrightarrow V_{K,\mathcal U}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ultrapower-soundness-factorization}
 v_{K,\mathcal U}e_{K,\mathcal U}=G_Mc_{A,S},
\end{equation}
and $v_{K,\mathcal U}$ has empty intersection with its inverse
image along $G_M\nu$. Consequently the interpreted theorem
subobject and its inverse image along $G_M\nu$ have empty
intersection. Equivalently, for the restriction idempotent
$t_A=\ran(c_{A,S})$ in $\Split(S_{\PA})$,
\[
 \widehat F_M(t_A)\,
       (\widehat F_M\nu)^*\widehat F_M(t_A)=0.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:ultrapower-regular} shows that
$\mathscr U(v_K)$ is monic, so $v_{K,\mathcal U}$ is monic.
Apply $\mathscr U$ to the factorization in
Proposition~\ref{prop:standard-code-soundness}. Naturality of
$\eta$ for $c_{A,S}$ gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 v_{K,\mathcal U}e_{K,\mathcal U}
 &=\eta_S\mathscr U(v_Ke_K)\eta_{P_{A,S}}^{-1}\\
 &=\eta_S\mathscr U(G_{\N}c_{A,S})
                          \eta_{P_{A,S}}^{-1}
   =G_Mc_{A,S}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The intersection of $v_K$ with $(G_{\N}\nu)^*v_K$ is a
finite limit with empty carrier. Its image under $\mathscr U$
again has empty carrier. Naturality for $\nu$, together with
the isomorphism $\eta_S$, identifies this limit with the
intersection asserted for $v_{K,\mathcal U}$.

The factorization places the regular image of $G_Mc_{A,S}$
inside $v_{K,\mathcal U}$. Proposition~\ref{prop:model-total-regular}
identifies this image with the interpreted theorem subobject;
Proposition~\ref{prop:interpreted-theory} identifies its partial
identity with $\widehat F_M(t_A)$. Taking inverse images along
$G_M\nu$ and using the preceding disjointness proves both
conclusions.
\end{proof}

For $s\in G_MS$, choose a family $(s_i)_{i\in I}$ in
$G_{\N}S$ such that $s=\eta_S([s_i])$.
Then
\[
 s\in\im(v_{K,\mathcal U})
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \{i:K\models\varphi_{s_i}\}\in\mathcal U.
\]
Indeed, membership on a set in $\mathcal U$ permits replacing
all other coordinates by one member of $V_K$, and the reverse
implication follows from a representative in $V_K^I$.
This description is independent of representatives because two
representatives agree on a set in $\mathcal U$. On a constant
standard sentence code it agrees with satisfaction in $K$.

Likewise, for $p\in G_MP_{A,S}$, choose a family $(p_i)_{i\in I}$
in $G_{\N}P_{A,S}$ such that $p=\eta_{P_{A,S}}([p_i])$.
Each $p_i$ is the code of a standard finite derivation with sentence conclusion,
and the definition of the factorization gives
\[
 e_{K,\mathcal U}(p)=[e_K(p_i)].
\]
