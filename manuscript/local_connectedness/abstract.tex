\begin{abstract}
We construct internal deductive systems for propositional and first-order
logic within completions of recursion categories, addressing
Di Paola and Heller's objective to formulate generalized incompleteness
in categorical algebra. In each system, chosen axioms generate a subobject
of derivable sentences. A categorical comparison theorem connects semantic
soundness with incompleteness, using total reductions and a lift of a partial
conditional. Total reductions of inseparable pairs give incompleteness
for consistent extensions in the specified effective classes.
We realize first-order logic with equality in completions of syntactic
recursion categories. For every consistent computably enumerable extension
of Peano arithmetic, we recover recursive inseparability of its theorem and
refutation sets and construct an independent sentence uniformly from an
index for its axiom enumeration.
A second first-order realization uses a recursion category constructed
from a Miller sequence. It gives a consistent theory of real closed fields
expanded by constants naming the sequence. Every consistent extension whose
axiom set is semidecidable by one oracle program from every binary
representation of the sequence is incomplete.
The completion of a recursion category constructed from cosheaves on a
three-point space realizes propositional logic. It gives two propositional
theories whose consistent computably enumerable extensions in the same
language are incomplete.
\end{abstract}
