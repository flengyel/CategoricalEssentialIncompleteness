\begin{abstract}
We construct logical consequence in categories of total morphisms in
restriction splittings of recursion categories, under regularity and initial-algebra
hypotheses. Initial algebras specify syntax and finite derivations, and
regular images of conclusion morphisms give the least deductively closed
theories containing prescribed axioms. A comparison theorem for range
functors derives incompleteness from semantic soundness, reductions, and
lifts of partial morphisms into binary coproducts. A total reduction of an
inseparable pair to a theory's provable and refutable sentences forces
every consistent represented extension to be incomplete. In arithmetic,
we recover recursive inseparability of the theorem and refutation sets
of every consistent computably enumerable extension of Peano arithmetic.
For each such extension, an independent sentence is constructed uniformly
from an index for its axiom enumeration.
We prove soundness by interpreting syntax codes in standard arithmetic
and evaluating formulas in a model of the represented axioms, and
transport the resulting factorization to arithmetic ultrapowers.
From a Miller sequence we construct a recursion category and a
consistent theory of real closed fields. Every consistent extension
whose axiom set is semidecidable by one oracle program from every binary
representation of the sequence is incomplete. A recursion category
constructed from cosheaves on a three-point space yields two
propositional theories whose consistent computably enumerable
extensions in the same language are incomplete.
\end{abstract}
