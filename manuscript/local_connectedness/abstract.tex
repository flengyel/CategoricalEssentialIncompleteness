\begin{abstract}
We prove an inseparability theorem for a recursion category equipped
with a functor into a locally connected restriction category.
The target is split, satisfies weak choice, and has inverse images
preserving binary joins of restriction idempotents. The functor
preserves restrictions, ranges, zeros, and binary restriction coproducts,
and satisfies a lifting condition for sections of partial monomorphisms.
Preservation of products is not required. A counterexample shows that
the lifting condition cannot be omitted, even when the functor is
faithful, preserves products, and reflects zeros.

For Miller's sequence $a:\N\to[0,1]$, we construct a recursion category
whose arrows are partial numerical functions computed uniformly from
every binary representation of $a$. The sets
$\{n:a(n)<1/2\}$ and $\{n:a(n)>1/2\}$ are inseparable in this category,
although each fixed representation computes a separator. A recursive
reduction from the pair obtained by the inseparability theorem proves
this assertion. These sets yield essential incompleteness, relative
to computation independent of the representation, for a theory of real
closed fields with constants specifying the rational cuts of $a$.

We also construct faithful interpretations in models of the syntactic
recursion categories associated with consistent extensions of Peano
arithmetic. The theorem gives inseparable restriction idempotents in
these categories. Its numerical instance yields recursive inseparability
of the theorem and refutation sets of every consistent computably
enumerable extension of Peano arithmetic. A cosheaf construction gives
a recursion category not equivalent, as a restriction category, to the
classical one, and a consistent propositional theory whose consistent
extensions represented by restriction idempotents are all incomplete.
\end{abstract}
