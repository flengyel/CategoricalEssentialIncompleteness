\begin{abstract}
We construct internal deductive systems for propositional and first-order
logic within completions of recursion categories and use them to prove
essential incompleteness, addressing Di Paola and Heller's objective to
formulate generalized incompleteness in categorical algebra.
We realize first-order logic with equality in completions of the syntactic
recursion categories of Di Paola and Montagna. For every consistent
computably enumerable extension of Peano arithmetic, we recover recursive
inseparability of its theorem and refutation sets.
A second first-order realization uses a recursion category constructed
from a Miller sequence. It gives a consistent theory of real closed fields
expanded by constants naming the sequence. Every consistent extension whose
axiom set is semidecidable by one oracle program from every binary
representation of the sequence is incomplete.
The completion of a recursion category constructed from cosheaves on a
three-point space realizes propositional logic. It gives two propositional
theories whose consistent computably enumerable extensions in the same
language are incomplete.
\end{abstract}
