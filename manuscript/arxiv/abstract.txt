From a recursion category we construct, under additional hypotheses, internal first-order theories in its restriction idempotent completion. Among disjoint pairs of restriction idempotents generating consistent theories, exactly the inseparable pairs generate essentially incomplete ones: theories with no complete consistent internal extension in their language. Di Paola and Heller proved diagonal domains inseparable toward an algebraization of incompleteness that never appeared. A comparison theorem for range functors gives conditions for diagonal pairs of Turing morphisms to be inseparable; we apply such pairs to theories for a categorical form of Smullyan's criterion: a consistent theory strongly separating a recursively inseparable pair has recursively inseparable theorems and refutations. A disjoint pair $(a,b)$ specifies two families of propositional atoms. The least theory proving the first and refuting the second is $T_{a,b}$. The construction is left adjoint to the functor giving theorem and refutation subobjects, so translations from $T_{a,b}$ into $T$ correspond to assignments of sentences of $T$ sending $a$ into theorems and $b$ into refutations. When $a,b$ are inseparable, every consistent theory receiving such a translation is essentially incomplete. Each theory determines a recursion category over its theorem object; under further hypotheses, the theory is essentially incomplete exactly when its copy in the completion is. Theory extensions induce fully faithful functors between these completions, adjoint on both sides to pullback. We apply this criterion in three recursion categories: Montagna's $S_{\mathrm{PA}}$ (for consistent PA), uniform computations on all binary representations of a Miller sequence, and one built from diagrams of c.e. sets presenting cosheaves on a three-point space. In each we prove an internal theory consistent and essentially incomplete.
