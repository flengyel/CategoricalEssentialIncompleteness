# (B) Major weaknesses

No unresolved major mathematical weakness was identified in the principal theorems. One local identification in Appendix A.2 was repaired during the review; the corrected statement has been checked:

- **Anchor:** Appendix A.2, “Numerical deduction stages,” p. 18; the former sentence “Thus the abstract closure is Cn on c.e. formula sets.”
- **Claim as first written:** The axiom domain contains both fixed nonlogical axioms and tautologies, but the resulting operation on an arbitrary input set is identified with the previously defined purely logical consequence operator `Cn`.
- **Failure mode:** **false**, locally.
- **Evidence:** **derivation**. If the fixed nonlogical axioms are `Γ₀`, the stage construction sends `A` to `Cn(Γ₀ ∪ A)`, not to `Cn(A)`. Taking `A` empty and a non-tautological axiom from `Γ₀` exhibits the difference.
- **Downstream impact:** The numerical identification of the background closure operator and its fixed points. The proof of numerical essential incompleteness is independent of this identification.
- **Severity and resolution:** Major for that local comparison; repaired and verified. The final text states the relative operator `A ↦ Cn(Γ₀ ∪ A)` and identifies its fixed points as the c.e. deductively closed extensions of `T₀`. The following paragraph separately treats the purely logical operator `Cn`.

The principal construction does not use the fixed-point characterization of theories. Theories in §8 are specified by tautologies and modus ponens; Appendix A describes closure relative to supplied rules. This distinction is necessary to keep a description of given deduction separate from the unproved intrinsic construction problem.

# (C) Minor issues

1. **Abstract quantification, repaired.** The first draft said that all weakest liberal preconditions are c.e. exactly when the program domain is recursive. Without restricting postconditions to c.e. sets, this is false: the identity program preserves an arbitrary non-c.e. postcondition. The final abstract states the right-adjoint result for direct image on c.e. domains. Failure mode: **ambiguous**; evidence: **counterexample**; impact: abstract only; severity: minor after the exact theorem was already stated correctly.

2. **Dependency statement, repaired.** The first paragraph of §9 originally said that “the separator arguments” require an inseparable input pair and a deductive presentation. The representation argument derives inseparability, and the domain-level transfer theorem does not need a closure operator. The paragraph now distinguishes the two arguments. Failure mode: **inconsistent_use**; evidence: **derivation** from their proofs; severity: minor.

3. **Support hypotheses are stronger than needed.** In Theorem 6.1 (`ct:certificate-transfer`, p. 9), the exact support equations `bar u = a` and `bar v = b` describe the intended proof-code constructions. The separator contradiction itself follows from the conclusion equations together with totality and the range property. This is not a false theorem and does not require deleting the support equations. It does prevent a claim that every displayed hypothesis is logically necessary. Failure mode if such a claim were made: **unused_hypothesis**; evidence: **derivation**; severity: minor.

4. **Fixed point versus generated closure, repaired.** The last sentence of Appendix A.2 initially referred to “the fixed point” for a c.e. axiom set. A closure operator generally has many fixed points. The intended object is the deductive closure of that axiom set, or the least fixed point containing it. Failure mode: **ambiguous**; evidence: **structural_argument**; severity: minor; verified resolution: the final sentence says “its deductive closure.”
