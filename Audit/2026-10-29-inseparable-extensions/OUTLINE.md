# Outline and dependencies

1. **Domains, separators, and reductions.** Define domain posets,
   total inverse image, separators, and Pair(R), including arrow types and
   composition. Proposition 1.1 transports inseparability along a total
   reduction when inverse images preserve joins.
2. **The free involutive presentation.** Lemma 2.1 proves
   D(X+Y)≅D(X)×D(Y). Define Inv(R), both functors, and their morphism
   actions. Theorem 2.2 proves Σ⊣J by an explicit natural hom-set
   bijection, unit, and counit. Theorem 2.3 identifies consistent extensions
   with disjoint enlargements and complete extensions with separators.
3. **The fixed interpretation for classical extensions.** The existing
   atom map θ yields the extension-poset functor and a constant-component
   natural transformation. Corollary 3.1 proves inseparability of the
   extension theorem/refutation pairs. The one-node proof equations verify
   the needed inequalities. The unsplit form is typed separately.
4. **Functors between computational categories.** Proposition 4.1 proves
   reflection, and preservation under bijectivity on the relevant
   endomorphism set. Proposition 4.2 supplies an evaluator-preserving
   projection counterexample, with nonzero complementary image domains.

The adjunction needs restriction zeros and binary restriction coproducts.
The signed equivalence additionally needs the binary joins on D(U).
It uses neither ranges nor stable inverse images. The general total-arrow
transport and classical application additionally require join preservation
by total inverse images. The classical application uses a consistent base
presentation constructed under the hypotheses of the earlier notes.

The exact signed equivalence is a classification of the specified extension
poset; it is not a claim that logical axiom schemes or modus ponens have
been constructed. No novelty claim is made for separator transport.
