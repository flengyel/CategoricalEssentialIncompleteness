## $B$ Major weaknesses

None confirmed in the assigned mathematical arguments. In particular, the following possible objections were examined and do not constitute failures of the written VERSION29 arguments.

1. **Nonstandard witnesses in interpretation.** At p. 22, §6, equation $10$, the text explicitly says that “all quantified traces and intermediate values ... range over the whole model.” Preservation of composition and ranges therefore uses arithmetic witnesses from the whole model, not an unjustified restriction to standard executions. The model interpretation is not asserted to be the ordinary external computation relation on the carrier of a nonstandard model.
2. **A pullback need not preserve an arbitrary initial algebra.** At pp. 32–34, Proposition 7.5, the proof does not make that assertion. It uses the numeral/complement decomposition of the parameter object, a slice equivalence, a natural isomorphism of inference functors, and an induced equivalence of algebra categories. Initiality is then transferred through that equivalence and projected to the numeral component. The product-category projection step explicitly pairs a candidate map with the identity of the other initial component.
3. **Uniformity over inconsistent theories.** At p. 34, Theorem 7.6, the uniform program and its parameter map are constructed before choosing a consistent fiber. Consistency is used only to show that the two proof predicates have disjoint standard truth sets at that fiber. The totality of the numerical specialization map is independent of consistency. The theorem does not require a uniform decision of consistency.
4. **A center evaluator in a non-discrete diagram need not be natural.** At pp. 68–70, Theorem B.7, the center evaluator is restricted to the c.e. domain on which both endpoint computations halt and agree with the projections of its output. This supplies both the subfunctor-domain condition and the naturality equations. The specialization proof checks the forward and reverse domain implications separately. The text does not simply install three unrelated universal partial recursive functions as a natural transformation.
5. **A separator computed from every representation need not be representation-independent.** At p. 46, Corollary 8.4, the bit-reading separator has a representation-dependent set at coordinates equal to (1/2). It therefore does not contradict inseparability in the category whose morphisms must have the same domain and value for every binary representation.
6. **Categorical products of cosheaves need not be pointwise products at the large open.** At pp. 64–65, Appendix B.1, this distinction is explicitly stated and demonstrated by a two-element versus four-element example. The partial-map category uses subobjects of the fork category, rather than arbitrary pointwise subsets of cosheaf values at every open.

No repair is requested for these points. Each is supported by steps present in the frozen text.

#### Miller sequences, §8
