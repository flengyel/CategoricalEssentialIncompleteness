# (D) Cross-section consistency failures

None confirmed. The precise dependency relations should be retained in later revisions.

1. **Arithmetic.** The local-connectedness theorem applied to J:Rec to Pfn yields the classical numerical inseparable pair. The finite-computation and proof-observation inclusions then yield essential incompleteness. The faithful family F_T separately supplies the extension-compatible interpretation of S_T and its internal inseparable pairs. Faithfulness of F_T is not needed to prove the classical numerical pair inseparable, and the manuscript does not need to claim that it is.
2. **Three-point category.** Section 7.1 verifies a genuine application of the local-connectedness lifting theorem to the full inclusion. Section 7.3 uses the already constructed presentation's singleton-output pair; its endpoint inseparability is proved directly from the explicitly preserved endpoint Turing morphism. That pair is not identified with the generally different summand-output pair furnished by the stated local-lifting theorem. These are compatible arguments, not a missing equality between the two pairs.
3. **Extent of functorial preservation.** E_1 and E_2 preserve the selected Turing morphism's universality by their explicit formula. The central component is separately shown not to do so. No claim that all structure-preserving functors preserve universality, or that E_i preserves every inseparable pair, is required or made.
4. **Nonnumerical source.** The isotypical recursion category and its split ambient category remain the source of the constructions. Component observation does not identify either with classical Rec or replace global cosheaf values by numerical quotients. The terminal-subobject argument explicitly rules out the relevant restriction equivalence.
5. **Computational scope.** Extensions remain selected computational domains with the stated logical closure and consistency conditions. The observation theorem concerns ordinary recursive inseparability and c.e. observations; it does not assert the stronger technical property of effective inseparability.
6. **Historical scope.** The introduction frames essential incompleteness as the application, attributes the earlier local-connectedness theorem and syntactic categories, and does not claim that PA incompleteness or the general possibility of nonnumerical recursion categories is newly discovered. No conclusion about priority follows from this audit.

# (E) Acceptance conditions, non-negotiable

The following conditions are met in the reviewed source and must be retained.

1. **Specify the observation functor.** Deliverable: `eq:endpoint-functor` and `prop:endpoint`. Endpoint: spans, variance, preservation laws, and the endpoint specialization proof remain explicit. Omitting the specialization proof would leave preservation of universality unproved.
2. **Retain the effective carriers.** Deliverable: `eq:probe-domain` and `thm:cosheaf-observation`. Endpoint: c.e. observations are carried by component names and pulled back along the total atom map to N^2. A proof that instead requires decidability of an unspecified c.e. syntax carrier must be rejected or supplied with that extra hypothesis.
3. **Retain the chosen presentation.** Deliverable: the total-category polynomial, logical schemes, and `eq:atomic-inequalities`. Endpoint: the proof observation is linked to the existing consistent classical presentation through the atom and proof-conclusion maps. Without those inclusions the essential-incompleteness conclusion would not follow.
4. **Keep the local-connectedness dependency precise.** Deliverable: the arithmetic J application and the distinction between Sections 7.1 and 7.3. Endpoint: no unsupported identification of the two kinds of diagonal pair and no claim that semantic target separators automatically lift. Such claims would require new proofs.
5. **Preserve prior-work attribution and stated scope.** Deliverable: Introduction and references. Endpoint: this remains an application and comparison of the named constructions. Strict-generalization, novelty, or completion claims require a separate source-grounded argument.
