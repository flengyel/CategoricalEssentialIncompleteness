\section*{The categorical construction}
The hypotheses in A.1 include finite products and disjoint, pullback-stable
finite coproducts. Put $\Two=1+1$, with injections
$\mathbf f,\mathbf t:1\to\Two$. Pulling this coproduct back along
$\pi_1:\Two\times\Two\to\Two$ decomposes $\Two\times\Two$ into two copies of
$\Two$. Decomposing each copy again gives
\[
\gamma=[\langle\mathbf f,\mathbf f\rangle,
\langle\mathbf f,\mathbf t\rangle,
\langle\mathbf t,\mathbf f\rangle,
\langle\mathbf t,\mathbf t\rangle]:
1+1+1+1\xrightarrow{\cong}\Two\times\Two.
\]
The chosen copairings define
\[
\neg_2=[\mathbf t,\mathbf f],\qquad
\wedge_2=[\mathbf f,\mathbf f,\mathbf f,\mathbf t]\gamma^{-1},
\qquad
\vee_2=[\mathbf f,\mathbf t,\mathbf t,\mathbf t]\gamma^{-1}.
\]
The coproduct universal property determines a morphism once its restrictions
have been specified. It does not choose those restrictions. The product
decomposition is also essential: it supplies the source needed for a binary
operation on $\Two$.

The notation $X^n$ denotes a chosen finite product: $X^0=1$, $X^1=X$,
and $X^{n+1}=X^n\times X$ for $n\geq1$. Rebracketing uses the canonical
associativity isomorphisms. The same convention defines products of morphisms.

These morphisms satisfy the Boolean algebra identities. For each finite $n$,
distributivity expresses $\Two^n$ as a coproduct of $2^n$ copies of $1$.
Its summand injections are jointly epimorphic, so equality of two morphisms
from $\Two^n$ can be checked on these injections. Composing the displayed
copairings verifies each axiom. For example,
\[
\wedge_2\langle1_{\Two},\neg_2\rangle=\mathbf f!_{\Two},
\qquad
\vee_2\langle1_{\Two},\neg_2\rangle=\mathbf t!_{\Two}:
\Two\to\Two.
\]
Precomposition with either $\mathbf f$ or $\mathbf t$ proves each equality.
This argument uses the particular coproduct decompositions, not an assumption
that global elements detect arbitrary morphisms. It makes no assertion that
$\Two$ classifies all subobjects.

\section*{Review scope and findings}
The version 16 check concerns notation: finite-product powers, products of
morphisms, parenthesization, and the two explicit pullback declarations. The
Boolean-algebra argument was checked separately for version 15. That check
covered the pullback decomposition, the ordered injections defining $\gamma$,
the jointly epimorphic families, and the types of the complement equations.

The four-agent review of version 14 remains a separate record: hostile
mathematical review, mathematical-writing review, six-section outline, and
reconciliation. These are not fresh full reviews of version 16. Their two
principal corrections---explicit copairings and the initial-algebra composite
in the substitution proof---are retained.
