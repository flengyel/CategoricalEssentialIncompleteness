## 1. The construction that the abstract needs to explain

The internal deductive system consists of categorical syntax and inference data. It is not one further object with an unexplained name. In the first-order case its data include a formula object `Φ`, a sentence subobject `j:S→Φ`, constructor and substitution morphisms, and rule objects with premise and conclusion morphisms. For chosen nonlogical axioms `i_A:A→S`, the rule data determine an inference endofunctor

\[
H_A:\mathcal D/\Phi\longrightarrow\mathcal D/\Phi.
\]

Its initial algebra has underlying conclusion morphism `c_A:P_A→Φ`. The object `P_A` represents finite derivations, and the conclusion morphism records their conclusions. Pulling back to sentences gives `c_{A,S}:P_{A,S}→S`. Its regular image

\[
m_A:T_{A,S}\hookrightarrow S
\]

is the subobject of sentences derivable in that internal deductive system from the chosen axioms. It is the least sentence theory containing `A`. This is the meaning of “generated theory” (Theorem A.6 and the paragraph after it, pp. 63–64; Section 4.1).

The categorical locations must be distinguished:

- `Φ`, `S`, `A`, `P_A`, `T_A`, and `T_{A,S}`, with their total morphisms and subobject inclusions, belong to the regular total category `D`.
- When `D=Tot(Split(R))`, this is the category of total morphisms of the restriction-idempotent splitting of the specified recursion category `R`.
- If the image inclusion has the stated partial inverse `r_A:S⇀T_{A,S}`, then the same sentence theory is represented by the restriction idempotent `t_A=m_A r_A=ran(c_{A,S}):S⇀S` in `Split(R)` (Lemma A.7, p. 64). For `Par(D,Mono)` that partial inverse exists automatically; for a given splitting of a recursion category it is an additional property verified in the realizations.
- Negation `ν:S→S` gives the refutation domain `ν* t_A`. Consistency means that the theorem and refutation domains are disjoint; completeness means that their join is the identity on `S`.

There is no object classifying all theories. When initial derivation algebras exist for every axiom subobject, varying `A` gives the associated deductive closure on sentence subobjects. The constructed objects are the individual generated theories.

The completion has a mathematical role: splitting restriction idempotents supplies objects representing partial domains; the total category provides the finite-limit and image constructions used to treat axioms and theories as subobjects. The examples separately prove that this total category is regular and has the required initial algebras. These properties do not follow for an arbitrary recursion category merely from taking its restriction splitting.

A plain account suitable for an abstract is therefore: **For each axiom subobject, the construction gives the subobject of sentences derivable from it; that subobject is the theory generated by the internal deductive system.** Naming initial algebras and regular images can then describe how this result is obtained. The current abstract instead names both mechanisms without identifying their input, output, or logical relation.

## 2. Actual outline of Appendix A

Its general construction precedes the numerical and other realization proofs in the applications.

| Existing subsection | Mathematical function, results, and dependencies |

Disjoint pullback-stable coproducts are added for first-order syntax; those extra coproduct conditions are not needed for the general closure theorem A.5. Specifies Boolean tests and conditionals categorically, finite products with chosen associations, a variable object `V∈D` with decidable equality, coefficient objects for bounded-arity function/relation symbols, and stability of initial algebras under parameters. | **A.3 Admissible substitution** (p. 60) | With parameter object `E=V×Tm`, constructs term substitution `a_T:Tm×E→Tm` and option-valued formula substitution `b_F:Fm×E→1+Fm`. | **A.5 First-order theories** (p. 63) | Builds the logical-axiom object from three propositional schemes, quantifier instantiation and distribution, and equality reflexivity and substitutivity. This is the construction used in Section 9. It does not require the variable-equality/substitution machinery used for first-order logic. |

The dependency order is substantive: Boolean decisions and parameterized recursion support substitution; substitution supports the first-order schemes; the inference endofunctor and initial derivation algebra support the image theorem; the image–range identification supports the incompleteness application. Removing the technical setting from the abstract does not remove any of these dependencies from the appendix.

## 5. Actual outline and role of Appendix C

Appendix C, **Necessity of section lifting for inseparability**, begins on p. 72 and contains **Proposition C.1**. It constructs a recursion category `C_K` of partial numerical functions with `K`-c.e. domains and ordinary partial recursive extensions. The inclusion into partial functions is faithful, preserves the listed range/restriction/product/coproduct structure, reflects zero, and has nonzero identity image. Nevertheless its chosen diagonal pair is separable. A target characteristic section has no source lift; disjoint source morphisms need not have a common upper bound.

Its role is to establish the substantive necessity of the comparison's lifting premise. It is not another construction of logical theories and need not be described in an abstract focused on the main logical contribution.

## 7. What belongs in the abstract

The abstract needs the following connections, with the application classes kept explicit:

1. The objective: connect categorical recursion with generalized/essential incompleteness.
2. The constructed data: propositional or first-order deductive systems internal to the appropriate completions.
3. The output and its location: an axiom subobject generates a deductively closed subobject of the internal sentence object—the system's provable sentences; negation gives refutable sentences.
4. Why the categorical computation matters: the comparison or inseparable-pair criterion is applied to those very theorem/refutation domains, not to an unrelated categorical object.
5. The extension conclusion and three applications: arithmetic c.e. extensions; the Miller uniform-in-all-representations class; the two same-language c.e. propositional endpoint classes.

The exact restriction splitting, total-category equivalence, regularity proofs, partial-inverse formula, rule arities, and frame calculations support the proofs. Their roles belong in the body. An abstract may mention initial algebras and images as the method, but its first task is to identify the theories they produce and explain their relation to the internal deductive systems. The current standalone sentence about “regular categories of total morphisms in the restriction splittings” classifies a technical setting before making this relation clear. Its proximate antecedent is the preceding realizations, but “the restriction splittings” does not identify what is being split or why the reader needs that information at this point.

This report recommends replacing that technical classification in the abstract with the system–axioms–derivable-sentences relationship. It recommends no deletion from the mathematical construction or its hypotheses in the body.
