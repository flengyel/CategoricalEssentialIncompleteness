## KLR source and application

I consulted the supplied text of Knuth, Larrabee, and Roberts, *Mathematical Writing*, Section 1, especially points 10–13 (printed pp. 2–3), 24 and 26 (p. 5). Their relevant advice concerns connecting ideas, attending to the reader's knowledge and expectations, making openings purposeful, and avoiding unnecessary jargon. They do not prescribe which theorems a research abstract must inventory. The object-by-object retention judgments above are editorial applications to this paper, not abstract-writing rules attributed to KLR.

The shortened text makes its applications identifiable without making their proof hypotheses into unexplained introductory objects. The repeated use of “completion” serves the placement of each realization; no new notation or categorical construction is introduced merely to classify the setting.
