# (A) Kill-shots / hard blockers

Author-side mathematical audit of Florian Lengyel, **Domains, finite proofs, and essential incompleteness**, 29 October 2026. The complete assembled LaTeX source, its 23-page PDF text and labels, and `OUTLINE.md` were read. This is an author-side review, not an external journal referee report. The review addresses correctness, typing, hypothesis use, source correspondence, and the scope of the incompleteness claim. Layout verification is separate. The local findings in (C) were subsequently checked against the delivered source and are resolved.

**Final reviewed source SHA-256:** `433f8a30df4f6d84e6055ac1d96ec58225e765e9f01354a8fd6794fd0ed226f0`.

No fatal defect in a stated theorem was established. In particular, Theorem 8.1 actually uses Turing universality: it obtains the total parameter map `g` and then the equation `dg = hg`. The finite-proof initial algebra is proved in the numerical computational category, and the numerical proof/refutation maps are constructed rather than assumed.

The outstanding generalization is not reclassified as solved. **Anchor:** abstract, p. 1, “The corresponding existence problem … is formulated separately,” and Problem 13.1, p. 22, which asks for logical presentations and proof maps in a specified completion of a general recursion category. A claim that this note completes the Di Paola–Heller program would be **unsupported_generalization**, with evidence **absence_in_text** and the explicit open problem. It would be fatal to that stronger claim. The note does not make that claim.

# (B) Major weaknesses

No unresolved major mathematical gap was found in the scoped categorical or numerical results. The distinction between the proved numerical construction and the nonnumerical existence problem is necessary to that assessment.

The old closure-operator comparison issue is no longer present: all four former appendices have been removed, and the main argument makes no appeal to them. This is deletion of an unused construction, not a proof of a previously open intrinsic construction. The new finite-proof theorem supplies a different, explicitly numerical existence result.

The Hoare semantics and the right-adjoint/bicomodule comparison in §§3–4 and 11 do not supply the logical presentation used for incompleteness. The latter is constructed independently in §12 from witnessed nullary rules and modus ponens. The outline correctly separates these dependency branches. Their coexistence in the note is not evidence that every Hoare profunctor canonically determines an essentially incomplete deductive system; no such statement is made.

# (C) Minor issues

1. **Arity and effectiveness in the scope sentences — resolved.** **Anchor:** abstract, p. 1, “Finite inference rules determine polynomial functors; … their initial algebras consist of finite proofs,” and §10, p. 14, “A c.e. list of rules can be presented in this form.” **Failure mode:** `ambiguous`. **Evidence:** `structural_argument`: the actual recursive presentation consists of `A_0,A_1,A_2`, and Theorem 10.1 proves initiality for recursive presentations of those arities. The c.e.-to-recursive annotation construction preserves the number of premises; it does not convert an arbitrary ternary rule into a binary rule over the same expression object. **Impact:** introductory scope only; neither Theorem 10.1 nor the propositional application uses larger arities. **Severity:** minor. **Repairability:** local: specify recursively presented rules with at most two premises in the initial-algebra summary, and say “A c.e. list of rules of these arities.” **Verified final repair:** the abstract now specifies recursive presentations with at most two premises per rule, and §10 qualifies the c.e. list by the same arity bound.

2. **Ambient hypotheses at the split-category application — resolved.** **Anchor:** §12.1, p. 19, “these maps also realize … Theorem 6.1, in the numerical restriction-idempotent splitting”; compare §10, pp. 13–14. **Failure mode:** `unproved`, at the level of an omitted routine verification. **Evidence:** `absence_in_text` plus `derivation`: Theorem 6.1 assumes ranges, binary restriction products, universal ranges, and finite stable unions. The split category is defined, and its c.e. domains are identified, but those four inherited numerical structures are not stated together. On c.e. carriers they are respectively images, products, pointwise agreement on images, and finite unions; the §7 pointwise argument verifies the required laws. **Impact:** explicit applicability of the abstract transfer theorem, not the direct numerical proof. **Severity:** minor. **Repairability:** add that verification to §10. **Verified final repair:** §10 identifies ranges and binary restriction products on c.e. carriers and explicitly verifies the Heller finite-union and universal-range properties by the pointwise numerical argument.

3. **Negative existence assertion in §13 — resolved.** **Anchor:** p. 22, “Finite joins of computational domains do not supply the initial algebras in this problem.” **Failure mode:** `ambiguous`. **Evidence:** `absence_in_text`: the text proves the numerical initial-algebra theorem by finite-tree recursion but does not prove a nonimplication theorem for all recursion categories with joins. **Impact:** interpretation of the open problem, not a proved theorem. **Severity:** minor. **Repairability:** state the precise existing boundary—“The numerical proof of initial-algebra existence uses recursive finite-tree coding and a computable fold into every algebra; an abstract construction requires corresponding existence and recursion principles”—or add an appropriately scoped counterexample if a nonimplication theorem is intended. **Verified final repair:** the negative sentence is deleted; the paragraph states the concrete finite-tree and computable-fold dependencies of the numerical existence proof.
