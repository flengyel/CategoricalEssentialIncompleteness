**04-02 — A J-indexed functor is attributed values at objects of O(X).** Anchor: PDF p.13, §8, source lines 1011–1015. Claim as written: “Set^J, where J=(1←0→2): the three components of a functor are the values at {c},{a,c},{b,c}, and the value at X is their pushout.” Failure mode: **ill_typed / ambiguous**. The immediate functor type is J→Set, whose arguments are 0,1,2. The opens and X are arguments of the cosheaf C:O(X)→Set. The sentence suppresses which functor is evaluated and conflates two indexing categories. Evidence: **structural_argument**, from the two explicitly named source categories. Downstream impact: this is the central identification used to pass from cosheaves to the functor category. Severity: major expository typing defect; the separate proof in Proposition A.1 supplies the intended identification, so the local repair is straightforward. Replacement: “Let J be the category with objects 0,1,2 and nonidentity morphisms 0→1 and 0→2. Restriction sends a cosheaf C to the functor with values C({c}), C({a,c}), C({b,c}) at 0,1,2, respectively. The cosheaf axiom identifies C(X) with C({a,c}) amalgamated with C({b,c}) over C({c}) (Proposition A.1).” Use the displayed pushout notation already employed in the Appendix.

**04-03 — Connectedness changes from a predicate on restriction idempotents to a predicate on subfunctors; the component labels are undeclared.** Anchor: PDF p.14, §8.1, source lines 1092–1095. Replacement: “For Y∈E and y∈Y_0, let D^y⊆Y have components D^y_0={y} and D^y_i={r_i^Y(y)} for i=1,2. The partial identity on D^y is a connected restriction idempotent. The same holds for the partial identity on a subfunctor whose component at i=1 or i=2 is a singleton and whose other components are empty.” Then retain the detection argument and Proposition A.3 reference. Use the component indices consistently in the later proof as well.
