# Source check: the unprimed syntactic category S_T

Date: 2026-10-29. This is a source and proof-check record, not a new manuscript.
All assertions about the primed systems S' and S'_T are excluded. The known
failure of composition for their pointwise-provability equivalence cannot be
repaired by importing those papers' category claims.

## What the unprimed sources define

Montagna p.105 and DPM p.646: the objects form the smallest class containing
omega and closed under Cartesian product and tagged binary disjoint union.
Each object has fixed effective numerical coding and is effectively isomorphic
to omega. In particular this is not the category of all definable subsets.

For typed program indices e,f:X -> Y, equality in S_T means one uniform proof
in T of their Kleene equality:
\[
 T\vdash\forall x\bigl[
  (\varphi_e(x)\downarrow\leftrightarrow\varphi_f(x)\downarrow)
  \land
  (\varphi_e(x)\downarrow\Rightarrow
          \varphi_e(x)=\varphi_f(x))\bigr].
\]
The quantifier is INSIDE the proof. Montagna's unprimed S is S_PA.
Composition is induced by ordinary program composition. Its well-definedness
is reconstructed below rather than inferred from the invalid primed claims.

Montagna p.108 gives the restriction domain of [e] as the class of a program
d(e) that runs e on x and, if it halts, returns x. PA proves uniformly that
its halting condition is that of e and its value, when defined, is x.
DPM pp.646–647 gives the same construction and a primitive recursive compiler
e -> d(e). Restriction totality is T-provable universal halting, not merely
external totality and not weak totality.

Montagna p.108 takes the Turing morphism to be the class of the universal
partial recursive function (e,x) -> phi_e(x). Its parameter property follows
from the formalized s-m-n theorem. The compiler is PA-provably total; hence
it is total in every S_T under consideration.

Montagna p.111 supplies ranges: the range of [e] is represented by the
partial identity with Sigma_1 domain \(\exists x\,(\varphi_e(x)=y)\).
Montagna pp.112–113 gives sections by searching the enumerated graph for
the first preimage, and coproducts by tagged disjoint union. DPM p.646
states that these unprimed facts pass to consistent r.e. T extending PA.

The sources explicitly call S_T a pointed p-recursion category and explicitly
say it is NOT dominical. The obstruction at Montagna p.108 is the dominical
nonzero-product axiom and the distinction between restriction totality and
weak totality. It must not silently be renamed a dominical Heller recursion
category.

Montagna Remark 3.1, p.114, states existence of effectively inseparable pairs
in S by adapting Di Paola–Heller Theorem 8.18 using Proposition 1.1.
Only the unprimed assertion is recorded here. The binary-case argument below
gives a direct verification of the weaker inseparability required here.

## Reconstructed calculations

Fix a primitive recursive computation-trace predicate for a deterministic
universal machine, with its functionality verified in PA. Write
\(G_e(x,y)\) for the Sigma_1 output relation obtained by existentially
quantifying a terminating trace. The output relation itself is not claimed
primitive recursive. The displayed Kleene
equality is equivalent over PA to
\(T\vdash\forall x\,\forall y(G_e(x,y)\leftrightarrow G_f(x,y))\).

For a fixed composition compiler comp,
\[
 PA\vdash G_{\mathrm{comp}(g,e)}(x,z)
 \leftrightarrow\exists y\,[G_e(x,y)\land G_g(y,z)].
\]
Uniform equality of either input can be substituted under this existential.
This proves both congruence laws. Associativity and identities follow from
the same graph formulas in PA. This proof uses no pointwise-to-uniform step.

Domains identify with Sigma_1 input predicates modulo T-provable uniform
equivalence: associate D with the partial identity that searches a witness
for D(x), then returns x. The domain order is
\(D\le E\) iff \(T\vdash\forall x(D(x)\Rightarrow E(x))\).
Meet is conjunction; bottom false; top true; finite join disjunction.
These claims follow by the uniform graph identities for the corresponding
partial-identity programs. In particular join is least for this provability
order, not just for external inclusion of standard subsets.

For \(f:X\rightharpoonup Y\), define \(R_f(y)\) by
\(\exists x\,G_f(x,y)\), and represent it by a partial identity on Y.
PA proves \(R_f f=f\). For a domain D on Y, the equation \(Df=f\)
in S_T is equivalent to
\[
 T\vdash\forall x\,\forall y\,(G_f(x,y)\to D(y)),
\]
which is equivalent by first-order logic to
\(T\vdash\forall y(R_f(y)\to D(y))\).
Thus \(R_f\le D\) whenever \(Df=f\), proving the least-domain
universal property of the range in the uniform proof order. The same
image program represents this range after theory extension.
It also has the agreement property. If \(g,h:Y\rightharpoonup Z\)
satisfy \(gf=hf\), an input witness for \(R_f(y)\), functionality of
f, and equality of the two composite graphs give
\(G_g(y,z)\leftrightarrow G_h(y,z)\). This is a first-order derivation
under the assumption \(R_f(y)\), using existential elimination. It proves
\(gR_f=hR_f\) uniformly in T.

For the universal agreement property of finite union, if T proves equality
of two partial maps after each of the restrictions D and E, first-order
case elimination proves equality after D or E. The argument persists under
product with any fixed identity domain. Total inverse image substitutes the
graph of the total arrow, and its finite-join law follows by distribution
of existential quantification over finite disjunction. These are finite
claims; no arbitrary countable joins have been inferred.

Suppose C,C' are complementary in D(X). Then T proves their uniform
disjointness and exhaustiveness. Dovetail their two Sigma_1 searches and
output 1 on a C witness and 0 on a C' witness. PA proves the graph
specification of this case program; T proves its universal halting from
C or C'. Disjointness proves the two required branch equations. Thus the
explicit binary-case hypothesis used by the diagonal inseparability theorem
holds for S_T. Consistency suffices to make 0 unequal to 1; Sigma_1
soundness is not needed for this internal argument.

For T contained in T', define Q_{T,T'}:S_T -> S_T' identically on objects
and by [e]_T -> [e]_{T'} on arrows. T-provable equations remain T'-provable,
so this is well-defined and preserves composition and identities.
The program compilers for product, zero, domain, range, finite union,
coproduct and Turing morphism are unchanged, so Q preserves those chosen
structures. It preserves restriction-totality but need not reflect it.
Likewise it need not reflect zero or complemented-domain equations.
No general preservation theorem for every inseparable pair is inferred.

The fixed PA-program diagonal pair defined by universal self-evaluation
with output 0 or 1 is sent by Q to the identically defined pair in S_T'.
Its inseparability follows again from the verified binary-case and
Turing argument whenever T' is consistent. This is preservation of this
specified pair, with a separate internal argument in every target.

## A useful extraction into ordinary computability

For a domain represented by D_e(x), put
\[
 E_T([D_e])=\{n\in\mathbb N:T\vdash D_e(\bar n)\}.
\]
This is well-defined on the uniform quotient and is a c.e. subset when T is
c.e. It is monotone, preserves finite meets and top, and preserves bottom
when T is consistent. These are elementary proof manipulations.

Do NOT assert join preservation for arbitrary T: a theory can prove a
disjunction without proving either disjunct. Under the temporary hypothesis
that T is complete and consistent, it does preserve finite joins. Under that
same hypothesis every E_T([D_e]) is recursive, by parallel search for a
T-proof of D_e(n) or its negation.

The standard c.e. diagonal output-0 and output-1 sets A,B are contained in
the extracted domains of their corresponding syntactic pair: each genuine
finite computation has a PA-verifiable numeral instance. PA proves the
two predicates disjoint, so consistency of T makes the extracted domains
disjoint. If T were complete, the first extracted domain would consequently
be a recursive separator of A,B. This describes a domain-level route;
it does not posit a category based on pointwise-provable arrow equality.

## Essential distinction about consistency

External consistency of T does not imply that T's own theorem and refutation
domains are internally disjoint in S_T. Such internal disjointness requires
a T-proof of the corresponding universal statement, essentially Con(T).
Thus an argument using E_T and external consistency must not be replaced
by an unsupported claim that the theorem domain of T is internally
consistent in S_T. The extraction argument avoids that step.
