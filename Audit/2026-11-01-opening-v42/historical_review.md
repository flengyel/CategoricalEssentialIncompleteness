# Version 42: historical opening

The proposed two-sentence opening is supported by the primary text:

> Di Paola and Heller introduced dominical categories to develop recursion theory in categorical algebra. They sought a formulation of generalized incompleteness in this setting \cite[pp.~594--595]{DPH}.

Di Paola–Heller, *Dominical categories: recursion theory without elements* (1987), §1, printed p.594, first paragraph, presents dominical categories as the basis for an abstract formulation of recursion theory. The same paragraph identifies the algebraization of generalized incompleteness as a principal reason for this formulation. Printed pp.594–595 then describe the intended generalized-incompleteness statements. Thus both claims in the opening have direct primary-text support.

The wording attributes the category construction and its objective; it does not claim that the earlier authors constructed the internal deductive systems or proved the present paper's comparison theorem. It also makes no assertion about their expectations or a priority date beyond the source attribution.

Removing the related-work sentence that repeats the introduction of dominical categories preserves that attribution in the opening. The next statement, that Rosolini's $P$-categories generalize dominical categories, remains supported by the existing cited literature. Heller (1990), §5, printed p.1260, explicitly states this generalization; the supplied Rosolini chapter, §1, p.308, also provides the representation framework for the more general notion.
