# Organization and economy audit of v26

This is an audit of the complete assembled source in `output/manuscript/local_connectedness`, in the order specified by `main.tex`, including its nested arithmetic inputs, Appendix A–D, abstract, introduction, and bibliography. The published assembly has 77 pages. No manuscript file was changed. Page numbers below are the current assembly's page starts and are approximate measures of occupied space, not promises about a revised assembly.

## Finding

The principal cause of length is mathematical scope. The paper now contains an abstract comparison argument, a detailed realization of first-order syntax in a provability quotient, a soundness theorem involving an ultrapower comparison, an internal indexed family of arithmetic theories, a separate numerical realization, a representation-independent Miller application, and a construction of a further recursion category with propositional theories. These are substantial mathematical packages. Removing empty phrases will not turn this into a short paper.

There is nevertheless genuine duplication in the numerical foundations and the three-point construction. It should be removed before any scope decision. The long realization proofs are not all duplicates: a fold computable as an ordinary function and a fold whose defining equations and totality are provable in PA have different obligations. Likewise, source inseparability obtained from total lifts and target incompleteness obtained from a partial proof-search lift have different hypotheses. Economy must preserve those distinctions.

My preferred scope division retains the comparison theorem, the syntactic and numerical realizations, soundness, the indexed arithmetic family and uniform independent sentences, and the Miller application in the main paper. It moves the entire three-point construction and the all-model faithful-family construction, with their supporting lemmas, into a companion document. This retains one substantive non-arithmetic application of the general theory and moves a separate construction of a new recursion category rather than hiding its proofs. It is a scope recommendation, not an instruction to omit proofs while retaining all the same claims.

## Verified writing principles and their application

The governing source was actually consulted: Knuth–Larrabee–Roberts, *Mathematical Writing*, the local text `tmp/review_sources/klr.txt` (corresponding PDF also supplied for the workflow). The following are source-grounded principles, not claims that KLR dictates the particular division into two papers.

| KLR location | Verified principle | Application here |
|---|---|---|
| §1, items 10–11, printed pp.2–3 | Connected exposition and definitions of variables; formula/prose explanations should complement one another | Preserve the new explicit carriers, graph-equivalence conditions, slice morphisms and comparison components. Do not count these as verbosity merely because a specialist can guess them. |
| §1, items 12–14, printed pp.3–4 | Establish reader purpose, make prose intelligible around displays, and use notation consistently | Organize the main paper around a theorem and its actual realization. The same numerical category should not be defined twice in slightly different object conventions. |
| §1, items 15 and 26, printed pp.4–5 | Avoid unnecessary indices and unnecessary jargon | This justifies consistent names for established functors and standard terminology; it does not justify erasing indices that distinguish objects, components or ambient categories. |
| §§4–5, printed pp.9–13 | Definitions may be located by reader need, but not held back excessively; explain proof direction; define symbols near use | The separate realization subsections are justified. The reader should be told exactly which Appendix A hypotheses each block verifies. There is no reason to repeat the full argument in the main text and an appendix. |
| §31, printed pp.69–70 | Know what is being presented and to whom; attend to structure; a solid example is valuable | Supports retaining a complete non-arithmetic application rather than reducing the paper to a dry framework. The recommendation to retain Miller and move the three-point branch is my mathematical/editorial judgment. |
| §32, printed p.71 | Worked examples remove prose that describes the authors' difficulty or selection process without adding content | No need to add statements such as “all hypotheses are explicit,” “this completes the audit,” or “the argument is fully categorical” to the manuscript. The current core is already much better in this respect. |

KLR does not prescribe a page target, require every subsection to have exactly one theorem, prohibit all explanation after a proof, or recommend replacing typed proofs by “similarly.” Those would be independent rules, and I have not attributed them to KLR.

## Repetitions that must remain

* **The abstract syntax specification versus each realization.** Appendix A states a structure in a category \(\mathcal D\), assumes specified initial algebras, and proves consequences. §§5,7.4,8.3,9.3 must prove those structures exist in their respective categories. A numerical program does not by itself establish the categorical universal property.
* **PA-provable recursion versus ordinary computable recursion.** The arbitrary algebra and parameter objects differ, as do the equality conditions for arrows. The existence and uniqueness arguments in §5 cannot be imported into §7.4 by an unproved initiality-preservation assertion about an interpretation functor.
* **Naturality of componentwise folds.** §9.3 must prove that formula and proof evaluations commute with the structure maps. Componentwise initiality alone is not the asserted initiality in \(\mathcal E\).
* **Standard soundness versus the ultrapower comparison.** The first proves induction on standard derivations; the second identifies interpreted internally coded objects with ultrapowers of their standard interpretations and transports a factorization. Neither conclusion contains the other without the comparison lemmas.
* **Initial derivations versus least closed image.** The realization proves the former; Appendix A proves the latter. The applications should cite the least-image theorem rather than reprove it, but cannot cite it to avoid establishing initiality.
* **Categorical preliminaries repeated for Appendix A self-containment.** The definition of a regular category, product conventions, the image/range interface, variable object and Boolean/conditional structure serve the appendix's internal dependencies. Their presence is not equivalent to an unnecessary second main-paper exposition. The Boolean copairing example and explicit equality convention also answer earlier authorial requirements.
* **The propositional specialization.** This is currently required by §9. Moving the entire cosheaf branch permits moving A.6 with it; retaining §9 requires retaining A.6 or an equally complete local specification. It cannot disappear merely because it resembles first-order syntax.

## Recommended division and resulting outline

I recommend preserving one main line and one non-arithmetic application, rather than removing all examples or making the core proofs terse.

**Main paper:** preliminaries; general diagonal comparison and the sufficient local-lifting mechanism; internal consequence objects and interpreted consistency; syntactic recursion category and full realization; model interpretation and standard/ultrapower soundness; fixed and indexed arithmetic applications; numerical realization and its categorical application; Miller recursion category and real-closed-field application. Appendix A retains the categorical first-order construction, with its literature dependencies and regular-image/range lemma. Appendix C retains the counterexample to omitting lifting. The introduction and abstract are rewritten last around these exact contents.

**Companion:** the three-point recursion category, its complete structural proof, propositional syntax and derivation initiality, endpoint observations and incompleteness. Include the all-model faithful-family construction as a separate comparison section only if that comparison is part of the companion's declared purpose; otherwise preserve it as an independent note. Move `lem:switch` and `cor:model-pair` with this all-model package. The two branches should not be advertised as a single theorem when they have no dependency on one another.

This moves §9 plus Appendix B, currently about fourteen pages, and Appendix D plus its isolated supporting subsection, about four pages. These figures measure the current blocks. They do not account for new section starts, bibliography changes, or the duplication a standalone companion must legitimately retain. The main paper would still be substantial because its explicit syntactic realization and Appendix A carry real proof obligations.

If the intended main contribution is instead *only* the fixed syntactic-category comparison and indexed consequence family, then §8 can also move. That is a coherent narrower paper, but it removes the main manuscript's detailed non-arithmetic realization. It should be a deliberate change of mathematical scope, not presented as mere copyediting. I prefer retaining Miller because it supplies a full example under a computability condition different from the numerical one, and because its separation after choosing a representation explains a limitation of functor comparison.

A companion must contain or cite actual available results for its foundations. It cannot depend on unspecified portions of an unpublished “long version” while being called self-contained. Conversely, self-containment does not require duplicating the entire Appendix A in both files: a published or jointly supplied theorem may be cited with its exact hypotheses, while each realization still supplies its own universal-property proof.
