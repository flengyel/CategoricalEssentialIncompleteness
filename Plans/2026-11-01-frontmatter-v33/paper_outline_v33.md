# Detailed outline of version 33

*Logical consequence and essential incompleteness in recursion categories*, 1 November 2026, commit `0531db597e015824af5861a8e5ad0c1976efdcb3`.

This describes the existing paper, not a proposed reorganization. All 22 compiled TeX files were read. The proposed front matter and literature comparisons are in the companion report.


The paper has nine main sections, followed by four appendices. The numerical source filenames do not give the compiled section order: `04-miller-sequences.tex` is Section 8, after all the nested arithmetic subsections of Section 7.

| Abstract (p. 1) | Previews inseparability, PA, the Miller real-closed-field theory, a functor that sends an inseparable pair to a separable pair, and cosheaf propositional theories. |
| **1. Introduction** (pp. 1–2) | Describes the comparison/conditional method and applications, but leads with source inseparability and the Miller example. |
| 1.1 Related work | Four paragraphs: the DPH program and existence theorems; categorical formalisms; DPH/Bernardi/Montagna inseparability; Miller and Bauer–Hanson. |
| **2. Categorical preliminaries** (pp. 3–5) | Fixes the categorical language and types used throughout. |
| 2.1 Partial maps and restriction idempotents | Domains, order, joins, consistency-relevant disjointness and inseparability, inverse image, ranges and their agreement law, restriction splitting, Karoubi envelope. |
| 2.2 Products, coproducts, and local connectedness | Restriction products/coproducts, range functors, coproduct comparison, connected domains, sections, weak choice. Explains that a range functor need not preserve products. |
| 2.3 Recursion categories | Prodominical/isotypical recursion categories with Turing morphisms; distinguishes these from cartesian Turing categories. |
| **3. Diagonal comparison and complementary conditionals** (pp. 5–6) | Establishes the categorical diagonal mechanism. |
| 3.1 The comparison theorem | Theorem **3.1** (`thm:comparison`): a lift of a partial reversed conditional for disjoint target domains (P,N), containing the images of the diagonal domains, produces total (g:U\to U) whose interpreted inverse images of (P,N) are zero. With nonzero identity and the requisite join law, (P\vee N\ne1). Corollary **3.2** excludes such a lift for a complementary target separator. |
| 3.2 Complementary conditionals | Corollary **3.3** (`cor:source-conditionals`) derives source inseparability from total source conditionals for every complementary source pair. Proposition **3.4** obtains these conditionals from joins of morphisms with disjoint domains. |
| **4. Internal theories and semantic consistency** (pp. 7–9) | Converts diagonal comparison into a theorem about logical theories and separates the two routes to incompleteness. |
| 4.1 Theories and their interpretation | In \(\mathcal D=\operatorname{Tot}(\operatorname{Split}(\mathscr R))\), sentence theories are regular images of sentence-conclusion morphisms and, with the stated partial-inverse hypothesis, ranges \(t_A=\operatorname{ran}(c_{A,S})\). A range functor extends through chosen splittings. Proposition **4.1** transfers a soundness factorization through a validity subobject to disjointness of interpreted theorem/refutation domains. |
| 4.2 Incompleteness from semantic consistency | Theorem **4.2** (`thm:logical-comparison`): a total reduction \(\theta:U\to S\), semantic consistency, reduction inequalities, and a partial conditional lift yield total \(g\) whose interpreted sentence values are neither provable nor refutable. Both interpreted and internal theories are incomplete. |
| 4.3 Inseparability in the source | Corollary **4.3** (`cor:domain-incompleteness`): reducing an inseparable source pair to the theorem/refutation domains of a base theory gives inseparability and incompleteness for every consistent larger represented theory. Full inclusion into the restriction splitting preserves an inseparable pair on an original object. |
| **5. The syntactic recursion category** (pp. 10–21) | Constructs \(S_T\) from program indices modulo universal \(T\)-provable graph equality, and realizes the categorical logical constructions in its total restriction splitting. |
| Section opening | Lemma **5.1** supplies PA-provable evaluation, specialization, and composition equations. Constructs ranges, products, coproducts, and a Turing morphism. Relates the term-object presentation to Montagna's set-object presentation. Distinguishes the primed pointwise quotients, which fail the stated composition. |
| 5.1 The total restriction splitting | Lemmas **5.2–5.3** identify \(\mathcal B_T=\operatorname{Tot}(\operatorname{Split}(S_T))\) with a category of \(\Sigma_1\) predicates and \(T\)-provably total functional graphs. Establishes regularity, disjoint stable finite coproducts, partial inverses of monos, and \(\operatorname{Par}(\mathcal B_T,\operatorname{Mono})\simeq\operatorname{Split}(S_T)\). Also verifies its Turing-category structure. |
| 5.2 Syntax and lists | Lemma **5.4** combines finite \(\Sigma_1\) membership conditions. Propositions **5.5–5.6** prove initiality of the syntax and list algebras, stable under parameters, for fixed bounded arity and coefficient objects in \(\mathcal B_T\). |
| 5.3 Free variables and substitution | Proposition **5.7** realizes the free-variable and substitution folds and the complemented free-for domain on raw named-variable syntax. |
| 5.4 Derivations and theories | Lemma **5.8** proves derivation initiality. Theorem **5.9** (`thm:syntactic-generated-theory`) realizes the Appendix A theory construction; regular images are the least closed formula subobjects and their sentence parts. Represents the generated sentence theory by a restriction idempotent of \(S_T\). |
| **6. Interpretation and soundness** (pp. 22–27) | Constructs interpretations, proves one-model soundness on standard codes, and transports it to arithmetic ultrapowers. |
| Section opening | Proposition **6.1** constructs \(F_M:S_T\to\mathbf{Pfn}\) for each \(M\models T\), preserving the stated structure; all arithmetic quantifiers range over the entire model. |
| 6.1 Lifting complementary conditionals | Lemma **6.2** constructs total source switches from complementary \(T\)-provable predicates. Corollary **6.3** obtains an inseparable pair in \(S_T\) using a single model. |
| 6.2 The total interpretation functor | Proposition **6.4** proves that \(G_M:\mathcal B_T\to\mathbf{Set}\) preserves finite limits, finite coproducts, and regular images. |
| 6.3 Soundness on standard codes | Lemma **6.5** proves semantic substitution. Proposition **6.6** (`prop:standard-code-soundness`) factors the standard interpretation of the represented conclusion morphism through the set of sentence codes valid in a separate structure \(K\models\Gamma\). Validity and negated validity are disjoint. |
| 6.4 Ultrapowers of the code interpretation | Lemmas **6.7–6.8** prove preservation properties of the set ultrapower functor and \(\mathscr U G_{\mathbb N}\cong G_M\) for \(M=\mathbb N^I/\mathcal U\). Theorem **6.9** (`thm:ultrapower-soundness`) transports the standard soundness factorization and disjointness to this interpretation, including nonstandard code elements. |
| **7. Essential incompleteness of arithmetic** (pp. 28–41) | Gives the fixed-ambient-category arithmetic application, uniform independence, and a comparison with the numerical realization. |
| 7.1 Comparison in the fixed syntactic category | In \(S_{\mathrm{PA}}\), Lemma **7.1** constructs numeral substitution \(\theta\) and the two reduction inequalities for every \(M\equiv\mathbb N\). Lemma **7.2** supplies a partial proof-search lift. Theorem **7.3** (`thm:arithmetic-internal-comparison`) proves incompleteness of each consistent represented c.e. theory deriving PA, internally and under the standard/ultrapower interpretations, and gives a total family of undecidable standard sentences. Corollary **7.4** proves recursive inseparability of theorem/refutation sets for every consistent c.e. extension of PA, hence essential incompleteness. |
| 7.2 An indexed family of arithmetic theories | Constructs one axiom family over \(E=(\omega,1_\omega)\), one initial derivation algebra and one theorem subobject. Proposition **7.5** (`prop:arithmetic-theory-fibers`) identifies the numeral fibers with the theories generated by the individual c.e. axiom sets \(\Gamma_e\). |
| 7.3 Uniform independent sentences | Theorem **7.6** (`thm:uniform-independent-sentences`) constructs a single total morphism \(q:E\to S\) whose standard value at every consistent fiber \(e\) codes a sentence neither provable nor refutable from \(\Gamma_e\). |
| 7.4 The numerical realization | Proposition **7.7** realizes the total restriction splitting of ordinary partial recursion as \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{rec}}\), the regular category of c.e. numerical carriers and total computable maps on them. Proposition **7.8** realizes categorical syntax and derivations there. Explicit computable comparison maps identify the generated theorem carriers with the standard syntactic interpretations. |
| 7.5 Numerical incompleteness | Uses Corollary 3.3 to produce the fixed recursively inseparable numerical pair; uses numeral-substitution inclusions and Corollary 4.3 to recover inseparability/incompleteness of all consistent c.e. extensions of PA. |
| 7.6 Numeral instances and theory extension | Packages the enlarged numeral theorem/refutation pairs covariantly under theory inclusion and compares this numerical argument with Bernardi's precomplete-equivalence-relation argument. |
| **8. Miller sequences and essential incompleteness** (pp. 42–48) | Constructs a new recursion category from representation-independent computation; obtains an inseparable cut pair and an essentially incomplete real-closed-field theory within the specified uniform computability class. |
| 8.1 Computation independent of the binary representation | Lemma **8.1** uses compactness of the representation fiber to compute unanimity. Proposition **8.2** makes \(\mathcal E_a\) a locally connected recursion category with ranges, weak choice, compatible joins, and Turing morphism \(\tau_a\). Fixed-representation inclusions \(J_p\) are faithful range functors preserving products. Corollary 3.3 gives the diagonal pair \(A_0,A_1\). |
| 8.2 The index sets below and above \(1/2\) | Lemma **8.3** constructs an ordinary total recursive reduction with exact inverse images \(h^{-1}(L_a)=A_0\), \(h^{-1}(R_a)=A_1\). Corollary **8.4** proves inseparability in \(\mathcal E_a\), although each representation computes a separator. Thus \(J_p\) sends this inseparable pair to a separable pair and does not preserve the universality of \(\tau_a\). |
| 8.3 A theory of real closed fields | Proposition **8.5** realizes syntax and derivations in \(\mathcal D_a=\operatorname{Tot}(\operatorname{Split}(\mathcal E_a))\). Theorem **8.6** (`thm:miller-incompleteness`) proves inseparability of theorem/refutation domains and incompleteness for every consistent extension of \(T_a\) with an \(\mathcal E_a\)-semidecidable axiom set. |
| **9. A recursion category from a three-point space** (pp. 49–55) | Constructs a recursion category from computable cosheaf/functor data, realizes propositional consequence in its completion, and obtains two essentially incomplete component theories. |
| Section opening | Identifies the cosheaves with diagrams on \(J=(1\leftarrow0\to2)\), forms \(\mathcal A=\operatorname{Par}(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{rec}}^J,\operatorname{Mono})\), introduces \(V\), and obtains \(\mathscr R_X\), its Turing morphism, and \(\operatorname{Split}(\mathscr R_X)\simeq\operatorname{Kar}(\mathscr R_X)\simeq\mathcal A\). |
| 9.1 Complementary domains and inseparability | Constructs complementary source conditionals, obtains an inseparable pair, and shows \(\mathscr R_X\) is not restriction-equivalent to ordinary partial recursion. |
| 9.2 Evaluation functors | Proposition **9.1** proves structure preservation of all component evaluations \(E_j\), and Turing-morphism preservation at endpoints \(i=1,2\). Gives probe descriptions and explains why the central evaluation \(E_0\) does not preserve this Turing universality. |
| 9.3 Propositional theories and component theorem sets | Propositions **9.2–9.3** prove formula and derivation initiality. Theorem **9.4** constructs the least generated c.e. theory subfunctor and proves consistency. Theorem **9.5** proves inseparability for compatible consistent c.e. extension families and recursive inseparability/incompleteness at each endpoint. Corollary **9.6** (`cor:cosheaf-essential-incompleteness`) proves essential incompleteness of each endpoint theory for consistent c.e. axiom extensions in its own language. |

| **Appendix C. Necessity of section lifting for inseparability** (pp. 72–74) | Proposition **C.1** builds \(\mathcal C_K\): a faithful product-preserving range functor to partial functions, with zero reflection and nonzero identity, whose diagonal pair is separable. Its reversed target conditional has no source lift; even disjoint morphisms need not have a common upper bound. |
