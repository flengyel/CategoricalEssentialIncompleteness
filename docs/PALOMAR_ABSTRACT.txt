A disjoint pair of subobjects of an object X determines a first-order theory with nullary predicates indexed by X. Its nonlogical axioms assert the predicates on one subobject and their negations on the other. We construct this theory in the category of total morphisms of the restriction idempotent completion of a recursion category. We assume regularity, disjoint pullback-stable finite coproducts, partial inverses for monomorphisms, and initial algebras for the indicated syntax and finite derivations. On pairs that generate consistent theories, the construction is left adjoint to the functor assigning to each consistent theory its theorem and refutation subobjects. For such a pair, inseparability is equivalent to essential incompleteness of the generated theory, that is, to incompleteness of every consistent internal extension.

For a theory with theorem subobject U, the partial maps in the slice over U form a Turing category. With deduction in the parameter slices and a chosen global logical theorem, a consistent theory is essentially incomplete if and only if its constant family is essentially incomplete in this category. Theory inclusions induce fully faithful functors. In the restriction idempotent completion of the syntactic recursion category for Peano arithmetic, a single total morphism assigns an independent sentence to each index for a consistent computably enumerable extension. For a theory of real closed fields with constants naming a Miller sequence, every consistent extension whose axioms one oracle program semidecides from every binary representation of the sequence is incomplete. In a cosheaf construction on a three-point space, the propositional theory at either endpoint is consistent and every consistent computably enumerable extension in its language is incomplete.
