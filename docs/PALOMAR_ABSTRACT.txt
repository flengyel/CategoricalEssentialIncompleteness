Gödel’s arithmetization represents formulas and finite proofs by numbers and their effective operations and relations within arithmetic. We begin with computation, axiomatized by recursion categories, and construct internal propositional and first-order deductive systems. The construction takes place in a regular, extensive category of total morphisms of a restriction idempotent completion. Monomorphisms must have partial inverses in the completion, and syntax and initial algebras for finite derivations must exist with parameters in every slice. A total reduction of an inseparable pair to the theorem and refutation subobjects of a consistent theory makes every consistent internal extension incomplete. Generation is left adjoint to inclusion of deductively closed theories, and logical translations induce Galois connections. Theory inclusions induce fully faithful functors between associated Turing categories. A consistent theory containing a fixed global logical theorem is essentially incomplete if and only if its constant family in the associated category is essentially incomplete. These constructions give a formulation of the generalized incompleteness sought by Di Paola and Heller. In the completion of the syntactic recursion category for Peano arithmetic, we represent computably enumerable extensions of Peano arithmetic by internal theories. For each consistent extension, a categorical comparison theorem proves incompleteness of both the internal theory and the arithmetic extension. A uniform construction gives an independent sentence from an index for the extension’s axiom enumeration. A realization based on a Miller sequence gives a consistent theory of real closed fields expanded by constants naming the sequence. Every consistent extension is incomplete if one oracle program semidecides its axioms using every binary representation of the sequence. A realization using cosheaves on a three-point space gives two consistent propositional theories whose consistent extensions in the same language with computably enumerable axiom sets are incomplete. These theories have conservative first-order extensions. The construction of associated categories applies to a first-order theory generated by the arithmetic diagonal pair, the Miller field theory, and the first-order extension of the internal cosheaf theory.
