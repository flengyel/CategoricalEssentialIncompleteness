## Summary
This paper develops an intrinsic categorical framework for formal deductive systems and essential incompleteness starting from axiomatic partial computation in recursion categories. Working within the regular, extensive category $\mathcal{D} = \mathrm{Tot}(\mathrm{Split}(\mathscr{R}))$ of total morphisms of a restriction idempotent completion, the author constructs named-variable first-order syntax, capture-avoiding substitution on a complemented admissibility subobject, and initial algebras of finite derivations, and establishes two general criteria—via range functors with lifted partial conditionals and via source reductions of inseparable pairs—for the incompleteness and essential incompleteness of consistent internal theories. The paper further develops a functorial meta-theory of deductive generation, logical translations, free first-order theories over disjoint domain pairs, and associated slice Turing categories $\mathrm{Par}(\mathcal{D}/T, \mathrm{Mono})$, and validates the entire architecture across three concrete realizations: Montagna and Di Paola–Montagna's syntactic recursion category $S_{\mathrm{PA}}$ for Peano arithmetic (including ultrapower soundness and uniform independent sentences), a recursion category $\mathcal{E}_a$ of partial functions computed uniformly across all binary representations of a Miller sequence for real closed fields expanded by constants, and a recursion category $\mathscr{R}_X$ of computable cosheaves over a three-point space.

## Strengths
* **Internalization of First-Order Syntax and Deductive Consequence in Split Recursion Categories**: The paper resolves a foundational open problem in categorical computability by constructing named-variable first-order syntax, complemented capture-avoiding substitution domains, and initial finite-derivation algebras directly inside the regular, extensive total category $\mathrm{Tot}(\mathrm{Split}(\mathscr{R}))$. By assuming monomorphisms have partial inverses in the completion, regular-image deductive closure is seamlessly represented by restriction ranges and refutation idempotents without requiring pretopos exactness or $\alpha$-equivalence quotients.
* **General Diagonal Comparison and Inseparability Criteria without Choice or Local Connectedness**: By formulating the diagonal comparison theorem (Theorem 3.1) using range functors and lifted partial conditionals rather than global choice or local connectedness, the framework applies directly to non-locally connected syntactic categories. Furthermore, the oracle-domain counterexample in $\mathcal{C}_K$ (Appendix C) rigorously demonstrates that the conditional-lifting hypothesis cannot be omitted even for a faithful, product-preserving, zero-reflecting range functor.
* **Functorial Meta-Theory, Free First-Order Theories, and Associated Slice Turing Categories**: The paper establishes a rich categorical meta-theory—including the deductive generation reflection, Galois connections for logical translations, and the free–forgetful adjunction $\Sigma_{\mathsf{FO}} \dashv J_{\mathsf{FO}}$ characterizing essential incompleteness via domain inseparability—and proves that theory inclusions induce fully faithful restriction functors between associated slice Turing categories $\mathrm{Par}(\mathcal{D}/T, \mathrm{Mono})$ that preserve and reflect essential incompleteness.
* **Arithmetic Realization, Ultrapower Soundness, and Uniform Independent Sentences**: Internalizing computably enumerable extensions of Peano arithmetic within the completion of $S_{\mathrm{PA}}$ enables both a categorical transfer of semantic soundness to nonstandard arithmetic ultrapowers via Łoś's theorem ($\mathscr{U}G_{\mathbb{N}} \cong G_M$) and a parameterized derivation family that yields an undecidable sentence uniformly from an axiom-enumeration index.
* **Non-Arithmetic Realizations in Computable Analysis and Topological Cosheaves**: The cross-model generality of the axiomatic framework is convincingly demonstrated through two non-arithmetic realizations: a recursion category $\mathcal{E}_a$ built from representation-independent oracle computation over a Miller sequence (yielding essential incompleteness for real closed fields expanded by constants) and a cosheaf recursion category $\mathscr{R}_X$ on a three-point space that is not restriction-equivalent to $\mathrm{Rec}$ and lacks decidable equality on its universal object.

## Weaknesses
* **Typographical Error in the Definition of Partial Monomorphism**: In Section 2.2 (page 5), the defining condition for a partial monomorphism $p : X \rightharpoonup Y$ is written as $pu = pv \implies p\bar{u} = p\bar{v}$ for $u, v : W \rightharpoonup X$, which introduces a domain/codomain mismatch when $W \neq X$ and holds trivially on total morphisms when $W = X$. Please consider updating the consequent to $\bar{p}u = \bar{p}v$ so that the composites are well-typed for arbitrary $W$ and match the downstream proofs in Proposition 9.2, Proposition B.3, and Theorem D.1.
* **Clarifying the Role of the Global Section Hypothesis in Theorem 5.3**: In Section 5.3, Theorem 5.3 assumes a fixed global logical theorem $\lambda : 1 \to S$ factoring through $U$ to construct the fiber-evaluation retraction $E_U : \mathscr{C}[U] \to \mathscr{C}$. The author might consider adding a brief remark explaining that while any deductively closed theory $U \hookrightarrow S$ admits a canonical section $S \to U \times S$ via propositional tautologies (which suffices to prevent an incomplete theory from becoming complete in $\mathscr{C}[U]$), the global point $1 \to U$ is specifically needed so that pulling back deductively closed extensions from $\mathscr{C}[U]$ to $\mathscr{C}$ evaluates multi-premise rules within a single fiber.
* **Discussing Abstract Conditions for Uniform Independent Sentences**: While Sections 8.2–8.3 construct a parameterized derivation family and a uniform independent-sentence morphism $q : E \to S$ in $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{PA}}$ using the $\Sigma_1$-definability of inequality $x \neq \bar{e}$ to decompose slices via $1 + E_{\neq e} \cong E$, the exposition could be enhanced by briefly noting what abstract hypotheses (such as decidable equality on the index object or pullback stability of initial derivation algebras along points) would be needed to formulate Theorem 8.6 in a general recursion category.
* **Minor Formatting Issue in the Bibliography**: In References item [18] (page 86), the publisher name "Cambridge University Press" has an awkward line-break hyphenation ("U-niversity") that could be easily corrected.

## Potential Issues And Suggestions
### [Framing, Introduction, Related Work, Categorical Preliminaries, and Bibliography] (Pages: 1-5, 86-87)
#### Potential Mistakes and Improvements

- **Definition of partial monomorphism (Section 2.2, page 5, line 21):** A potential concern is that the defining implication for a partial monomorphism $p : X \rightharpoonup Y$ is written as
  $$pu = pv \implies p\bar{u} = p\bar{v} \quad \text{for all } u, v : W \rightharpoonup X,$$
  where the restriction bars are placed over $u$ and $v$ rather than over $p$. Under the paper's composition convention ($gf = g \circ f$), this formula has two issues:
  1. **Type mismatch:** Since $u, v : W \rightharpoonup X$, their restrictions $\bar{u}, \bar{v} : W \rightharpoonup W$ have codomain $W$, whereas $p : X \rightharpoonup Y$ has domain $X$; thus the composites $p\bar{u}$ and $p\bar{v}$ are not defined when $W \neq X$.
  2. **Failure to enforce injectivity on total test morphisms:** Even when $W = X$, if $u, v : X \to X$ are distinct total morphisms into the domain of $p$, then $\bar{u} = 1_X = \bar{v}$, so $p\bar{u} = p\bar{v}$ holds trivially as $p = p$ without forcing $u = v$.

  Replacing the consequent with $\bar{p}u = \bar{p}v$ (or equivalently $u\overline{pu} = v\overline{pv}$ by the fourth restriction axiom), so that the condition reads
  $$pu = pv \implies \bar{p}u = \bar{p}v \quad \text{for all } u, v : W \rightharpoonup X,$$
  ensures that the composites $\bar{p}u, \bar{p}v : W \rightharpoonup X$ are well-typed for arbitrary $W$, matches the existence of a section $s : Y \rightharpoonup X$ (since $ps = \bar{s}$ and $psp = p$ imply $sp = \bar{p}$, giving $\bar{p}u = spu = spv = \bar{p}v$), and aligns with the downstream proofs of Proposition 9.2 (page 52), Proposition B.3 (page 77, where total morphisms $u, v : H_j \to D_p \to Y$ factoring through the domain subfunctor $D_p$ satisfy $\bar{p}u = u$ and $\bar{p}v = v$), and Theorem D.1 (page 84).

#### Minor Corrections and Typos

- **References, item [18] (page 86):** "Cambridge U-niversity Press" is hyphenated after the single initial letter `U-` at the line break.
