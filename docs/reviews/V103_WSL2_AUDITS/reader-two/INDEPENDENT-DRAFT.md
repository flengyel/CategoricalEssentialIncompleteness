## Checks specific to this edition

**First-order adjunction.** A total assignment on the whole atom object extends uniquely to a logical translation. The signed bounds say precisely when this translation is a theory morphism. Restricting it recovers the assignment. `homEquiv` gives both the inverse and uniqueness, while `unit_arrow` identifies the comma object's underlying morphism. The source also proves the converse in Corollary 4.8 with the true fiber of a Boolean assignment and verifies consistency for the particular arithmetic witness of Lemma 8.9. The comma construction and the greatest-lower-bound claim immediately after 4.7 are expanded at the actual types in the supplemental checks. [Proof inputs](PROOF-INPUTS.md) · [Supplemental source](Applications.lean.txt)

**Numerical preface proof.** Lemma 8.1 supplies a primitive-recursive map sending each number to the code of the indicated closed numeral instance. The preimage of a putative recursive sentence separator is recursive. Its switch has first fiber the complement and second fiber the preimage. The proof produces an index for a partial morphism in the PA category and proves equality of its standard interpretation with that switch, including the coproduct comparison. Corollary 3.2 rules out this lift. Its totality in the standard model imposes no source PA proof of totality. [Proof inputs](PROOF-INPUTS.md)

**Slice, Miller and fork.** Both adjunctions in Theorem 5.2 concern the underlying ordinary categories. For the Miller cuts, the first-digit decision at a particular representation need not define the same set at the next representation. If a fixed set had one deciding program on every representation, its two partial identities would give a complementary separator. In the fork each pair of complementary subdiagrams partitions the c.e. carrier at each vertex. On that carrier the two searches terminate in exactly one branch. Naturality of the copair and its inverse forces the branch to be preserved by both diagram morphisms. The undecided endpoint atom exists for each consistent extension with c.e. axioms in that language. [Formal coverage](FORMAL-COVERAGE.json.gz)

**Sources and page references.** Di Paola and Heller explicitly announce their intended use of the diagonal result. Theorem 8.15 states its dominical hypotheses. A search of the publication record returned their 1987 article and no subsequent joint treatment of the announced topic. Shoenfield's Theorems 1 and 2 use the same degree and the inverse image of provability. Smullyan's Theorems 22 and 23(b) have different definability and separation hypotheses, respectively. Ramesh's Definition 2.7, its following semantic explanation and Theorem 4.19 give the introspective theory and guarded fixed-point comparison. Murwanashyaka, Pakhomov and Visser give the complete sign-assignment comparison in their definition of a propositional-logic-like theory, Theorem 4.3 and §4.4. The manuscript's inference that a complete signed extension is c.e. exactly for a recursive set of positive signs uses computability of the two signed generators and their disjoint theoremhood.
