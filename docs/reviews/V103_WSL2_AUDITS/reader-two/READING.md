## Abstract, preface, and introduction

The central claim is conditional on consistency of the freely generated sentence theory. For any two disjoint subobjects of one object of atoms, absence of a complementary separator is equivalent to absence of a complete consistent closed extension in the same presentation. A map from the atom object to the sentences of a consistent target, taking positive atoms to theorems and negative atoms to refutations, induces a unique logical translation. Pullback then gives inseparability of the theorem and refutation pairs in every consistent extension. In the reverse direction one needs the specific closed theory of the Boolean sentences true under the assignment associated with a separator. Concrete examples additionally owe existence and consistency of actual theories. A theorem conditional on realization data will not certify an example by itself.

- **3 (03, 1–179).** The two summand domains annihilate and cover by coproduct uniqueness. - **4.1–4.3 (03a, 1–272).** Initial inference algebra plus regular images gives the theorem subobject, and the partial inverse of its mono identifies its idempotent with the conclusion range. Negation pulls it back to refutations. Explicit splittings extend any range functor to split objects by conjugation with inclusion/retraction. Check identities, composition, restrictions, ranges, zeros, coproduct isomorphisms, and joins. Soundness through a validity mono gives a unique factorization of the image, hence disjointness when validity and its negations are disjoint. Under the diagonal bounds/lift and pointwise consistency, semantic and source joins are proper. Any larger disjoint theory/refutation pair receiving a reduction stays inseparable by pulling back any complementary separator. A proper subobject need not omit a global element: the regular action of a nontrivial group admits no fixed point in its free summand. Endomorphisms of the identity splitting identify source and split separability.
- **4.4 (03b, 1–192).** Gen is extensive, monotone and idempotent, gives the reflection onto fixed points and, after ordinary image, the adjunction from the sentence slice to the theory order. Translations fix variables, preserve four logical constructors, preserve admissibility and its value, and admit maps on the arity-indexed logical rule instances. - **4.5 (03b, 193–328).** A regular change of base with total sentence comparison and a conclusion-preserving derivation comparison for every axiom subobject commutes with generation after image. - **4.6 (03b, 329–574).** For every atom object, the only nonlogical constructors in the source are nullary atoms, terms are variables. A closed-sentence assignment uniquely extends by the free algebra. List isomorphism respects concatenation, membership and deletion, so the fold respects free occurrences, successful substitutions and the seven schemes, as well as generalization and modus ponens. The identity assignment on target sentences produces a consistent inverse-image theory, verifying that J actually lands among pairs generating consistent theories. The signed bounds exactly characterize theory morphisms, and restricting/ extending are natural inverses. Unit, counit and comma-initial object are explicit. The true fiber is closed under all rules, contains the signed axioms and is complete and consistent.
- **5.1 (04-theory, 1–130).** A monic span represents right leg composed with the partial inverse of the left. Domain splitting inverts this assignment; the domain of a composite is obtained by pullback. In each slice the universal object is the constant original Turing object and the evaluator retains the parameter. Retractions are graph embeddings with partial inverses; the parameter for a slice map combines its parameter in the base with the projection to the slice base. Constant objects form an isotypical recursion category and every slice object splits a domain on one. In every parameter slice the pullback sentence theory is closed by the rule construction.
- **5.2 (04-theory, 131–227).** For a monic theory inclusion, postcomposition on monic spans is fully faithful. The terminal object on the larger base cannot lie in the essential image for a proper inclusion. - **5.3 (04-theory, 228–371).** A single theorem point splitting through the chosen theory makes evaluation of its constant-family functor a retraction up to natural isomorphism.
