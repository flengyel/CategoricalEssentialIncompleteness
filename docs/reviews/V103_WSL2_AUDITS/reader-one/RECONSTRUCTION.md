## Abstract, preface and introduction

The condition on the base is substantial. Write `D = Tot(Split(R))`. Regularity, disjoint stable finite coproducts, partial inverses of monos, a common decidable variable object, parameter-stable syntax and lists, and initial inference algebras for the required axiom objects allow one to use the image of the conclusion of a proof as a subobject. The authors of each model construction must exhibit these properties for their indicated languages. The central equivalence is quantified over disjoint subobjects of an object of atoms for which the signed theory is consistent. A complete consistent **internal** closed extension in the same language amounts to a separator. A total assignment into the sentence object of a consistent target extends uniquely to a logical translation. It preserves the theory precisely when both signed bounds hold. This gives a comma-category initial object and a free/forgetful adjunction. A target receiving an inseparable pair has inseparable theorem and refutation domains in every consistent extension. A point outside a proper subobject requires a separate argument.

In the arithmetic example the inverse image under the primitive-recursive numeral map pulls a recursive separator of theorem/refutation codes back to a separator of the even/odd diagonal domains. A program for the resulting total, tagged decision represents a **partial** source morphism. No proof of PA-totality is needed. Each oracle representing a Miller sequence can decide a separator by reading one digit. No representation-independent separator is decided by one program on all its names. The diagram example imposes computability at each vertex and naturality on the two structure maps. Its two endpoints independently have an undecided atom in every consistent c.e.-axiomatized extension. The final preface claim concerns the functor induced by a theory inclusion on the partial-map completions. Adjoints are asserted for their underlying categories. The Palomar claim has a specified entry, version and count of selected declarations. Literary claims include a 1987 program, a statement about its publication history, Shoenfield’s inverse-image argument, Smullyan’s criterion, Heller’s motivation and newer comparisons in the introduction.
