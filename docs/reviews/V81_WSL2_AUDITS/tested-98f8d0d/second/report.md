## Two findings in the printed prose

1. **Section 1, lines 173–175.** The sentence reporting Di Paola–Heller 8.15 needs a nonzero identity on its Turing object. Take the category with one object and one morphism. It has absorbing zeros, dominical near products, sections and binary coproducts. Its unique τ of type $X\times X\to X+X$ parameterizes every morphism of that type, since both sides of the parameterization equation are the sole morphism. The two subdomains obtained from $\tau\Delta$ coincide. The sole pair of domains is complementary, and it bounds both. The published last step identifies the pullback of a cover with both zero and $1_X$. A contradiction follows only from $1_X\ne 0$. At the printed fixed source, `DominicalLiteratureChecks.nonzero_turing_object` proves the disjointness and nonexistence of a separator after adding this hypothesis. `TrivialDominicalCategory.Checks.turing_with_separable_domains` proves that the hypothesis can fail. The repair is to state $1_X\ne 0$ in the historical qualification. Corollaries 3.2 and 3.3 already impose a nonzero target identity, and each concrete application verifies the corresponding condition. The affected conclusion is the unqualified assertion in §1. A consistent complete theory with a decidable axiom presentation refutes the second reading. Take the extension with empty additional axioms. Every sentence is decided. The intended opening can say “A presentation is effectively extensible if a recursive function assigns an independent sentence to the index of each c.e. set of additional axioms whose addition leaves the theory consistent.” The only affected passage is the description of Pour-El’s property.
