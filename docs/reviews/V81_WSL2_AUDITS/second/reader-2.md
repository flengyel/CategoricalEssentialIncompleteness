**L3. The numerical equivalence-relation theory.** After identifying the work of Murwanashyaka, Pakhomov and Visser, the manuscript states that for disjoint sets the resulting theory is essentially undecidable iff the sets are recursively inseparable. The authors' theory extends Janiczak's theory of an equivalence relation. The signs specify existence or nonexistence of a class of size n+1. Their §4.4 gives the biconditional with the alternative of inconsistency for arbitrary c.e. pairs. Disjointness makes the signed extension consistent. I found no checked definition of this theory and no application establishing its essential-undecidability biconditional. Corollary 4.8 concerns nullary sentence symbols, arbitrary internal subobjects, and essential incompleteness of consistent closed extensions. An application to Janiczak's theory would require a mathematical interpretation and a separate decidability argument. The historical comparison is accurate and independent of every later proof. [Manuscript's assertion](https://github.com/flengyel/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/blob/cb455f79853241e0214d710f1a73a6e94b9c02be/manuscript/latex/sections/01-introduction.tex#L189) · [Authors’ published result, §4.4](https://doi.org/10.1093/logcom/exad005) · [Authors’ open version, §4.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.08174#page=10).

The reading supported by Pour-El's definition is that *effective extensibility of the chosen presentation* means the existence of such a function. If consistency alone is its antecedent, take the complete theory of the one-element structure in the language of equality, which has a finite axiomatization, and take the empty set of additional axioms. The extension is consistent and has no independent sentence. Theorem 8.6 explicitly requires extensions of PA and supplies an actual morphism before quantifying over the input indices. Its argument is unaffected.
