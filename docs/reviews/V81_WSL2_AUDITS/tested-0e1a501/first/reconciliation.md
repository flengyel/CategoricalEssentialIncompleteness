## The degenerate case of Di Paola–Heller's theorem

The original Definition 2.0 defines totality by cancellation of zero. Definition 2.2 gives near-product axioms N1–N4. Definitions 4.1–4.2 require all objects to be isomorphic and a fixed-source Turing morphism. Definition 6.2 requires sections. Definition 7.1 gives compatible coproducts, requiring totality of sums of total morphisms and invertibility of distribution. Section 8 assumes a recursion category and Theorem 8.15 assumes the indicated section and coproduct structure. Definition 8.14 allows any complemented domain as separator. The last step on p. 626 produces $0_X=1_X$. That equality can hold under the stated hypotheses. [Di Paola–Heller, §§2, 4, 6–8](https://doi.org/10.2307/2274352)

Take one object $X$ with one endomorphism. It serves as the identity, zero, every projection and injection, every section and the Turing morphism. The only endomorphism is total by Definition 2.0, the two coproduct conditions hold, and both domains of the diagonal test are the sole restriction idempotent. This idempotent is its own complement. It separates the two domains. My Lean instance verifies the dominical axioms, sections, coproducts, distribution, exact evaluator type, equality $1_X=0_X$, and an explicit separator. The evaluator and identity equality use no axioms. The separation proof uses propositional extensionality. The additional hypothesis $1_X\ne 0_X$ supplies the contradiction at the end of the printed proof. [Counterexample at the Q types](DominicalOne.lean) · [Kernel output](DominicalOne.output)

The v81 sentence in §1 reports the historical theorem and mentions two kinds of structure. It gives no new proof of the 1987 result. Section 3 makes the nonzeroness needed by its own argument explicit. I record one failed *historical universalization* only in the last row of the count table. The 1987 paper itself uses a total morphism as necessarily nonzero in its preceding discussion of creativity. This indicates that nonzeroness was intended. ## New proof evidence on Visser and §8.3

For Visser, write $T\preceq U$ when every sentence provable in $T$ is provable in $U$. A decision procedure for the ordinary theorem words of $U$, applied to the encoded assignment, decides a set containing the positive side. If it contained an element of the negative side, injectivity of encoding and $T\preceq U$ would make $U$ prove a sentence and its negation. This contradicts its consistency. My proof works for any encoded first-order sentence language, any such computable assignment and extensions given by inclusion of theorem sets. The second reader used inclusion of axiom sets. The first formulation also applies to different presentations of the same theory. This supplies the general mathematical argument as independent Lean evidence. Its source file is an audit addition after Q. [First reader's proof and statement](Visser.lean) · [Kernel output](Visser.output) · [Visser's definition of extension, §2.1](https://research-portal.uu.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/230468330/s00153-024-00911-y.pdf)

Both readers identified the ambiguity in §8.3 independently. Before opening the second judgment, I also compiled a counterexample using a computably axiomatized complete Janiczak theory. The theorem words of the canonical model for the empty spectrum are decidable. Soundness identifies them with its axiom words, and every sentence or its negation is an axiom. At an index for the empty additional axiom set, no sentence assignment can give the two required unprovability statements. This refutes the universal-existence reading even for an assignment without a computability requirement. The intended property of a presentation and Theorem 8.6 with PA as its base keep their original meanings.
