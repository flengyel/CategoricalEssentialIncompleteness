## A counterexample, a scope ambiguity and a locator

**D1.** The assertion attributed to Di Paola–Heller in §1, lines 173–175, is false at the stated hypotheses. Definitions 4.1–4.2 and Theorem 8.15 in the journal paper admit the one-object category with one morphism. It has a zero, near products, compatible coproducts, sections and a Turing morphism. The identity is zero. Both diagonal domains are the sole restriction idempotent, which separates them from one another. The published proof derives identity equals zero and treats this equality as contradictory. My notation in the first source-only notes referred to a nontrivial setting. Comparison with the original definitions and the checked example corrects that qualification. Corollary 3.3 explicitly assumes the nonzero identity needed in its comparison. [Original paper](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2274352) · [counterexample and separator](../../../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46TrivialDominicalCategory.lean#L106) · [Corollary 3.3](../../../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46Comparison.lean#L132)

**Q1.** §8.3, lines 4–8, says that Pour-El investigates effective extensibility of a fixed consistent presentation, followed by a declarative assertion that a recursive function gives independent sentences. This can describe the definition of the property or assert the existence of the function under consistency alone. The complete decidable presentation with an empty family of additional axioms refutes the second reading. For the specified PA presentation the existence statement is proved at the required order of quantifiers. [Opening paragraph](../../../../../manuscript/latex/sections/08b-uniform-independent-sentences.tex#L4) · [counterexample](../../../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46EffectiveExtensibility.lean#L24) · [PA witness](../../../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46ArithmeticUniformIndependent.lean#L204)

**L1.** Di Paola–Heller, numbered item (3) in the introduction to the 1987 paper, appears on printed page 595. The previous page ends at item (2). §8 gives page 594. Under consistency and numeralwise definability of all recursive sets, theorem and refutation supports are recursively inseparable as asserted. The error concerns the locator. [Printed citation](../../../../../manuscript/latex/sections/08-essential-incompleteness-of-arithmetic.tex#L241) · [arithmetic theorem](../../../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/ArithmeticInseparability.lean#L84)

## Original assertions with native proofs

- Cockett's Theorems 7.8 and 7.14 use different objects. The first constructs two program domains using discrete diagonal tests and stable compatible joins. The second uses a code recognizing restriction order among total program points to produce a proper nonzero idempotent on the restriction terminal object. [Inseparable pair](../../../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46DiscreteTuringInseparability.lean#L112) · [terminal predicate](../../../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46ProgramOrder.lean#L137)
- The actual binary-relation theory of Murwanashyaka–Pakhomov–Visser adds assertions and denials of finite equivalence-class sizes to Janiczak's base. The proof uses native first-order theorem codes, disjoint enumerations, a model for each recursive separator and a decision procedure for its complete theory. [Signed theory](../../../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46JaniczakSignedTheory.lean#L60) · [decision on raw codes](../../../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46JaniczakNativeDecision.lean#L91)
- Cockett–Lack III 4.5 has both directions. For a P-category use the partial pairing in PCats, the two projection laws, totality of both projections, restriction of the pairing and its uniqueness by projections. In the opposite direction all P-category laws follow from the binary restriction products, with the same restriction operator. The Cockett–Hofstra 3.4 evaluator/retract recognition is also proved with total parameters for every object. [P-category equations](../../../../../Lean/PCats/PCat.lean#L148) · [restriction and uniqueness](../../../../../Lean/PCats/PCat.lean#L346) · [converse and compatibility](../../../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46RestrictionProductCategory.lean#L100) · [Turing recognition](../../../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46TuringObjectRecognition.lean#L22)
- Di Paola–Montagna's failure of local connectedness is realized for every consistent c.e. extension of PA on its unprimed syntactic category. The purported primed composition fails at the congruence of representatives. Failure of composition is an imported theorem of `FailureOfComposition`, applied with the actual c.e. axiom set; the unprimed presentation is identified with the formal object terms. - Visser's reduction-to-essential-undecidability implication works for arbitrary encoded first-order sentences on a common language and every consistent extension. Kirst–Peters’ theorem has the synthetic enumeration of partial functions, semidecidability and strong polarity hypotheses of their construction. Voutsadakis’ adjunction ranges over hierarchies on finite variable supports and formula-valued relational signature morphisms. [Visser](../../../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46EssentialUndecidability.lean#L30) · [Kirst–Peters](../../../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46SyntheticIndependence.lean#L79) · [Voutsadakis](../../../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46RelationalSignatureAdjunction.lean#L67)

The other references in the [39-entry publication notes](primary-reading.md) furnish definitions, named operations or bibliographic context. In particular the exposition identifies the bound-variable conventions of Fiore–Plotkin–Turi, Gabbay–Pitts and Katsumata, and describes a first-order signature with a quantifier. These are statements about those authors' definitions and examples. V81's named-variable syntax and successful substitution have their own different definitions, including an explicit admissibility mono. Hilbert schemes specify the adopted calculus. The abstract initiality of syntax, lists and derivations is a hypothesis, then witnessed in the three concrete settings.
