# Checks against the original publications

I completed the reading-order reconstruction and sealed its digest before consulting the candidate. This supplementary comparison uses authors’ texts and journal scans. The direct bibliographic statements have distinct mathematical scopes. A citation or a coincident conclusion for one special class of categories does not by itself supply an application at a more general printed type.

| Printed passage | What I checked in the original |
|---|---|
| §1, lines 124–125 and §9, lines 35–46 | Miller, Theorems 6.1 and 6.3, and Lemma 6.2. The value at coordinate `e` must equal the real computed uniformly by oracle program `e` from every representation. The existence proof has a simultaneous fixed point of a closed convex-valued correspondence on the Hilbert cube. Merely containing all the computed reals somewhere in a list cannot give the `h(e)` calculation in §9. Bauer and Hanson, Theorem 2.2, uses real-number names parameterizing the sequence. [Miller](https://people.math.wisc.edu/~jsmiller8/Papers/continuous.pdf) [Bauer and Hanson §2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.01256v3#S2.SS2) |
| §1, lines 126–127 | Bauer and Hanson, Theorem 6.4. Their parameterized realizability construction gives a topos in whose internal logic the Dedekind reals are countable. One needs an epimorphism from its natural-number object onto its Dedekind real object. This sentence would still need a kernel application if all of the cut example and its Miller-sequence existence were independently formalized. No later mathematical inference in v81 uses the topos. [Bauer and Hanson §6.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.01256v3#S6.SS1) |
| §1, lines 166–171 | Heller 1990, Theorem 9.2, assumes a locally connected iteration category and a uniformly generated isotypical B-sharp subcategory of its total morphisms and says the Turing completion is a recursion category. Lengyel 2004, Theorem 26, replaces local connectedness by a faithful iteration functor to a separated iteration category. The cited corrected version states this under `thm:main`, lines 619–636. The specialization with identity functor gives the separated case. Its proof constructs the uniform Turing index for a morphism with arbitrary parameter object. The explicit fork construction in Appendix B is independent. [Heller](https://doi.org/10.2307/2274486) [Original APAL article](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0168-0072(03)00060-5) [Author’s corrected source](https://github.com/flengyel/errata/blob/5555e4fa1da7b8e3a0836dd1fbf1ca385cacd03b/lengyel2004/lengyel2004_repairs_v10.tex#L619-L636) |
| §1, lines 173–175 and §8, lines 240–245 | Di Paola and Heller, Theorem 8.15, assumes c-plus-dominicality and a Turing morphism `τ : X × X ⇀ X + X`. Definition 6.2 requires a partial right inverse for **each morphism**. Definition 7.1 requires compatible binary coproducts, including distributivity. The two domains are obtained by the two summand projections on `τΔ`. Result (3) appears on original journal page 595, although v81 §8 cites page 594. In that result, the numerical criterion uses the ability of the consistent arithmetic theory to define every recursive set. The two source assertions must be compared separately. [Journal article](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-symbolic-logic/article/abs/dominical-categories-recursion-theory-without-elements1-2/2E11506A81794B06EF34603881D79394) |
| §1, lines 175–178 and §8, lines 315–327 | Bernardi’s numerical proposition gives a fixed point of every total recursive self-map modulo a precomplete relation and recursive inseparability of distinct classes. The example in the paper requires precompleteness of the particular PA-provably-equal-graphs quotient. The printed categorical results also quantify over categorical endomorphisms and the corresponding notion of precompleteness. [Bernardi’s article](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1986-0848889-9) |
| §1, lines 179–180 | Montagna, Remark 3.1, expressly includes `S` among the settings with effectively inseparable pairs. The application appeals to Di Paola and Heller, Theorem 8.18. Effective inseparability includes production of a witness outside disjoint effective enlargements. [Montagna](https://doi.org/10.1305/ndjfl/1093634998) |
| §1, lines 180–187 and Appendix C | Lengyel 2006, Theorem 4.1, p. 11, states the comparison under a weak Turing morphism, a locally connected split target with weak choice, and its specified preservation and nonzero conditions. The printed proof uses a section obtained in the target to form a source index after identifying the two categories. The counterexample in Appendix C is relevant to these exact hypotheses. Cockett, Theorem 7.8, uses discrete Turing categories with finite compatible joins stable under composition. Its proof obtains an inseparable pair of restriction idempotents. [Lengyel’s report](https://academicworks.cuny.edu/gc_cs_tr/277/) [Cockett’s notes](https://cspages.ucalgary.ca/~robin/talks/estonia-winter-2010/estonia-notes.pdf) |
| §1, lines 189–196 | Murwanashyaka, Pakhomov and Visser, §4.4, variant of Theorem 1.1. For c.e. sets X,Y their theory is J extended by `A_i` for i∈X and `¬A_i` for i∈Y. Here `A_i` is their equivalence-class-size sentence. For disjoint X,Y the inconsistency alternative vanishes by joint independence of the size sentences. The remaining iff is exactly essential undecidability versus recursive inseparability. [Author text §4.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2207.08174#S4.SS4) |
| §1, lines 198–206 | Van Dijk and Gietelink Oldenziel, Theorems 5.19–5.20, concern arithmetic universes. The first says a syntactically complete internal arithmetic universe in the indicated recursive initial setting is trivial. In the second, consistency of the initial arithmetic universe implies that its subobject `[[True′ = False′]] → 1` is different from bottom. The internal category and truth-value subobject matter in both. [Authors’ text §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2004.10482#S5) |
| §1, lines 208–214 and §2 | Cockett and Hofstra, Theorem 3.4, characterizes a Turing category by cartesian restriction structure, retractions to its Turing object and a Turing morphism. Theorem 4.9 has two directions. A partial combinatory algebra gives its category of computable morphisms, which is Turing. Conversely a Turing object gives a PCA, and the initial Turing category is equivalent to a suitable relative computable category after splitting idempotents. Cockett, Theorem 7.14, gives a restriction idempotent on 1 unequal both to 0 and 1 from a nontrivial Turing category with zero and the specified provability predicate for the restriction order on programs. [Cockett and Hofstra](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apal.2008.04.005) [Cockett §7.5](https://cspages.ucalgary.ca/~robin/talks/estonia-winter-2010/estonia-notes.pdf) |
| §4, signed examples | Visser, Lemma 23, maps a recursively inseparable pair into the proved and refuted sentences to infer essential undecidability. Kirst and Peters, Theorem 20, assumes an EPF enumeration and strong separation of the two synthetic diagonal sets, then produces an independent sentence for any allowed consistent semidecidable extension. Neither statement follows merely by giving an arbitrary decidable subobject. [Visser](https://research-portal.uu.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/230468330/s00153-024-00911-y.pdf) [Kirst and Peters](https://www.ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/KirstPeters_2023_Goedel.pdf) |
| §4, first-order adjunction | Voutsadakis, Theorem 7, gives `F ⊣ U` between HSet and Sign, with `U` assigning the many-sorted hierarchy of formulas, `F` the free signaturization, and functorial formula substitution. The theorem has the hom naturality or equivalent unit and counit triangle equations for those categories. The paper develops an independent free signed-predicate theory. [Published paper](https://www.voutsadakis.com/RESEARCH/PUBLISHED/fol.pdf) |
| §6, comparison with the primed construction | Lengyel, Corollary 2, exhibits failure of congruence of pointwise provable equality under composition of unary programs for every consistent c.e. extension of PA. Only uniform PA-provable graph equality is used to form the unprimed category in v81. [Author text, Corollary 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.25556v3) |
| Appendix A, lines 373–375 | In Aczel, Proposition 1.1.4, an arbitrary finitary set of rules generates precisely the conclusions with finite proofs. Proposition 1.4.5 gives its tree formulation. Finitarity means each premise set is finite, with no single bound required for their cardinalities. A transcription that begins with a uniform bound N or a chosen initial proof algebra has extra premises. [Primary text, §§1.1 and 1.4](https://lawrencecpaulson.github.io/papers/Aczel-Inductive-Defs.pdf) |
| Appendix A, lines 416–418 | Wißmann, Lemma 3.6, says that for any endofunctor preserving the left class E of an (E,M)-factorization system, the system lifts to Alg(F) with E- and M-carried morphisms. The paper’s initial algebra in the slice and the image of its conclusion morphism are one use of the factorization idea. [Published lemma, p. 31:10](https://lmcs.episciences.org/10014/pdf) |
| Appendix D, lines 60–62 | Di Paola and Montagna, Corollary 4.4, pp. 657–658, proves failure of local connectedness of unprimed `S_T` for all consistent c.e. T extending PA. In the Σ₁-unsound case the zero domains are the only connected ones. In the Σ₁-sound case the connected nonzero domains amount to provable numeral domains. The finite-consistency partial identity agrees there with the identity but differs globally. The target interpretation category in Appendix D has externally set-indexed coordinates and is locally connected. These two assertions require different objects. [Journal article](https://doi.org/10.2307/2274707) |
