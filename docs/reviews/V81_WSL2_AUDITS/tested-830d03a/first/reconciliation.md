## The two range-category claims

Appendix A, lines 564–566, identifies a theorem for **every split range category** `C`. In `Tot(C)`, the left class consists of total morphisms of full range, and the right class consists of total morphisms which are partial isomorphisms in `C`. Theorem I.4.7 supplies the factorization, orthogonality and stability of the left class under pullback along the right class. The official source’s `range_regularImage` starts with an individual regular epimorphism, a specified morphism and its partial inverse. The theorem identifies the range of that composite. A general factorization system on `Tot(C)` requires more. [Range Categories I, Theorem 4.7](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/265194097_Range_categories_I_General_theory) · [A.7’s Lean statement](../../../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46RegularImage.lean#L119)

Lines 567–568 cite §3.16 and Proposition II.3.17. Let `D` have finite limits and let `(E,M)` be a stable factorization system on `D`. Then this is the regular-epimorphism/monomorphism system if and only if `M` comprises all monomorphisms. In the reverse implication one must recover regular epimorphisms from the given stable system. The project instead constructs ranges on `Par(D,Mono)` for a given regular `D`. It obtains the partial inverse of each mono in this category and applies A.7 to the conclusion image. The category and the range identity in lines 570–590 are thus verified. I found no application of I.4.7 or II.3.17 at the two original types.
