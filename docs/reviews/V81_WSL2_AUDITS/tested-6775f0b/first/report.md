## Findings

1. In §1, lines 173–175, the stated section and coproduct hypotheses allow the one-object, one-morphism category. Its unique morphism is a Turing morphism, its identity equals zero, and its diagonal domains admit a separator. The last inference on p. 626 of Di Paola and Heller requires the identity of the Turing object to differ from zero. Adding that hypothesis gives the checked `DominicalLiteratureChecks.nonzero_turing_object`. Section 3 states its own nonzero hypothesis where the contradiction needs it. The explicit counterexample and the qualified theorem settle the two statements separately. [Printed passage][dphtex] · [Original, pp. 625–626][dph] · [Counterexample][counter] · [Qualified inference][qualified]
2. The opening of §8.3 leaves unclear whether effective extensibility is a property of a presentation or a consequence of consistency for every presentation. A complete consistent decidable theory and the empty added axiom set refute the latter reading. State that a presentation is effectively extensible if a recursive function selects such a sentence for every consistent c.e. extension of that presentation. Theorem 8.6 supplies one morphism for the specified family over PA. Its existence precedes the quantifier over externally consistent indices. [Printed passage][pourtex] · [Pour-El][pourel] · [Witness][extender] · [Uniform morphism][uniform]
3. In §8.1 the citation before Corollary 8.4 assigns Di Paola and Heller’s item (3) to p. 594. Item (3) is on p. 595. This is one additional bibliographic error. The inclusions and the numerical incompleteness conclusion have separate proofs. [Printed locator][pagetex] · [Original article][dph]
