## Printed structure, assumptions and dependencies

The central result compares two conditions on disjoint subobjects of a total category. The theory generated by signed nullary predicates must itself be consistent. The conclusion quantifies over **all consistent closed internal extensions in the same language**. At numerical and endpoint realizations the external extension classes instead carry explicit semidecidability or c.e. axiomatizability. The constant-family theorem requires deductions for arbitrary slice axiom families and a point in each consistent theory. The abstract explicitly assumes a regular total category, disjoint stable sums, partial inverses for monos, decidable variable equality, parameterized syntax and lists, initial derivations. Ordinary closure alone cannot supply stable syntax, nor does existence of a single representative assume uniformity across several indices. The abstract's uniform numerical claim uses a fixed c.e. numbering with the PA axioms hardwired, as in 8.6. The code output denotes a sentence, not its truth value.

- §1 introduces generalized elements, the signed axiom image and closure, restriction/extension of atoms, the comparison with interpretation, the three applications, and related work. The historical descriptions of Rosolini, Heller, Di Paola–Montagna, Di Paola–Heller, Bernardi, Montagna, Cockett, Murwanashyaka–Pakhomov–Visser, van Dijk–Gietelink Oldenziel and Cockett–Hofstra are claims about other authors' texts.
- §2 gives the four restriction axioms, zero, order, compatibility, meets/joins, range minimality and the additional agreement law, splitting, all-idempotent completion, monic-span calculus, restriction products and coproducts, coproduct comparison, connected domains, weak choice, recursion and Turing categories. The product-by-two-colours functor preserves restriction, range, zeros, sums but its product comparison omits differently coloured pairs. Proper subobjects need not have missing global points (§4 example of a free group orbit adjoined to a fixed point); component tests in the numerical examples are additional.
- §§3–4 compare the lifted conditional to a Turing diagonal, pull a signed extension back and extend interpretations by parameterized initiality. Distinguish object existence from the universal property of an already-present algebra. The regular-image notion of generation factors through the sentence pullback. When changing base, the proof comparison for every axiom family is an actual hypothesis; a distributive law together with a right adjoint is one way to obtain it.
- §5 treats split restriction categories as partial maps, the ambidextrous functor on partial-map slices induced by a monomorphism, and evaluation along a theorem. An infinite c.e. domain is recursively bijective with the naturals in the splitting by first-halting stages and preimage search. It is unnecessary for the theorem to choose a recursive enumeration of all models, a separator, or an isomorphism of the unsplit base categories.
- §6 uses uniform graph provability over arithmetic. Formal products and tagged sums have unique set-theoretic representatives with von Neumann naturals and Kuratowski pairs. The paper rejects pointwise provability as a quotient compatible with the proposed composition, by a separately cited counterexample. Sigma-one search and split idempotents realize carrier predicates and functional total relations, bounded-arity data, sequence witnesses, derivation trees, images, admissible substitution. An arbitrary axiom subobject is reduced to its carrier presentation. The selector in 6.4 uses the finite disjunction of positive graph tests in PA; standard finite range of an arbitrary implementation does not imply that PA proves its universal output bound. The written proof explicitly defines the guarded formula. The tree-code estimate used for course-of-values recursion is valid for the shifted two-adic pairing.
- §7 distinguishes the model M interpreting arithmetic codes from the model K validating the sentences in a theory. All interpreted graph witnesses range over M. Completeness is used later to obtain K; standard programs alone do not justify interpreting nonstandard proofs by ordinary set-theoretic induction on externally finite trees. Regularity of the ultrapower functor and Łoś transfer standard-code validity without defining full satisfaction on nonstandard codes of K. The validity criterion is invariant under equal representatives and agrees with truth in K on constant families.
- §8 treats the signed comparison in a fixed PA category, an indexed derivation algebra, a uniform morphism, the numerical split-equivalence and computable proof-code comparison, inclusions for numeral instances, and a separate theory in a nullary signature for the slice application. The arithmetic map sends n to the code of the assertion that the diagonal computation at n is even. Inclusion of theorems/rebuttals is parametric in Γ; an e with Γ_e inconsistent is outside the conclusion. The countable-model observation of Appendix D is distinct from numerical evaluation at the standard model.
- §9 requires the same oracle program to compute one common partial function, with the same domain and values, from every representation. The precision-wise rational names can depend on the oracle. Negative information on a compact representation fibre and positive halting information are enumerated to test unanimity. The two cuts of the Miller sequence at one-half have a separator depending on each oracle. No single separator can be decided by one index on all names. In the deduction example we retain oracle-independent results in the regular-image/kernal-pair argument even when the preimage found depends on the oracle. The language is that of real closed ordered fields with named coordinates, not the nullary free language.
- §10 gives the fork category of c.e. numerical diagrams, embeds its object into a universal natural diagram, transports through the Karoubi envelope, obtains a Turing evaluation at either endpoint and obtains a nonuniversal central evaluation. The endpoint completeness conclusions quantify over ordinary c.e. consistent extensions. The language retraction from first-order to propositional formulas is sound for the single-element equality semantics and yields literal conservativity. A global tautology lifts it to the slice.
- Appendix A builds named-variable terms, formulas and occurrence lists, parameterized substitutions with a failure summand, complemented free-for domain, inductive closure via regular images, seven Hilbert axiom schemes, generalization and modus ponens. Canonical propositional calculus has no unary inference. In regular categories without extensive sums, R₁=0 need not make its pullback initial, but its required operation is the given pullback projection and imposes no restriction. This detail suffices.
- Appendix B proves a Set-valued cosheaf/diagram equivalence, then works in numerical diagrams, not numerical cosheaves on arbitrary covers. A pushout at the whole space need not preserve products or monomorphisms. A universal fork stores central and two endpoint programs with compatibility tests. Weak choice uses injectivity, and the counterexample counts subobjects of the terminal object. The variable object for first-order syntax on the fork is constant ℕ, whereas V lacks decidable equality.
- Appendix C takes ordinary partial recursive extensions restricted to K-c.e. domains. It separates the specific diagonal by the parity of an ordinary partial computation and proves its total conditional has no source lift. Disjoint morphisms need not even have a common upper bound. Appendix D uses all countable models with underlying set ℕ, completeness, downward Löwenheim–Skolem, coordinatewise weak choice, extension quotient, diagonal counterfunction and the theory of the standard model. Appendix E gives seven worked examples and archival links: restriction/extension, both separator directions, joint finite-support valuation (internally finite in arbitrary PA models), the lifted comparison and its counterexample, evaluation along a logical point, the uniform arithmetic diagonal, and parameterized Miller proofs and their ranges for arbitrary axiom monos. Bibliographical sources and provenance links are outside object-language theorem statements.
