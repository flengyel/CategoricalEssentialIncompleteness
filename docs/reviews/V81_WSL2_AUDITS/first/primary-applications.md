## Results used in mathematical inferences

- **Ordinary and oracle programming.** Montagna gives the Kleene graph convention, a formalized parameter theorem and the Σ₁ partial identity in *Pathologies*, pp. 108–109 and Note 1 on p. 115. Its object system at p. 105 distinguishes universally proved equality from agreement separately proved at the numerals. Di Paola and Montagna, pp. 644–646, discuss primitive-recursive definitional extensions and use the latter distinction for extensions of PA. I read the two journal scans. Montagna does not specify the present first-success trace, our two pairing systems, or a transport of universal equations to the raw oracle transition machine. Those are local constructions. The actual ordinary program specialization uses Mathlib’s partial-recursive code evaluation and curry law. The PA equations are represented at 6.1. B.7 and Appendix C separately construct their ordinary and oracle total parameters. [Montagna](https://doi.org/10.1305/ndjfl/1093634998) · [Di Paola–Montagna](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2274707)
- **Pointwise versus universal equivalence.** Lengyel’s result on ordinary program indices for every consistent c.e. (T\supseteq\mathrm{PA}) concerns failure of the proposed operation of composition on the quotient by numeralwise provable equality. Its unprimed quotient uses a single proof universally quantified over inputs and outputs. The local `Lean/FailureOfComposition/ConcreteGodelRE.lean:30,42,49` gives the noncongruent indices and excludes the proposed quotient operation with the c.e. hypotheses. [Article record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25556)
- **Completeness, Löwenheim–Skolem and Łoś.** For an arbitrary consistent first-order (T\supseteq\mathrm{PA}), first-order completeness provides a model of (T), and a model falsifying a formula that (T) fails to prove. The countable Skolem hull of an infinite arithmetic model is countably infinite and its structure transports to ℕ. This establishes existence and joint faithfulness over an actual set of models on ℕ. No c.e. assumption enters this existence. In the ultrapower of the standard arithmetic structure, Łoś compares satisfaction with coordinatewise truth on an ultrafilter-large set. The paper’s external nonempty structure used for soundness can be different from the arithmetic structure in which proof codes are evaluated. Actual arguments are recorded under reconciliation questions 2, 3 and 7. [Keisler, §3](https://people.math.wisc.edu/~hkeisler/ultraproducts-web-final.pdf)
- **Non-local-connectedness of the unprimed syntactic category.** Di Paola and Montagna, Lemma 4.1, Theorem 4.3 and Corollary 4.4, pp. 656–658, treat nonzero connected domains by standard numeral singletons and separate the Σ₁-sound and nonsound cases. The corollary applies to every consistent c.e. extension of PA. Theorem 4.5 on the next page concerns the primed system. I inspected these pages of the original scan. `V46SyntacticNonLocalConnectedness.lean:143,161,215,236` proves the stated obstruction directly by distinct morphisms not distinguished by any connected domain, including all object codes after normalization. [Di Paola–Montagna](https://doi.org/10.2307/2274707)
- **Miller’s sequence.** In Miller 6.1–6.3, compactness and a closed-graph multifunction produce a fixed point of intervals. The end of the proof of 6.3 and the paragraph following it state the stronger index equality (a(e)=x) for a single (e) representing the same (x\in[0,1]) on every name of (a). This justifies the manuscript’s range restriction and order of quantifiers. Bauer–Hanson §2.1 uses binary digit rows and a computable rational representation. Their Definition 2.1 allows an (x\in\mathbb R) while the output in Theorem 2.2 is a unit interval coordinate. Taken without restriction this would include a constant program naming 2. Their singleton-interval argument in §2.2.1 explicitly treats (x\in[0,1]), as does v81. Their Theorem 6.4 about internally countable Dedekind reals is an introductory comparison. The manuscript’s weighted topology, unanimous search, index reduction, cuts and theory require separate proofs. The new fixed-point construction specializes directly to the exact evaluator and rational decoding. [Miller, pp. 12–13](https://people.math.wisc.edu/~jsmiller8/Papers/continuous.pdf#page=12) · [Bauer–Hanson, §§2.1–2.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.01256v3#page=6)
- **Precompleteness.** Bernardi’s paper p. 125 explicitly gives PA-provable equality of indices as a precomplete equivalence. Proposition 2(b), p. 126, yields recursive inseparability of two distinct classes. Theorem 2 uses a c⁺-dominical category, nonempty saturated domains and disjointness. For subsets of ℕ the conclusion on unions follows by choosing one class on each side. The two sets need not be c.e. I read the original article text at these three passages. `V46PrecompleteClasses.lean:26,59` and `V46ProvableProgramEquality.lean:112` handle the numerical theorem and its particular PA equality. [Bernardi](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1986-0848889-9) · [Journal article text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/303491669_Precomplete_equivalence_relations_in_dominical_categories)
- **Restriction and Turing structure.** Cockett–Lack I gives the restriction axioms, restriction-idempotent splitting and monic spans. Part III, Proposition 4.5, identifies a p-category structure with binary restriction products. The general definition of range functor is strengthened in this paper to preserve zero and the specified coproduct isomorphisms. Cockett–Guo–Hofstra I, Theorems 4.5 and 4.7, relates stable factorizations and split ranges. The extra agreement axiom appears in §4.11. Part II, Proposition 3.17, supplies the condition on the right class for a regular-epi/mono factorization. Appendix A assumes a regular base for its monic-span conclusion and exhibits the inverse span used in its image calculation. Cockett–Hofstra Theorem 3.4 recognizes a Turing category by a universal endoevaluator and retractions of every object. The stronger local witness constructs all typed evaluators and specializes to actual partial slices. [Restriction I](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-3975(00)00382-0) · [Restriction III](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0610500) · [Range I](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/265194097_Range_categories_I_General_theory) · [Range II](https://tac.mta.ca/tac/volumes/26/18/26-18abs.html) · [Turing categories, p. 189](https://www.di.ens.fr/users/longo/files/Cockett-TuringCateg.pdf#page=7)
- **The earlier false comparison.** The author-held `LOC18Jul06.tex`, lines 872–880, assumes a range functor into a split locally connected range category with coproducts, zero and weak choice, a weak Turing morphism and a nonzero image identity. Its proof sets the functor to identity at 894–896, obtains a section in the target and chooses its program index at 897–910. I read the TeX and compared its PDF hash. ## Comparisons and definitions used in formulating results

- **The category-theoretic recursion program.** Rosolini’s thesis and representation paper introduce p-categories of partial maps. Heller §5 uses the p-category structure with the dominance and zero conditions, and §§8–9 construct a recursion category under the stated hypotheses. His §3 frames an isotypical object by product, coproduct and free-semigroup isomorphisms. The three concrete fork isomorphisms are constructed in B.6. The corrected Lengyel existence theorem requires a faithful iteration functor into a separated category and a uniformly generated isotypical subcategory of total maps. I read the pinned correction, lines 619–625. The related-work use never introduces these assumptions into the independent diagonal comparison. Robinson–Rosolini pp. 108–109 gives the model of partial maps on a finite-limit category; v81 constructs the effective total category and its concrete partial inverses separately. [Rosolini](https://doi.org/10.1007/BFb0081368) · [Heller](https://doi.org/10.2307/2274486) · [Lengyel 2004](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0168-0072(03)00060-5) · [Pinned clarification](https://github.com/flengyel/errata/blob/5555e4fa1da7b8e3a0836dd1fbf1ca385cacd03b/lengyel2004/lengyel2004_repairs_v10.tex#L619) · [Robinson–Rosolini](https://doi.org/10.1016/0890-5401(88)90034-X)
- **External diagonal antecedents.** Di Paola–Heller, p. 594 item 3, discusses theorem/refutation codes under representability of all recursive sets. Definition 8.14 and Theorem 8.15 give the two summand domains for a Turing morphism into a coproduct under c⁺-dominical hypotheses. I read the original scan, including pp. 625–626. Their modal observations about formalized Löb and numerical quantification are historical. Cockett’s lecture notes use join and discreteness assumptions for Theorem 7.8, and a provability predicate at 7.14. Neither general theorem asserts incompleteness of arbitrary internal closed extensions. Visser Lemma 23 pulls an alleged recursive separator back along a numerical reduction, yielding essential undecidability. Kirst–Peters Theorem 20 yields an actual independent numerical sentence for formal systems strongly separating the two diagonal sets. The v81 general categorical reduction gives properness and inseparability of subobjects. Producing an outside point requires the extra concrete application. [Di Paola–Heller](https://doi.org/10.2307/2274352) · [Cockett’s notes](https://cspages.ucalgary.ca/~robin/talks/estonia-winter-2010/estonia-notes.pdf) · [Visser, Lemma 23](https://research-portal.uu.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/230468330/s00153-024-00911-y.pdf) · [Kirst–Peters, Theorem 20](https://www.ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/KirstPeters_2023_Goedel.pdf#page=9)
- **Free theories and uniform effective extension.** In Murwanashyaka–Pakhomov–Visser §4.4 the numerical equivalent is essential undecidability *or inconsistency* of a particular equivalence-relation theory. Disjoint input sets allow a model with the specified finite class sizes and infinitely many infinite classes, yielding the specialization printed here. Voutsadakis Theorem 7 gives a free-signature/underlying-hypergraph adjunction, with relation symbols interpretable by formulas. The v81 left adjoint sends a signed pair to a consistent generated theory at a fixed variable object. Van Dijk–Gietelink Oldenziel Theorems 5.19–5.20 concern an internal arithmetic universe in the initial arithmetic universe. Pour-El’s fixed calculus with an enumerated family of added axioms has an effective independent sentence operation on the consistent extensions. The definition by an index in an author’s later account is explicit. The v81 construction also realizes that operation as the standard evaluation of one total internal morphism. [MPV, §§4.1, 4.4](https://backoffice.biblio.ugent.be/download/01H3WX1TZ4VA10NE6KGRK973YN/01H3WYVBENDCNXYRYQ63CSZGWW) · [Voutsadakis](https://www.voutsadakis.com/RESEARCH/PUBLISHED/fol.pdf) · [Arithmetic universes](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.10482) · [Pour-El 1968](https://doi.org/10.2307/2270052) · [Pour-El’s index formulation](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~yytran/PourEl_recursion_axiomatizable_theories.pdf)
- **Syntax with binding and lists.** GTWW’s many-sorted terms and Goguen–Burstall’s syntax/interpretation construction give initial-algebra semantics. Cockett’s Lemma 2.2/Definition 2.3 and Maietti’s Definition 2.4 give recursion preserved by constant-family passage to parameter slices. Fiore–Plotkin–Turi, Gabbay–Pitts, and Katsumata provide syntax and substitution with binding and freshness conditions in their own settings. Keeping both a subtree and its fold value is a device in Gabbay–Pitts’s structural recursion argument; our syntax retains named bound variables. Appendix A requires a simultaneously parameter-stable two-sorted algebra, retains repeated free-variable occurrences, and constructs a **partial** substitution with a capture test. These requirements and their concrete instances must be proved from the v81 hypotheses. [GTWW, §2](https://fldit-www.cs.tu-dortmund.de/~peter/IniAlg.pdf) · [Goguen–Burstall](https://www.lfcs.inf.ed.ac.uk/reports/86/ECS-LFCS-86-10/ECS-LFCS-86-10.pdf) · [Cockett](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-4049(90)90121-W) · [Maietti](https://www.math.unipd.it/~maietti/papers/jartacrev.pdf) · [Fiore–Plotkin–Turi](https://www.dcs.ed.ac.uk/home/dt/FiorePlotkinTuri.pdf) · [Gabbay–Pitts](https://doi.org/10.1007/s001650200016) · [Katsumata](https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/~sinya/paper/generalpre.pdf)
- **Rules and finite derivations.** Aczel compares finitary closure with finite proof trees and, more generally, inductive closure with well-founded proofs. Wißmann Lemma 3.6 lifts a factorization system to endofunctor algebras when the endofunctor preserves its left class. The manuscript constructs its inference endofunctor and proves regular-epi preservation for every permitted arity before applying the initial algebra. Hájek–Pudlák 0.10 is the source for the three propositional schemes, including the contrapositive orientation. Hirst–Hirst §§2.11–2.12 supplies the two quantifier side conditions. Their variable equalities in §3.1 are adapted here to term parameters with two prescribed substitutions. Each of the seven schemes is a typed parameter object and each inference rule has the indicated ordered premises. [Aczel](https://lawrencecpaulson.github.io/papers/Aczel-Inductive-Defs.pdf) · [Wißmann](https://lmcs.episciences.org/10014/pdf) · [Hájek–Pudlák](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811071/68411/excerpt/9781107168411_excerpt.pdf) · [Hirst–Hirst](https://www.appstate.edu/~hirstjl/primer/hirst.pdf)
- **The topological example.** Curry’s Definition 2.6 states the covariant cosheaf gluing law in terms of the colimit on a cover nerve. For sets, agreement of a family on pairwise intersections forces the higher compatibilities. The empty cover gives the empty set. The arbitrary-cover equivalence with the three evaluations and the failure of whole-open evaluation to preserve products or monos use the concrete space and separate witnesses in B.1–2. Heller’s frame terminology applies to actual effective product, coproduct and word isomorphisms used later. [Curry](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1607.06942#page=3) · [Heller](https://doi.org/10.2307/2274486)

The checked Lean claims are those applied to the manuscript’s chosen presentations. Historical statements made only to locate this work in the literature retain their stated hypotheses and scope. For example, v81 does not depend on a formalization of the construction of the entire Bauer–Hanson topos, the complete theory of nominal sets, or the definition of arbitrary dominical precompleteness. For some exposition-only works the published topical comparison and available primary passage were used without a new end-to-end reading of the original article. Numerical consequences and actual categorical witnesses are recorded separately above. I did not infer a source proof from a historical attribution.
