## Primary-source comparison for the introductory categorical literature

The cited sources support the v80 claims listed below. Line numbers refer to the TeX in `manuscript/latex/sections/` at `d80a23b07406dc0e44b010a7d78f2318980b6046`. These are comparisons of statements, terminology and attribution with primary texts. They do not supply Lean imports or establish a formalization of the cited authors’ proofs.

| Manuscript passage | Comparison with the primary text |
|---|---|
| `01-introduction.tex:161–165` | Rosolini’s *Continuity and effectiveness in topoi*, ch. 2, pp. 28–29, introduces p-categories by weakening the conditions on dominical categories. Proposition 2.2.1, p. 33, says explicitly that every dominical category is a p-category and identifies its two notions of totality. Remark 2.2.2 explains the failure of the converse. The opening of the published 1988 chapter, p. 307, motivates the more general structure by partial-map categories that need not be dominical. The assigned introductory passage invokes no stronger representation claim. [Published chapter](https://doi.org/10.1007/BFb0081368) · [Author’s text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/225697089_Representation_theorems_for_P-categories) |
| `01-introduction.tex:166–171` | Lengyel 2004, §4, p. 27, defines separation by an iteration functor through faithfulness and a separated target. Theorem 26 takes iteration categories C,D and such a functor, and constructs the recursion category Tur(B) for a uniformly generated isotypical B-sharp subcategory in the specified ambient category. The pinned author’s corrections are dated 20 October 2026, version 10 (line 13). Lines 619–625 give the corrected completion theorem for the uniformly generated isotypical subcategory of total maps; the preceding simulation includes equality of partial domains. The attribution does not assert that faithfulness alone constructs a recursion category. Independently, Di Paola–Montagna 1991, p. 644, explicitly propose extending Heller’s locally connected construction to encompass their syntactic examples. [Original article](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0168-0072(03)00060-5) · [Pinned corrections](https://github.com/flengyel/errata/blob/5555e4fa1da7b8e3a0836dd1fbf1ca385cacd03b/lengyel2004/lengyel2004_repairs_v10.tex#L619-L625) · [Di Paola–Montagna](https://doi.org/10.2307/2274707) |
| `01-introduction.tex:208–210` | Cockett–Hofstra, Theorem 4.9(i)–(ii), p. 200: an internal partial combinatory algebra A yields a Turing category Comp(A). Conversely, a Turing object A of D defines a partial combinatory algebra in D, and D is equivalent to the category obtained by splitting **some class** E of idempotents in Comp(A,D). Neither a canonical class of all idempotents nor an unsplit equivalence is claimed here. [Published article, p. 200](https://www.di.ens.fr/users/longo/files/Cockett-TuringCateg.pdf#page=18) |
| `02-categorical-preliminaries.tex:6–17,80–97,99–115` | Cockett–Lack I, §2.1.1, pp. 226–227, gives R.1–R.4, and §2.1.2 defines totality by identity restriction. Section 2.3.3, Proposition 2.26, pp. 241–242, constructs the restriction on the splitting of **any selected set of idempotents**. For an arrow with underlying equation dfe=f, the new restriction is the old restriction composed with e. Taking restriction idempotents gives their Kr(C); taking every idempotent gives the Karoubi construction in the manuscript. Section 3 and Proposition 3.1, pp. 245–247, give spans with the prescribed monic left leg, their pullback composition and the restriction represented by (m,m). The manuscript sets the class of admissible monomorphisms equal to all monomorphisms. [Published article](https://cspages.ucalgary.ca/~robin/FMCS/FMCS_06/RestrictionsI.pdf) |
| `02-categorical-preliminaries.tex:126–128,144–148` | Cockett–Lack III, Proposition 4.5, states the binary restriction product characterization of p-categories. Cockett–Guo–Hofstra, p. 424, define a range functor as a restriction functor also preserving ranges. Lengyel’s 2006 report, §3.3, p. 7, adds preservation of coproducts and zero morphisms. These are exactly the two conventions distinguished in the manuscript. [Restriction III](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0610500#page=21) · [Range Categories I](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/265194097_Range_categories_I_General_theory) · [2006 report](https://academicworks.cuny.edu/gc_cs_tr/277/) |
| `02-categorical-preliminaries.tex:192–210` | Cockett–Hofstra, Definition 3.1, p. 188, makes the indexing map total for every parameter object. Their recognition theorem, Theorem 3.4, p. 189, says equivalently that every object is a retract of a single object A and that one universal application A × A → A exists. A section of a retraction is monic and hence total in a restriction category (Cockett–Lack I, Lemma 2.1(vi)). Thus the total section required in the manuscript agrees with the cited retract condition. [Definitions and theorem](https://www.di.ens.fr/users/longo/files/Cockett-TuringCateg.pdf#page=7) |
| `04-theory-extensions.tex:64–76`, related citation | Cockett–Hofstra, §3.5, p. 195, gives the actual extension argument. The source object (X,e) is a retract of (X,1); for an arrow represented by f from (X,e) × (A,1), precompose an ordinary index of f by e. The source text includes all identities in its chosen idempotents; §2, p. 188, also notes product closure for inheriting the cartesian structure. The manuscript splits the restriction idempotents, which are closed under restriction products, and separately proves terminality. The citation concerns the extension of the evaluator. [Published article, §3.5](https://www.di.ens.fr/users/longo/files/Cockett-TuringCateg.pdf#page=13) |

### Further claim-bearing introductory passages

- `01-introduction.tex:3–5,173–175,198–202`. Di Paola–Heller, pp. 594–596 and 629, give the research program and the considerations about provability algebras and arithmetic expressiveness. Their Theorem 8.15, p. 625, assumes a c-plus-dominical category and a Turing morphism into the coproduct X+X and concludes inseparability of the two diagonal summand domains. This is their own section/choice and coproduct setting. [Primary article](https://doi.org/10.2307/2274352)
- `01-introduction.tex:97–101,179–182`. Montagna, *Pathologies*, pp. 114–115, asks how to connect the numerical and syntactic categories. Remark 3.1 distinguishes existence of effectively inseparable pairs from the further question about pathological such pairs. The 2006 source for the next sentence is compared in the separate historical verification. [Montagna’s article](https://doi.org/10.1305/ndjfl/1093634998)
- `01-introduction.tex:175–178`. Bernardi, pp. 125–128, explicitly gives PA-provable equality of partial recursive programs as an example. His Theorem 2 concerns recursively inseparable unions of classes for a precomplete equivalence relation under its categorical and inhabitedness hypotheses. [Primary article](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1986-0848889-9)
- `01-introduction.tex:183–187,210–214`. Cockett’s notes attribute Theorem 7.8 to F. Lengyel. Theorem 7.14 produces a predicate on the terminal object different from zero and identity in a nontrivial Turing category with a provability predicate and joins, or minimally a restriction zero. The first theorem concerns inseparable idempotents in the notes’ recursion categories. [Author's notes](https://cspages.ucalgary.ca/~robin/talks/estonia-winter-2010/estonia-notes.pdf)
- `01-introduction.tex:189–195,202–206`. In the signed Janiczak construction, the alternative proof of Theorem 1.1 of Murwanashyaka–Pakhomov–Visser gives essential undecidability **or inconsistency** exactly when the two enumerated sets are recursively inseparable. For the disjoint inputs and their consistent signed theory used in the comparison, the inconsistency alternative drops out. Van Dĳk–Gietelink Oldenziel, Theorem 5.19, says a complete arithmetic-universe object in the initial arithmetic universe is trivial. Theorem 5.20 expresses the second incompleteness conclusion by inequality of the indicated consistency-test subobject with bottom. [Signed construction](https://doi.org/10.1093/logcom/exad005) · [Arithmetic universes, Theorems 5.19–5.20](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.10482#page=25)
