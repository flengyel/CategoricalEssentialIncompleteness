## Paper-first statement check

With composition read right to left, let $h=b\tau\Delta:U\rightharpoonup U+U$ and $a_j=h^*\varepsilon_j$. Given disjoint $P,N$ on $FU$ with $Fa_0\le P$, $Fa_1\le N$, suppose $s:FU\rightharpoonup FU+FU$ has fibers $N,P$ in that order and $F\ell=\chi s$ for a source partial morphism $\ell:U\rightharpoonup U+U$. Then some total $g:U\to U$ pulls both $P,N$ back to zero through $Fg$. **Every** such $g$ rules out coverage when $F1_U\ne0$ and $(Fg)^*$ preserves the specified join. A complementary target separator admits no such pair of morphisms under total inverse-image join preservation. A source separator would give one if complementary source pairs on $U$ admit total conditionals with $c$ sent right and $c'$ left. The final proposition constructs the conditional as a least upper bound from disjoint-arrow joins, without requiring stability under composition.

The v80 theorem binds “any such $g$” explicitly. The v80 introduction also qualifies the constant-family equivalence by the associated theorem's hypotheses. These resolve the two scope questions that arose on my initial paper reading. The two summand orientations, both orders of composition, the domains of all four partial/total arrows, and the distinction between disjointness and noncoverage agree with the sources below.

### Consequences for the verification claim

The source types and proof steps for Theorem 3.1, both corollaries and Proposition 3.4 match the independent reconstruction. An exact-type identification for the most general stand-alone reading of the summand-join observation has not been shown. Historical/theorem comparisons listed in §1 and the Cockett–Hofstra characterization in §2 cannot be counted as checked applications on the strength of their citations. Most significantly, a theorem conditional on `ha : IsMiller a` establishes what happens **for any given** such sequence. It does not construct the sequence used for the existence of a concrete realization. No clean whole-paper count follows from this inventory.
