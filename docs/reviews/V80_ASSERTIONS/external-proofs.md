# Two cited existence and nonexistence results

These are manuscript-side derivations for the open comparisons in §9 and Appendix D. All section references and Lean paths below refer to v80 at `d80a23b07406dc0e44b010a7d78f2318980b6046`. Neither derivation has a WSL2 receipt. They specify intermediate mathematical claims whose proofs can be checked independently.

## §9, existence for the specified enumerations

Write $Q=[0,1]^{\mathbb N}$, $P=2^{\mathbb N}$, and let $\rho:P\to Q$ be the manuscript's continuous surjection. Fix the paper's oracle enumeration and rational enumeration. The required conclusion is that some $a\in Q$ satisfies the following condition for every program index $d$ and real $r\in[0,1]$. If, for each name $p\in\rho^{-1}(a)$ and each $k$, the computation at $(d,p,k)$ returns an index $j$ with $|q_j-r|<2^{-k}$, then $a(d)=r$. The index in the conclusion is the original $d$.

1. For fixed $d$, enumerate the finite halting certificates $(k,j,\sigma)$ for which all names extending the finite binary word $\sigma$ give output $j$ at precision $k$. Use the finite-computation certificates of the evaluator, so each actual halt is witnessed. For a name $p$, start with the closed interval $[0,1]$. At each certificate whose word is a prefix of $p$, intersect the current interval with $[q_j-2^{-k},q_j+2^{-k}]$ if the intersection is nonempty. Otherwise keep the current interval. At each finite stage the interval depends on finitely many bits of $p$. An enumeration by stages with repetitions or empty stages works equally well.
2. The intersection $I_d(p)$ of the decreasing intervals is nonempty and compact. Its graph in $P\times[0,1]$ is closed, since each finite-stage graph is closed. If $d,p$ computes $r$ with the prescribed strict bounds, every applicable interval contains $r$, so every such update is accepted. A certificate occurs at each precision. Any two points in the limiting interval differ by at most $2^{1-k}$ for every $k$, hence $I_d(p)=\{r\}$.
3. Put $H_d(a)=\bigcup_{\rho(p)=a} I_d(p)$. Its graph is the continuous image of the compact graph of $I_d$ under $(p,y)\mapsto(\rho(p),y)$. Each fiber is nonempty by surjectivity of $\rho$. Its interval hull $J_d(a)=[\min H_d(a),\max H_d(a)]$ also has compact graph. Indeed membership means there exist $u,v\in H_d(a)$ with $u\le y\le v$, a projection of a closed subset of a compact product. If every name of $a$ computes the same $r$ via $d$, then $J_d(a)=\{r\}$.
4. Define $F(a)=\prod_d J_d(a)$. This correspondence has nonempty convex values. Its graph is closed because it is the intersection of the inverse images of the closed coordinate graphs under $(a,b)\mapsto(a,b(d))$. The Hilbert cube fixed-point theorem for closed correspondences with nonempty convex values gives $a\in F(a)$. The singleton-fiber conclusion in step 3 gives $a(d)=r$ with the required quantifier order.
5. The fixed-point input in step 4 can alternatively be reduced to finite-dimensional Kakutani. Extend a point of the first $m$ coordinates by a zero tail, apply $F$, and project to those $m$ coordinates. This finite-dimensional correspondence has nonempty compact convex values and compact graph, by projection. Its fixed point gives $(a_m,b_m)$ on the original graph agreeing in the first $m$ coordinates. A convergent subsequence in the compact metrizable product has a diagonal limit, which belongs to the closed graph.

The finite-certificate characterization for the numbered evaluator is in [V46MillerOracleProgramEffective](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46MillerOracleProgramEffective.lean#L280). The target is [IsMiller](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46MillerRealComputation.lean#L30). To close the existence obligation, construct the graph for that evaluator and supply a checked fixed-point theorem at the type in step 4 or 5. The paper has a valid external input. Miller's Lemma 6.2 and the paragraph following Theorem 6.3 explicitly identify the coordinate with the program index. Bauer and Hanson give the binary-name version. Their §2.2 gives the closed-graph fixed-point formulation. [Miller, §6](https://people.math.wisc.edu/~jsmiller8/Papers/continuous.pdf) · [Bauer–Hanson, §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.01256v3)

## Appendix D, failure of local connectedness

Let $T\supseteq\mathrm{PA}$ be consistent and computably enumerable. Work on $\omega$ in the unprimed category $S_T$. For a restriction idempotent $c=[C]_T$, order is $T$-provable universal implication between domain formulas. For each standard $n$, let $s_n$ be the partial identity on $x=\bar n$. When $T$ is unsound it is particularly important that $c\ne0$ here means $T\nvdash\forall x\,\neg C(x)$.

**Claim.** Every nonzero connected $c$ lies below some $s_n$.

For any primitive recursive test $D(x)$ given by a PA-provably total bit-valued program, both outcomes define restriction idempotents. Intersecting each with $c$ gives disjoint domains whose join is $c$. Connectedness implies that $T$ proves one of $\forall x(C(x)\to D(x))$ and $\forall x(C(x)\to\neg D(x))$. Since $c\ne0$, it cannot prove both. Suppose $c$ lies below no $s_n$. Apply this dichotomy to each numeral equality. For each fixed standard $t$, PA's description of the finite initial segment then gives $T\vdash\forall x(C(x)\to x>\bar t)$. No uniform proof in $t$ is needed.

Let $A,B$ be the recursively inseparable sets of indices whose diagonal computation halts with even, respectively odd, output, as in §8. For each standard $n$, let $D_n(x)$ test whether that computation has first halted with even output by stage $x$. This is a uniform primitive recursive test with PA-provably complementary outcomes. Enumerate $T$-proofs until one of the two universal implications for $D_n$ occurs. The preceding dichotomy makes this a total recursive decision procedure. If $n\in A$, its finite halting trace with stage $t$ gives a PA proof that $D_n(x)$ holds for every $x\ge\bar t$. The finite initial-segment conclusion forces the positive decision. If $n\in B$, its finite odd trace and determinism of the evaluator force the negative decision. The resulting procedure separates $A$ and $B$, which proves the claim. This argument does not assert that $s_n\le c$, or that a nonzero $c$ has a standard point.

Now choose indices $f,i,g$ with $f,i$ pointwise provably equivalent at every standard numeral but the composites with $g$ failing that equivalence. The [concrete noncongruence theorem](../../../Lean/FailureOfComposition/ConcreteGodelRE.lean#L30) supplies these indices with the stated hypotheses on $T$. Uniform graph equivalence implies pointwise equivalence and respects composition. Hence $[f]_T\ne[i]_T$ in $S_T(\omega,\omega)$. Pointwise equivalence at $n$ gives $[f]_T s_n=[i]_T s_n$: compose each graph with the partial identity on $x=\bar n$ and use the provable equality of the two output predicates there. If $0\ne c$ is connected, choose $n$ with $c\le s_n$; composing on the right with $c$ gives $[f]_T c=[i]_T c$. The same equation holds for zero. This disproves joint epimorphy on $\omega$, hence local connectedness.

The formal obligations are the connected-domain claim, the passage from numeralwise graph equivalence to equality after each $s_n$, and their application to the actual unprimed category.
