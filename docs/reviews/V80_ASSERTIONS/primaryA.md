## Appendix A primary-source supplement

All six requested references support their stated roles in v80. The source of the additional range agreement law and the distinction between variable and term equality schemes are material to the comparison. Line numbers below refer to `manuscript/latex/appendices/a-syntax-and-theories.tex` at `d80a23b07406dc0e44b010a7d78f2318980b6046`.

| 13–16 | J. R. B. Cockett, *List-arithmetic distributive categories: Locoi*, §2, pp. 5–7, Lemma 2.2 and Definition 2.3 on p. 7. | For a category with products, the recursive object rec(A,B) receives a map from B and an A-action; maps out of it are determined by a seed and an A-action on the target. For each Y, the constant-family functor sends X to X×Y→Y. Lemma 2.2 identifies the recursive object on the lifted inputs, and Definition 2.3 calls recursion parameterized when the functor preserves this recursion. Lists take B=1. This supports the historical attribution and the constant-family interpretation of parameters. It does not establish existence of these algebras in every regular category. [Journal article](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-4049(90)90121-W) |
| 122–127 | J. A. Goguen, J. W. Thatcher, E. G. Wagner and J. B. Wright, *Initial Algebra Semantics and Continuous Algebras*, §2, especially Proposition 2.1, p. 71. | For a set of sorts and a many-sorted signature with specified input and output sorts, the category of set-valued algebras and operation-preserving homomorphisms has an initial algebra. The construction uses well-formed expressions or trees of each sort. This is the cited abstract syntax method. Internal coefficients in a general category, quantifier binding and stability after change of parameter are manuscript hypotheses or constructions. [Original journal article](https://doi.org/10.1145/321992.321997) · [Full text, p. 71](https://fldit-www.cs.tu-dortmund.de/~peter/IniAlg.pdf#page=4) |
| 129–135,239–242 | M. J. Gabbay and A. M. Pitts, *A New Approach to Abstract Syntax with Variable Binding*, §6, Theorem 6.5 and Corollary 6.7 with its proof, p. 17 of the authors’ 1–23 pagination. | Their structural recursion concerns untyped λ-terms modulo α-equivalence in FM-sets; the abstraction operation satisfies the freshness requirement (68). In the proof take the product of the expression object with the result object, iterate into this product, identify the first projection with the identity by uniqueness and take the second projection as the desired result. The reference supports the precise device of retaining the original subtree. The manuscript’s named-variable admissibility test and its failure summand are its own construction. [Authors’ text](https://www.gabbay.org.uk/papers/newaas-jv.pdf#page=17) |
| 455–469 | P. Hájek and P. Pudlák, *Metamathematics of First-Order Arithmetic*, 0.10, p. 7; Cambridge reissue of the 1993 text. | The first three schemata are exactly the printed propositional schemes, including the orientation of the third, contrapositive scheme. Section 0.10 also gives universal instantiation and a conditional generalization rule; §0.11 treats equality as special axioms. V80 attributes just its three propositional schemes to this passage. [Publisher's excerpt, p. 7](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811071/68411/excerpt/9781107168411_excerpt.pdf) |
| 470–486 | H. P. Hirst and J. L. Hirst, *A Primer for Logic and Proof*, §3.1, pp. 64–65, Axioms 6–7, followed by examples. | Axiom 6 is universally quantified reflexivity of equality on variables. Axiom 7 gives replacement of a freely substitutable variable in a formula, under an equality antecedent. The next page gives one-step examples and excludes substitution for a bound occurrence. The manuscript explicitly uses terms as parameters: its reflexivity is t=t and both substitutions in its equality scheme use the admissibility domain. Calling these adaptations of the cited principles is accurate. The general term schemata are not verbatim Axioms 6 and 7. [Authors’ text](https://www.appstate.edu/~hirstjl/primer/hirst.pdf) |
| 564–573 | J. R. B. Cockett, X. Guo and P. Hofstra, *Range Categories I: General Theory*, Theorem 4.2 p. 434, Theorem 4.5 p. 435, Theorem 4.7 p. 437, §4.11 p. 439 and the remark after Theorem 4.14 p. 442. | Theorem 4.5 constructs split ranges in Par(C,M) from an M-stable (E,M) factorization system with the indicated system of monics. Its range is the partial identity on the M-image of the value leg. Theorem 4.7 goes the other direction: in a split range category, total maps have an M-stable factorization into full-range total maps and total partial isomorphisms. This alone makes no regular-epimorphism claim. Section 4.11 introduces the **additional** agreement axiom [RR.5]; the remark on p. 442 explains that in a regular category each map is epic onto its range. For regular D the regular-epi/mono factorization is pullback-stable and meets the hypotheses, giving the manuscript’s application. The total maps are the image of D, explicitly Theorem 4.2. The cited §4.11 is a section heading, not a theorem asserting agreement for every range category. [Journal PDF, reliable EMIS mirror](https://www.maths.tcd.ie/EMIS/journals/TAC/volumes/26/17/26-17.pdf) |
