**Di Paola–Heller and the role of the comparison.** In *Dominical Categories: Recursion Theory Without Elements* (1987), pp. 594–596, the authors describe a program of algebraizing generalized incompleteness; item (3) on p. 594 lists recursive inseparability of proofs and refutations when every recursive set is definable in a consistent arithmetical theory. On p. 629 they again describe the arithmetic application as future work. Definition 8.14 and Theorem 8.15, pp. 625–626, concern categorical domain separation. The theorem assumes a c⁺-dominical category and a Turing morphism X×X→X+X. Its two domains are obtained by composing the diagonal with that morphism and the two complementary summand idempotents; they are recursively inseparable. This is an antecedent of the shape of v80 Corollary 7.3, not a proof that an arbitrary unprimed S_T satisfies the c⁺-dominical hypothesis, or a proof of v80’s deduction-theoretic application. Montagna’s Remark 3.1, p. 114, attributes existence of effectively inseparable pairs in the syntactic category to an extension of Di Paola–Heller Theorem 8.18 via his Proposition 1.1; it explicitly leaves open the existence of *pathological* such pairs. His concluding remark, pp. 114–115, describes the desired link between the syntactic and classical categories. [Publisher](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022481200029649)

No primary citation in these four files asserts that an externally specified primitive-recursive program coincides on nonstandard inputs with the formalization’s chosen representative of the same standard function. The selector-identity discrepancy recorded in the statement audit cannot be resolved by these citations.
