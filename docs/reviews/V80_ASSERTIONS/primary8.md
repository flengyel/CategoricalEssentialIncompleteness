For two c.e. sets X,Y, their §4.4, p. 1169, second variant of the proof of Theorem 1.1, adds the positive X-instances and negative Y-instances to Jan. Their displayed equivalence says that failure of recursive separation is equivalent to the resulting theory being essentially undecidable **or inconsistent**. When X,Y are disjoint, build a model with one finite class of size i+1 for each i in X, none for the remaining sizes, and countably many infinite classes. This meets Jan’s two axiom schemes and all signed instances. The manuscript correctly gives the resulting equivalence on disjoint inputs. This is an antecedent in ordinary computability, separate from the categorical theorem’s proof.
