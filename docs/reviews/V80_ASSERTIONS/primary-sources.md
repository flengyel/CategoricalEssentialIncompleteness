| §4, numerical reduction to the provable and refutable sentences | Visser's Lemma 23 assumes a recursive map from the natural numbers to sentences of a theory and an inseparable pair mapped respectively to the provable and refutable sentences. The conclusion is essential undecidability. A decision procedure for an extension would, by pullback, give a recursive separator. [Visser, Lemma 23](https://research-portal.uu.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/230468330/s00153-024-00911-y.pdf) | Historical comparison. The paper's Corollary 4.3 proves inseparability internally for a pair of domains. |
| §4, diagonal reduction in synthetic computability | Kirst and Peters, Theorem 20, use a map strongly separating the two diagonal sets in a formal system. An extension retains the positive and negative implications. Partial Boolean decision on the image fails at some input, and the corresponding sentence is independent. Their formal systems have semidecidable provability and a consistency condition. [Kirst–Peters, Definitions 7, 17 and Theorem 20](https://www.ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/KirstPeters_2023_Goedel.pdf) | Numerical antecedent. Corollary 4.3 does not assume a global point outside the theorem and refutation domains. |
| §4, adjunction for logical languages | In Voutsadakis's Theorem 7 the left and right adjoints have types $F:\mathrm{HSet}\to\mathrm{Sign}$ and $U:\mathrm{Sign}\to\mathrm{HSet}$. A signature morphism assigns formulas to relation symbols. The unit and counit satisfy the triangle identities. [Voutsadakis, p. 484](https://www.voutsadakis.com/RESEARCH/PUBLISHED/fol.pdf) | The signed theory adjunction instead extends an assignment of sentences to nullary predicates under the two specified subobject inequalities. |
| Appendix A, parameterized lists | Maietti's Definition 2.4 requires unique recursion for each initial-value morphism $B\to Y$ and step $Y\times A\to Y$. The paragraph following identifies the equivalent formulation by preservation of recursive objects under pullback from the terminal object to a slice. [Maietti, p. 42](https://www.math.unipd.it/~maietti/papers/jartacrev.pdf) | A hypothesis on the specified list object and its recursion under parameters. |
| Appendix A, syntax as an initial algebra | Goguen and Burstall, §4, use a many-sorted syntactic signature; its initial algebra is the syntax and its unique homomorphism to a meaning algebra is interpretation. Fiore, Plotkin and Turi equip the model of a binding signature with compatible algebra and substitution operations. Katsumata, §2, Example 1(3), gives a typed first-order signature with an existential binder and identifies terms up to renaming of bound variables. [Goguen–Burstall, §4](https://www.lfcs.inf.ed.ac.uk/reports/86/ECS-LFCS-86-10/ECS-LFCS-86-10.pdf) · [Fiore–Plotkin–Turi](https://www.dcs.ed.ac.uk/home/dt/FiorePlotkinTuri.pdf) · [Katsumata, Example 1(3)](https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/~sinya/paper/generalpre.pdf) | Appendix A retains variable names and constructs substitution on its domain of admissible triples. |
| Appendix A, closure under finitary rules | For a finitary rule set, Aczel's Proposition 1.1.4 identifies the least closed set with the elements having finite proofs. Proposition 1.4.5(i) identifies an inductively defined set with the elements having well-founded proof trees. The latter theorem also treats rules with infinitely many premises. [Aczel, §§1.1, 1.4](https://lawrencecpaulson.github.io/papers/Aczel-Inductive-Defs.pdf) | The manuscript gives an internal proof that the regular image of the conclusion morphism of the initial derivation algebra is the least closed subobject. |
| Appendix A, image of an algebra morphism | Wißmann's Lemma 3.6 lifts the factorization system to algebras if the endofunctor preserves the left class. In the manuscript this class is the regular epimorphisms in the slice. Stability under pullback, products and the stated finite coproduct construction supply preservation for the rule functor. [Wißmann, Lemma 3.6](https://lmcs.episciences.org/10014/pdf) | The local proof in the slice establishes leastness for the given rule and conclusion maps. |
| Appendix A, quantifier schemes | Hirst and Hirst, §2.12, Axioms 4 and 5, require respectively that the substituted term be free for the variable and that the generalized variable have no free occurrence in the antecedent. Their rules are modus ponens and generalization. [Hirst–Hirst, pp. 51–52](https://www.appstate.edu/~hirstjl/primer/hirst.pdf) | These are the two side conditions imposed by the paper's named syntax. |
| Appendix A, regular images and ranges | Cockett, Guo and Hofstra II, Proposition 3.17, assume finite limits and a stable factorization system. Its factorizations are regular epi–mono exactly when its right class consists of all monomorphisms. [CGH II, p. 480](https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/EMIS/journals/TAC/volumes/26/18/26-18.pdf) | The equivalence does not confer regularity on an arbitrary range category. Appendix A takes the base to be regular in the partial-map construction. |
