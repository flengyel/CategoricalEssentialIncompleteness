# Di Paola–Montagna comparisons

I compared the manuscript with the journal facsimile on printed pp. 644–646 and 657–658 (PDF pp. 3–5 and 16–17). [Journal article](https://doi.org/10.2307/2274707) · [Facsimile identity](../V81_WSL2_AUDITS/tested-0e1a501/first/primary-sources-receipt.json)

* **Primitive-recursive notation, p. 644.** In the paragraph preceding §1, Di Paola and Montagna associate a formula of PA with each formula in the primitive-recursive extension, preserving the free variables. This supports the convention in §6 of expanding the primitive-recursive symbols in the graph formula. [Manuscript §6](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/05-syntactic-recursion-category.tex#L58)
* **Zero law, p. 645.** Condition (∗) gives $f\times0=0=0\times f$. In the manuscript the displayed right-zero law gives the left-zero law by the symmetry of the restriction product. Di Paola and Montagna’s Definition 1.6 imposes further conditions for a category to be dominical. In particular, condition (i) requires a zero product to have a zero factor. Their statement on p. 646 that the syntactic categories fail to be dominical is compatible with the manuscript’s prodominical axiom. [Manuscript definition](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/02-categorical-preliminaries.tex#L187)
* **Syntactic category, p. 646.** The set-valued presentation starts from $\omega$ and takes Cartesian products and tagged disjoint unions. In the unprimed category, a single proof in $T$ asserts equivalent definedness on every input and agreement of values whenever defined. For the functional graphs in §6, this amounts to provable equality of the graphs on all inputs and outputs. The primed relation on the same page quantifies over standard inputs outside provability. [Uniform graph equivalence](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/05-syntactic-recursion-category.tex#L129) · [Set presentation](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/05-syntactic-recursion-category.tex#L153)
* **Corollary 4.4, pp. 657–658.** For every consistent c.e. extension of PA, the unprimed $S_T$ fails to be locally connected. If $T$ fails $\Sigma_1$-soundness, only the zero domain is connected. In the sound case the authors fix a partial identity $[e]_T\ne1_T$. For **every** connected domain $[c]_T$, they prove $[e]_T\circ[c]_T=[c]_T=1_T\circ[c]_T$. The supposition $[e]_T=1_T$ would give a proof in $T$ of the consistency sentence for its numeration. The corollary is stated on p. 657 and the last equality is established at the top of p. 658.
