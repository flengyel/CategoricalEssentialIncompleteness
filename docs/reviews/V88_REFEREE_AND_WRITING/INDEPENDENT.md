| Setup and conditionals | An effective fork has shape $1\leftarrow0\to2$. A complementary pair on the universal object gives disjoint c.e. component subsets covering each carrier. Parallel searches compute the inverse of the copairing componentwise. Its inverse commutes with the fork maps because the original copairing does. | Proposition 10.1 and Appendix B | At an endpoint the partial function evaluates program $p$ on the code of a two-tuple and decodes the result as a two-tuple. Halting is exactly its domain. To represent an arbitrary partial morphism on $Z\times\mathbb N^2$, restrict the computation to its c.e. domain and specialize at $z$. The parameter has the form $(0,s(z))$. At the centre, projecting $(k,z,x_1,x_2)$ to the left forgets $z$. The left projection of the output of the map $(k,z,x_1,x_2)\mapsto(0,0,z,0)$ recovers $z$. Naturality therefore precludes any fixed program at the centre for this total map. |
| Propositions 10.2–10.3 and Theorem 10.4 | Evaluating finite trees yields componentwise folds. Naturality of the target algebra makes them commute with relabelling. For derivations the two ordered premises carry the designated antecedent and implication, so their target values satisfy the pullback condition for the rule. The image of the conclusion is the least closed subfunctor. Given two putatively contradictory proofs at a vertex, make precisely their positive leaf labels true. Every negative leaf is then true because its label is in the disjoint negative support. Induction contradicts the two conclusions. [Lean proof presentation](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46ForkDerivationImages.lean#L208) |
| Theorem 10.5 and Corollary 10.6 | At each endpoint the provable and refutable atoms form disjoint c.e. sets containing the two original inseparable sets. If they covered the carrier, parallel enumeration would decide one and separate the original pair. An independent consistent c.e.-axiomatized extension at endpoint $i$ has c.e. deductive closure. Enlarge the base only at $i$. Compatibility holds since $i$ is a sink and the image of the central base already lies in the original theory at $i$. Thus an atom remains undecided for each independently chosen endpoint extension. [Endpoint axioms](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46ForkUndecidedAtoms.lean#L66) |
| Proposition 10.7 | Equality of the constant variable names is decidable. Formula relabelling only changes nullary predicate labels and commutes with admissible substitution. A finite proof code includes its rule parameters. Checking the finitely many required coefficient and axiom memberships yields a c.e. carrier even when the coefficients are merely c.e. Holding the slice parameter fixed at every node gives initiality for each axiom subobject over that parameter. |
| Proposition 10.8 | Erasure sends equality to a fixed tautology and deletes quantifiers. With variables as the only terms and nullary nonlogical predicates, admissible substitution does not change the erasure. The four nonpropositional schemes erase to instances of $H\Rightarrow H$, its instance at $H\Rightarrow K$, the fixed tautology, and $\top\Rightarrow(H\Rightarrow H)$. Finite Hilbert derivations for these and closure under both rules give $T_{\mathrm f}\leq k^*T_{\mathrm p}$. Since $kj=1$ and the inclusion preserves propositional derivations, $j^*T_{\mathrm f}=T_{\mathrm p}$. Commutation with negation gives consistency and transfers endpoint completeness along $j_i$ to a contradiction. [Conservative restriction](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46ForkConservativeRestriction.lean#L168) |

The general extension theorem gives the fully faithful comparison, its properness, and essential incompleteness in the slice.
