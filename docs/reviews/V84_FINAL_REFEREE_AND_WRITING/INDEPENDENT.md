# Independent author-side reading of manuscript v84

The central constructions survived the tests below. I found two local matters of mathematical wording and two Lean documentation edits. None supplies a counterexample to a numbered conclusion. I read the 103-page manuscript and its active TeX inputs before consulting the formalization, correspondence or earlier reports. Page numbers refer to the published PDF. TeX filenames are relative to `manuscript/latex`; Lean filenames are relative to `Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness`.

## (A) Kill-shots / hard blockers

None established. The potentially damaging counterexamples concern a converse adjoint on total maps, soundness for nonstandard proofs in an arbitrary model of PA, and an externally bounded selector. The first two claims are absent from their theorem statements. The selector calls for a distinction in Lemma 6.4.

## (B) Major weaknesses

No major mathematical or formalization defect found in the results compared. In particular the formal signed classification supplies the Boolean extension and the consistency of the source pair is a hypothesis. The arithmetic uniformity theorem constructs its morphism before quantifying over the indices of consistent fibers.

## (C) Minor issues

1. **The finite selector in Lemma 6.4, pp. 31–32.** `sections/05a-internal-syntax.tex:291–321` describes a primitive-recursive function into a finite set and writes the selected condition as \(D_{\ell(a_i)}(a_i)\). The proof fixes a numerical program with graph \(L\). In a nonstandard model of PA, the notation has two readings. PA-provable totality and functionality of that graph do not by themselves prove that every output is an indicated numeral. For \(s=1\), return 1 precisely if a PA proof of \(0=1\) has code at most \(x\), and return 0 otherwise. Assuming PA is consistent, this primitive-recursive program always returns 0 on standard inputs. By Gödel's second theorem and completeness, a model of \(\mathrm{PA}+\neg\operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{PA})\) sees a value 1. Take \(D_0=\top\) and the singleton sequence containing such an input. The proof's guarded formula is false for that sequence. An interpretation using the everywhere-zero representative of the same external function gives true. The discrepancy tests the stronger reading only. The proof explicitly uses the guarded formula, and `V46SelectorGraph.lean:23–46` checks exactly its PA and model instances. In the statement replace the last clause by “Fix a \(\Sigma_1\) graph \(L(x,w)\) of the chosen selector program. The same conclusion holds for the entry condition \(\bigvee_{j<s}(L(x,\bar j)\land D_j(x))\).” The bound can instead be made a PA-provable specification if a total selector into the internal finite set is intended. Failure mode *ambiguous*, evidence *counterexample* to the stronger reading, severity *minor*, repair local to the statement. [Gödel’s second theorem](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/goedel-incompleteness/)

2. **The functor called \(B\) in Appendix E.5, p. 100.** `appendices/e-reading-lean-proofs.tex:138–147` gives \(E:\mathcal D/U\to\mathcal D\), then writes \(B=B_U\) and \(EB\cong1_{\mathcal D}\). The previously defined \(B_U:\mathscr C\to\mathscr C[U]\) acts on partial maps. Composition with this \(E\) requires its restriction to total maps. The main proof explicitly gives that restriction in `sections/04-theory-extensions.tex:287–295`. The formal instance is `constantFamilyFunctor U` in `V46AssociatedClosedTheories.lean:24–41`. Replace “Write \(B=B_U\)” by “Let \(B:\mathcal D\to\mathcal D/U\) be the restriction of \(B_U\) to total morphisms.” Failure mode *ambiguous* functor notation, evidence *derivation* from the displayed source and target, severity *minor*, repair one clause. Theorem 5.3 and its Lean application have the required types.

3. **Lean module introductions.** `V46AssociatedIncompletenessChecks.lean:10` introduces the slice theorem under “manuscript v58”. A reader trying to find v84 Theorem 5.3 must consult release history. Replace the heading by “Essential incompleteness in the slice over a consistent theory (Theorem 5.3).” `V46MillerCutReduction.lean:13–24` gives two top-level headings and describes premises that used to require proof. Use one heading, “Recursive reduction to the lower and upper cuts”, and the sentence “The same total recursive function pulls back the lower and upper cuts of every Miller sequence to its even- and odd-output diagonal domains.” The quantified type appears at lines 77–80. Failure mode *inconsistent_use* of version and heading conventions, evidence *citation*, severity *minor* for navigation. Both changes are documentation-only and preserve public names. [Knuth, Larrabee and Roberts, §1](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/papers/cs1193.pdf)
