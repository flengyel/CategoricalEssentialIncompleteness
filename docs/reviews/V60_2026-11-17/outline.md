# Outline of the reviewed manuscript

*Logical consequence and essential incompleteness in recursion categories*, v59. Page numbers below are those of the archived 87-page PDF. The order follows the compiled text, including the four additional files included by §8.

The paper asks when a computational pair of disjoint domains gives an essentially incomplete internal theory, and how its incompleteness behaves under interpretation and extension. Three dependencies organize the argument. Appendix A constructs deduction from initial algebras and regular images. Section 3 supplies the diagonal comparison used in §§4 and 8. Sections 4 and 5 prove the results on free theories and associated partial-map categories that the three realizations instantiate.

| Section and first page | Subsections, in printed order, and their mathematical functions |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Introduction, p. 1 | The opening sets out the two incompleteness arguments and the three realizations. §1.1, Related work (p. 3), compares recursion-category existence theorems, categorical inseparability, syntactic recursion categories, Turing categories, and Miller-sequence realizability. |
| 2. Categorical preliminaries, p. 4 | §2.1 defines restriction idempotents, inseparability, ranges, splittings and partial maps. §2.2 fixes restriction products and coproducts, range functors, local connectedness, partial monomorphisms and weak choice. §2.3 states the recursion-category and Turing-category axioms. |
| 3. Diagonal comparison and complementary conditionals, p. 6 | §3.1 uses a lift of a partial morphism whose two summand inverse images are the target domains in reverse order to construct a total source morphism whose interpretation avoids both target domains (Theorem 3.1). §3.2 turns the result into source inseparability when complementary domains admit conditionals. Appendix C tests the lifting hypothesis. |
| 4. Internal theories and essential incompleteness, p. 8 | §4.1 constructs sentence-theorem ranges, refutation by pullback along negation, and their interpretation. §4.2 derives source and target incompleteness under consistency of the interpretation and the indicated lift (Theorem 4.2). §4.3 transfers inseparability along a reduction to every disjoint extension of the theorem and refutation domains. §4.4 proves the generation reflection and the Galois connections induced by logical translations. §4.5 compares deduction under a change of ambient category. §4.6 constructs the free first-order theory of a disjoint pair, proves its adjunction, and characterizes essential incompleteness by inseparability when the generated theory is consistent (Theorem 4.7, Corollary 4.8). |
| 5. Theory extensions and recursion categories, p. 14 | §5.1 constructs the associated partial-map category over a slice, with Turing morphism inherited from the original one. §5.2 proves full faithfulness under a theory inclusion and the two underlying adjunctions. §5.3 uses evaluation at a fixed logical theorem to transport essential incompleteness in both directions and characterizes the existence of a total extension of the partial decision morphism (Theorem 5.3). |
| 6. The syntactic recursion category, p. 17 | The opening defines equality of program graphs by uniform provability and gives evaluation and specialization. §6.1 identifies the total restriction splitting with a category of arithmetically presented predicates. §6.2 constructs syntax and lists, including parameterized initiality. §6.3 represents free-variable and substitution operations. §6.4 constructs the initial finite-derivation algebras and their generated theories. |
| 7. Interpretation and soundness, p. 30 | The opening interprets program graphs in each arithmetic model. §7.1 lifts the conditionals needed in §3. §7.2 establishes regularity of the total interpretation. §7.3 factors standard sentence-proof conclusions through validity in a model of the axioms. §7.4 carries soundness to arithmetic ultrapowers by comparison with an ultrapower of the interpretation functor. |
| 8. Essential incompleteness of arithmetic, p. 35 | §8.1 verifies diagonal representation and proof-search lifting in the fixed syntactic category for PA, then obtains internal and numerical incompleteness. §8.2 constructs the indexed axiom and derivation families. §8.3 gives a total morphism selecting an independent sentence uniformly for each consistent indexed extension. §8.4 identifies the numerical restriction splitting and realizes syntax and derivations on computably enumerable carriers. §8.5 applies the reduction criterion to the numerical theorem and refutation domains. §8.6 traces numeral instances under theory extension. §8.7 verifies consistency of the separate theory freely generated by the arithmetic diagonal pair, and applies the associated-category theorems to its extensions. |
| 9. Miller sequences and essential incompleteness, p. 51 | §9.1 constructs the recursion category of functions computed uniformly across binary representations, including its Turing morphism. §9.2 proves inseparability for the indices on the two sides of one half. §9.3 realizes syntax and deduction and transfers inseparability to the theory of real closed fields expanded by constants naming the sequence. §9.4 applies the associated-category construction to extensions of that theory. |
| 10. A recursion category from a three-point space, p. 58 | The opening introduces cosheaves and the computable fork diagram category. §10.1 obtains inseparable restriction idempotents. §10.2 checks component evaluation and the endpoint Turing morphisms. §10.3 constructs propositional syntax and derivations, the internal theory, and its two essentially incomplete endpoint theories. §10.4 gives the first-order realization and its conservativity and incompleteness claims. §10.5 applies the associated-category construction. |
| A. Syntax and deductively closed theories, p. 67 | §A.1 fixes categorical hypotheses, Boolean morphisms, decidable variables, and parameter stability. §A.2 constructs named-variable terms, formulas, free-variable lists, and the sentence object. §A.3 constructs admissible substitution by a fold and pullback. §A.4 proves that the image of the initial rule algebra is the least rule-closed subobject. §A.5 specializes to the first-order schemes and rules and identifies the theorem subobject and its range. §A.6 specializes to propositional theories. |
| B. The three-point cosheaf construction, p. 75 | §B.1 identifies the cosheaf category with the fork functor category and describes the computable total category. §B.2 proves the partial-map, range, and connectedness properties. §B.3 produces a universal object and a frame. §B.4 constructs its Turing morphism. §B.5 identifies the recursion subcategory and its idempotent splittings and distinguishes it from the recursion category of partial recursive functions. |
| C. Necessity of section lifting for inseparability, p. 81 | A category of restrictions of partial recursive functions to domains computably enumerable relative to the halting set has complementary domains whose conditional admits no lift. The faithful forgetful functor verifies the other comparison conditions. |
| D. Faithful model interpretations and theory extension, p. 84 | A family of countable model interpretations separates provably inequivalent program graphs. §D.1 transports diagonal pairs to stronger consistent theories. §D.2 relates provability of numeral instances to the faithful family. §D.3 identifies the syntactic category of true arithmetic with the category of partial recursive functions and shows the insufficiency of bare source inseparability for external incompleteness. |
