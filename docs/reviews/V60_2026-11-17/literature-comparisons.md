# Literature and contribution of manuscript v60

Referee report of 17 November 2026 on *Logical consequence and essential incompleteness in recursion categories*, v60 at [dda8eed](https://github.com/flengyel/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/tree/dda8eed0a8ae44ecedb0652a98c4ae9d9111037f). Page references below are to the [printed manuscript](../../../manuscript/logical_consequence_and_essential_incompleteness_v60.pdf).

## (A) Kill-shots / hard blockers

**Revise the literature comparisons before submission.** The closest numerical results already give both the reduction from inseparability to incompleteness and, for a particular theory built from a pair, the converse. A signature adjunction already describes substitution of formulas for predicate symbols. Neither observation refutes a numbered result. Both rule out using the direction from computation to theories, or the substitution bijection alone, as the paper’s claim to originality.

The case for the paper rests on what the categorical constructions prove. Under stated hypotheses on syntax and finite deduction, a disjoint pair presents a theory internal to the chosen category. Its universal property is a natural bijection between translations out of the generated theory and total morphisms respecting the two prescribed subobjects. Provided that theory is consistent, a separator gives a complete internal extension and a complete internal extension gives a separator. Theorem 5.3 compares the original theory with its constant family against *all* consistent closed extensions in the slice. The Miller example exhibits a computational difference between a fixed representation and computation independent of the representation. This is a defensible account of the contribution. Priority for the precise combination remains open.

**Outline used for the review.** The abstract and §1 state the questions and applications. §§2–3 give the categorical hypotheses and the lifted comparison. §4 distinguishes reduction to an existing theory, deductive closure, translation, and free generation. §5 treats partial slices and extensions. §§6–8 realize the hypotheses in syntactic arithmetic and recover numerical conclusions. §§9–10 give the Miller and cosheaf realizations. Appendices A and B supply deduction and the cosheaf category. Appendices C and D bound the comparison claims. The [subsection outline](outline.md) supplies the finer division. The existing order serves the argument.

## (B) Major weaknesses

**1. The reduction criterion has a close published numerical formulation.** At p. 10, Corollary 4.3 concludes that the theorem and refutation domains of every consistent extension are inseparable, and consequently their join is proper. Visser’s Lemma 23 already gives essential undecidability when a recursive function sends a recursively inseparable pair to provable and refutable sentences. Kirst and Peters, Definition 17 and Theorem 20, use a map with the same two preservation conditions on their diagonal pair. In constructive type theory with their computability assumptions they obtain an independent sentence for each extension. Kleene’s original paper constructs the underlying numerical obstruction. Neither predecessor appears in §4.3. The categorical proof pulls back a complementary pair along a total morphism. Its conclusion applies to restriction idempotents and does not require a chosen sentence outside their join. [Visser, Lemma 23](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00153-024-00911-y) · [Kirst and Peters, §§3–4](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.CSL.2023.30) · [Kleene, pp. 800–802](https://dwc.knaw.nl/DL/publications/PU00018825.pdf).

After the proof of Corollary 4.3, insert the following paragraph.

> Visser’s Lemma 23 gives the numerical reduction from a recursively inseparable pair to provable and refutable sentences. In synthetic computability, Kirst and Peters use a diagonal pair to obtain an independent sentence for each extension (Definition 17 and Theorem 20). Here inverse image along the total morphism takes any proposed separator back to the original domains. The conclusion is inseparability of the theorem and refutation domains of every consistent extension.

**2. Theorem 4.7 has an antecedent at the level of syntax.** On p. 14 the unit assigns an atomic sentence to each index, and the counit interprets the new predicate named by a sentence as that sentence. Voutsadakis, Theorem 7, had already given a substitution adjunction for relational signatures in finite variable contexts. Its morphisms assign formulas to predicate symbols. His objects at this stage are signatures. In v60 the target objects are consistent theories, and the right adjoint remembers the two subobjects of the sentence object. The two adjunctions share the extension of an assignment by substitution. A formal generalization of Voutsadakis’s categories would require further comparison data. [Voutsadakis, pp. 479, 483–484](https://www.voutsadakis.com/RESEARCH/PUBLISHED/fol.pdf).

In §4.6, insert the following paragraph after defining the two categories and functors, immediately before Theorem 4.7.

> Voutsadakis gives an adjunction for substitution of formulas for predicate symbols in relational signatures (Theorem 7). Here a total morphism from the indexing object to the target sentence object assigns closed sentences to the nullary predicates. It extends to a theory morphism precisely when it takes the two prescribed subobjects into the theorem and refutation subobjects, respectively.

A referee can recover the hom-set bijection from free syntax and least deductive closure. Restrict a translation to the generators, or extend an assignment by syntax initiality and apply closure to its two axiom families. There is no further choice of proofs in a translation. The converse in Corollary 4.8 requires an additional argument in the ambient category. The complementary subobjects give a total morphism to $1+1$, and the Boolean valuation gives a closed, consistent, complete extension. With arbitrary subsets and functions in **Set**, every disjoint pair is separable. The numerical precedent uses computable separators. The general criterion is one-way for a theory specified in advance. Visser records Ehrenfeucht’s example of an essentially undecidable theory with recursively separable theorems and refutations. Thus the restriction of the biconditional to the generated theories matters. [Visser, discussion following Theorem 9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00153-024-00911-y).

**3. The arithmetic conclusions have two different precedents.** At p. 37, Corollary 8.4 establishes the recursive inseparability conclusion of Di Paola–Heller’s example (3) for consistent c.e. extensions of PA. Their example assumes definability of every recursive set in a consistent arithmetical theory. V60 constructs its reduction in the PA case. At p. 41, Theorem 8.6 constructs one total morphism assigning independent sentences to extension indices. Pour-El’s *effectively extensible theories* already isolate the numerical operation of finding an independent sentence effectively from a presentation of a consistent extension. The first comparison needs its hypothesis, and the second needs its antecedent. Keep the full effective hypothesis and the ambient category in the comparison. [Di Paola and Heller, p. 594, (3)](https://doi.org/10.2307/2274352) · [Pour-El, §3, pp. 271–272](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~yytran/PourEl_recursion_axiomatizable_theories.pdf).

At Corollary 8.4 add the following sentence pair. “Di Paola and Heller’s third formulation assumes that every recursive set is definable in the theory. Corollary 8.4 obtains its inseparability conclusion for every consistent c.e. extension of PA.” At Theorem 8.6 add the following comparison. “Pour-El studied the effective production of independent sentences from presentations of consistent extensions. Theorem 8.6 realizes such an assignment by a single total morphism $q:E\to S$ in the total category of the restriction-idempotent completion of the syntactic recursion category for PA.”

## (D) Cross-section consistency failures

The following are tests of proposed comparisons. The printed theorem statements pass them.

- **A proper join need not miss a global point.** In sets with an action of the group of order two, take two fixed points together with one free orbit. Take the two singleton fixed points as subobjects. Their union is proper and contains every global point. This refutes the inference from a proper join alone to an independent global sentence. The example tests that inference only. Corollary 4.3 states noncoverage. Theorem 8.6 separately constructs a numerical witness. The paper can use both results without identifying their conclusions.
- **A slice over the theorem carrier does not classify the theory.** At pp. 15–17, Lemma 5.1 constructs a Turing morphism in the partial slice. Theorem 5.2 treats postcomposition along a monomorphism. Theorem 5.3 uses a chosen logical theorem to evaluate an arbitrary closed slice extension at a point of the theorem carrier. Parameterized deduction establishes that the resulting theory is closed, and the finite-limit, image and coproduct comparisons preserve consistency and completeness. Once these preservation properties are given, reflection follows by the retraction. The final example in §5 notes that isomorphic carriers have equivalent slices. Keep the constant sentence family in any summary of the result.
- **The applications demonstrate different parts of the argument.** Corollary 8.4 treats PA extensions. Corollary 8.10 applies slice transfer to the separate nullary-predicate theory of the arithmetic diagonal pair. In §9, Miller supplied the sequence and Bauer–Hanson its use in parameterized realizability. Lemma 9.3 constructs one total recursive map with the two diagonal domains as the exact inverse images of the cuts below and above one half. Every fixed binary representation computes a separator, yet no separator is computable with representation-independent values (Corollary 9.4). Theorem 9.7 uses the sentence $c_n<1/2$ and the reduction criterion for every consistent extension whose axioms a single oracle program semidecides from every representation. In §10, Theorem 10.5 concerns compatible families, while Corollary 10.6 and Proposition 10.8 permit an independently chosen consistent c.e.-axiomatized extension at either endpoint. The Miller and fork examples justify space in the introduction because their quantifiers change the mathematics. [Miller, Theorem 6.3 and following paragraph](https://people.math.wisc.edu/~jsmiller8/Papers/continuous.pdf) · [Bauer and Hanson](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01256).
- **The source-lift condition has an independent purpose.** Theorem 3.1 (p. 6) indexes a morphism in the source. Appendix C (pp. 81–84) exhibits a target conditional that cannot be lifted, contradicting the earlier 2006 functor claim under its printed hypotheses. The existing related-work paragraph already identifies both results. Keep this concrete reason for the revised hypotheses. [Earlier report, Theorem 4.1](https://academicworks.cuny.edu/gc_cs_tr/277/).

## (E) Acceptance conditions, non-negotiable

1. **Introduction and abstract.** Retain the theorem-led abstract and the two-way numerical precedent on p. 3. Orient the introduction around the free internal theory and the slice-transfer theorem. If “reversal” is used, specify the input, output and assumed calculus, and credit the earlier numerical constructions. Avoid priority claims based on a search.
2. **At §§4.3 and 4.6.** Add the two local comparisons in (B). A reader should be able to identify the earlier reduction, the earlier syntactic adjoint, the extension hypotheses, and the contribution of the internal Boolean valuation. No new subsection is needed.
3. **At §§8.1 and 8.3.** Give the Di Paola–Heller and Pour-El comparisons once, with their respective hypotheses. Reserve a claim to the general definability theorem for a proof constructing the required reduction under that hypothesis.
4. **Choose one application to show what the category changes.** One sentence in the existing Miller paragraph in §1 suffices. “Each binary representation computes a separator for the indices at which the sequence lies below or above one half, but no separator is computed with the same values from every representation (Corollary 9.4).” Use the existing sentence about uniform semidecision to state Theorem 9.7.
5. **Use one location for each substantive comparison.** The introduction should identify the main neighboring results and the question left open by them. Give the detailed comparison beside a theorem when its hypotheses, output or proof method would otherwise be mistaken for those of its predecessor. Prefer the local mathematical comparison over a second bibliographical paragraph repeating the first.

Items 1–3 determine the recommendation on positioning. Items 4–5 suggest how to present the comparisons economically. The numbered statements require no change on the evidence reviewed.

**Decision.** The strongest confirmed objection is that §§4 and 8 omit prior results supplying recognizable parts of the principal constructions. The strongest unresolved objection is that a more general syntactic or indexed-logic result may yield one of the internal constructions by specialization. No source located in this review supplies the required comparison of categories and functors. The most important unchecked question is priority for the precise internal and representation-independent theorems. It is unnecessary to settle that question to make the recommended comparisons. Revise the comparisons before submission.
