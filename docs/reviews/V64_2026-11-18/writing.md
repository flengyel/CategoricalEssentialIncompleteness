# Mathematical writing review of manuscript v64

The paper's opening now gives the reader two results to follow. A disjoint pair generates a first-order theory, and evaluation at a logical theorem transfers essential incompleteness between a theory and its constant family. The three applications have different meanings of “extension,” which the abstract states with appropriate care. I would keep this order. The most consequential local edits are to identify the standard sentence codes in the introduction, say which idempotent splits at the end of Lemma 5.1, and let the syntactic example in §6 use that lemma.

## Definition order, notation and diagrams

- **Keep the false/true order of the Boolean object.** Corollary 4.8 defines the injections into $1+1$ before using its separator. Their order in the Boolean object is false, true. The positive branch of the separator is listed first because its *source* is $C+D$. Appendix A.1 fixes the same convention and proves the Boolean identities by jointly epic summand injections.
- **Keep the distinctions among $A$, $\operatorname{im}(a)$ and $\operatorname{Gen}(A)$.** In §4.4 the arbitrary morphism has type $a:A\to S$. The axiom object for deduction is its image subobject of $S$. Calling $a$ itself an axiom subobject would change what the initiality hypothesis requires.
- **Say which category supplies the partial inverse.** In §5.1 the monomorphism lies in the total category, its partial inverse lies in the restriction completion, and the graph of $p:X\to T$ with $m_X:X\to Q$ is over $T$. Keeping the three types visible takes one equation. At §5.3 the types $B_U:\mathscr C\to\mathscr C[U]$ and $E_U:\mathscr C[U]\to\mathscr C$ are already displayed before use.
- **Retain the meaning of composition and the existing diagrams.** Section 2.1 fixes $gf=g\circ f$. In the theory-extension square on p. 16 the two paths agree by $ui=t$. In Appendix A.4 the diagonal has source the rule functor applied to the image and target the image carrier. The first square compares theories and the second constructs an algebra. Their labels do work that a second display of equations would duplicate.
- **Explain the change of interpretation in words.** The structure $K$ in §7.3 satisfies the given sentence axioms. The arithmetic model $M$ in §7.4 interprets program graphs. A standard sentence has a code in $\mathbb N$. An element of $G_M S$ can be an ultrafilter class of varying codes. Section 9 explicitly defines a binary representation and uses one program for *all* such representations. Section 10 defines the two endpoint morphisms before invoking componentwise incompleteness.

I would retain the present section and appendix order. Appendix A supplies common hypotheses to several main-text proofs. Moving it forward in full would interpose the substitution and proof-tree constructions between the statement of the problem and its solution.

## Prioritized replacement wording

1. **§1, p. 2, final sentences on arithmetic incompleteness.** “For each consistent c.e. extension of PA, the sets of standard sentence codes provable and refutable from the extension are recursively inseparable (Corollary 8.4). Under the standard interpretation, one total morphism assigns an independent sentence to each index for the axiom enumeration of a consistent extension (Theorem 8.6).”
2. **Lemma 5.1, p. 15, last sentence.** “For each $p:X\to T$, the graph embedding $m=\langle p,m_X\rangle$ and its partial inverse satisfy $m^\dagger m=1_X$ and $mm^\dagger=\overline{m^\dagger}$ over $T$. Thus $p$ splits a restriction idempotent on $T\times Q\to T$. Since $\mathscr R[T]$ is full and $\mathscr C[T]$ is split, this gives the claimed restriction equivalence.”
3. **End of the proper-inclusion argument in Theorem 5.2, p. 16.** “Such a factorization would make the monomorphism $i$ a split epimorphism, hence an isomorphism. For proper $i$, the object $1_U$ therefore lies outside the essential image of $\Sigma_i$.”
4. **§5 opening and Theorem 5.3, p. 17.** Replace the second sentence of the opening with “Evaluation at a chosen logical theorem lets us compare a theory with its constant family in the associated category. Under the deduction hypotheses of Theorem 5.3, either is essentially incomplete exactly when the other is.” In the proof replace the passage beginning “Let $v$ be a closed sentence theory” through the end of that paragraph with “Write $F$ for $B_U$ or $E_U$. If $v$ is closed, applying $F$ to its finite rule diagrams equips $F(C(v))$ with a rule algebra containing the axiom subobject $Fv$. The initial derivation algebra for $Fv$ therefore maps to that algebra. Taking the image of its conclusion morphism and restricting to sentences gives $\operatorname{Gen}(Fv)\leq Fv$. Extensivity gives equality.” In the next paragraph introduce the reverse direction with “Conversely, let $W$ be any consistent complete closed extension of $B_Uu$ in the slice. Then $E_UW$ is such an extension of $E_UB_Uu\cong u$.”
5. **Lemma 6.3, pp. 22–23.** After the concrete presentation of the restriction terminal object, products and retractions, replace the passage from “The Turing morphism extends” through “therefore applies” with “The regularity, coproducts and partial inverses proved above give the hypotheses of Lemma 5.1 for $S_T$. Its Turing morphism on $U=(\omega,1_\omega)$ is represented by the original evaluator $\tau_T=[u]_T$.” Retain the following paragraph on ranges, which proves a different assertion.
6. **§8.7, p. 50, before the definition of $S^{\pm}$.** “To apply the associated-category theorem to the arithmetic diagonal pair, we form its free first-order theory in the total category $\mathcal B_{\mathrm{PA}}$. Its language has one nullary predicate $P_n$ for each label $n\in\omega$.” Combine this with the existing choices of variable object and signature so that each is stated once.
