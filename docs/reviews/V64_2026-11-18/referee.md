## (B) Major weaknesses

I found no substantial missing proof step in the free-theory adjunction, the separator converse or the transfer to arbitrary slice extensions. Three attempts to break these arguments explain this assessment.

- **Can the free construction silently assume consistency for every disjoint pair?** The domain of the adjunction in Theorem 4.7, pp. 13–14, consists of pairs whose generated theory is consistent. For the right adjoint, a sentence of the target interprets the free predicate bearing its label. The inverse image of the consistent target theory is a consistent closed extension of the signed axioms. This proves the object condition in the one direction where it must be deduced. For an arbitrary axiom morphism, Theorem 4.4, p. 11, first takes its regular image. Initial derivation algebras for axiom subobjects suffice.
- **Does the converse of Corollary 4.8, p. 14, use classical choice of a separator at each point?** A complementary pair with inclusions $m:C\hookrightarrow X$ and $n:D\hookrightarrow X$ gives a monic copairing by disjoint stable coproducts. Its image is the join, hence the identity subobject. Its inverse therefore gives the decision morphism on $X$ itself. Equality evaluates to true, quantification to the value of the body, and substitution leaves the value invariant. The truth fiber is closed, consistent and complete. All three properties are subobject statements, including when global elements fail to detect equality.
- **Does Theorem 5.3, p. 17, test only constant extensions?** Given an arbitrary consistent complete closed $W\hookrightarrow U\times S$ containing $B_Uu$, pull back at the specified logical theorem $\lambda_U:1\to U$. Finite limits, coproducts and images give consistency and completeness of $E_UW$. Transport the rule algebra of the closed formula subobject, use initiality for the image axioms in the target, then restrict to sentences. This gives closure. The retraction identifies $E_UB_Uu$ with $u$, so $E_UW$ extends $u$. The reverse direction takes a constant family. For a concrete test in sets, take two parameters and the complete propositional theories assigning a free atom opposite values in the two fibers. Evaluation yields the chosen fiber and requires no equality between the fibers. The final clause also needs Corollary 4.3 to carry the reduction through each consistent extension.

## (C) Minor issues

1. **The final inference in Lemma 5.1, §5.1, p. 15.** The proof concludes “a retract … by a total embedding, so splitting” gives the partial slice. The word *retract* by itself supplies an ordinary idempotent. For example, in partial functions take $m:\{0\}\hookrightarrow\{0,1\}$ and the total constant retraction $r:\{0,1\}\to\{0\}$. Then $rm=1$, and $mr$ is a total, nonidentity idempotent. A total restriction idempotent must be an identity. In the actual proof, $m=\langle p,m_X\rangle$ has the specified **partial inverse** over $T$, so $mm^\dagger=\overline{m^\dagger}$. This is a restriction idempotent on an object of the full subcategory, and its splitting recovers $p$. The example tests only the abbreviated inference. The equivalence and the applications in §§8–10 survive the test. **Ambiguous · counterexample and derivation · minor.** Reuse the witness in the final sentence.
2. **The conclusion named in Theorem 5.2, §5.2, p. 16.** The last paragraph says “a monic split epimorphism is an isomorphism, proving properness.” Properness of $i$ is the assumption for this clause. An isomorphism from $1_U$ to an image object would give a section of $i$, contradicting that assumption. What has just been proved is failure of essential surjectivity of $\Sigma_i$. The two displayed adjunctions are unaffected. **Inconsistent use · derivation · minor.** Name the functor conclusion in that sentence.
3. **The carrier of recursive inseparability, §1, p. 2.** “The theorem and refutation sets are recursively inseparable” immediately follows discussion of both the standard model and its ultrapowers. Corollary 8.4, p. 38, pulls a recursive subset of the natural numbers back along the numeral-substitution function. For an arbitrary ultrapower, Theorems 7.9 and 8.3 give disjoint subobjects and failure of coverage in the interpreted sentence object. The manuscript supplies no effective numbering of that object's elements. Thus the numerical assertion needs an explicit subject at its first appearance. The later theorems have the appropriate types. **Ambiguous · structural argument · minor.** Say “The sets of standard sentence codes provable and refutable from the extension are recursively inseparable.”

**A test of the warning after Corollary 4.3, p. 10.** In the computable fork $1\leftarrow0\to2$ of §10, give the center and endpoint 2 the propositional language on one atom $p$, with axiom $p$. At endpoint 1 add atoms $P_n$ and assert $P_n$ for $n\in A$, $\neg P_n$ for $n\in B$, where $A,B$ are disjoint recursively inseparable c.e. sets. The transitions include the common atom. The deductive closures are c.e., compatible along both transitions and consistent. For consistency at endpoint 1 choose an ordinary set valuation satisfying the signed axioms. A complementary pair separating the theorem and refutation domains there would decide a separator of $A,B$ on the atom codes. Also $A\cup B\ne\mathbb N$, since otherwise both would be recursive. An atom at any omitted index can take either truth value, so the join is proper at that endpoint. Yet a global sentence is determined by its center component. The axiom $p$ decides every formula in that one-atom language, and both transitions preserve its derivation or its refutation. Thus **every global sentence** lies in the join. This gives an inhabited example where replacing properness by an independent global sentence would be a genuine strengthening.

## (E) Acceptance conditions, non-negotiable

For the mathematics as stated, the essential conditions are present. Their endpoints can be checked directly.

1. In §§4.4–4.6, generation must factor an arbitrary axiom morphism through its regular image, and use initiality for an axiom subobject. Corollary 4.8 must retain consistency of the generated theory. These conditions are met. Dropping them would require fresh proofs of the broader claims.
2. In §5, the split witness must be a restriction idempotent and evaluation must take *any* consistent complete closed slice extension to a base extension. Both constructions are available. If either failed, the corresponding equivalence or transfer claim would have to be withdrawn. The explicit witness in (C1) would make the first construction easier to verify.
3. In §§7–10, each computability conclusion must name its carrier and its allowed oracles or axiom presentations. The numbered results do so. Applying recursive inseparability to arbitrary ultrapower carriers, or taking one name-dependent separator as a common separator, would invalidate those stronger assertions. The change in (C3) resolves the introductory ambiguity. The local copy edits do not warrant rejection.
