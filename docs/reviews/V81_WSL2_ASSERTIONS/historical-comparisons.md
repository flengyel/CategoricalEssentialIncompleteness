## Domains in recursion categories

The nonzero version of Di Paola–Heller 8.15 takes the published fixed-source Turing morphism with codomain the binary coproduct. A section of the copairing of two complementary domains is total. Its two inverse images are exactly the original domains. Reversing the summands gives a total index whose inverse images of both halves of a supposed separator are empty. Their copairing has full range, so this index is zero, contrary to the nonzero identity. The one-morphism dominical category proves the necessity of that qualification. Corollary 3.3 proves the application under its own explicit nonzero hypothesis. [§1](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/01-introduction.tex#L173) · [Original Theorem 8.15](https://doi.org/10.2307/2274352) · [Section and fixed-source diagonal](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46DominicalDiagonal.lean) · [Statement types](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46DominicalLiteratureChecks.lean) · [Zero case](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46TrivialDominicalCategoryChecks.lean).

Bernardi's precompleteness condition on a relation domain η supplies, for each partial endomorphism ψ, a total h with the same restriction on ψ and on the partial pairing of ψ and h followed by η. Totalize the Turing diagonal followed by φ, then take an index of this totalizer. Their composite g satisfies the domain equation of Theorem 1. For Theorem 2 a domain e is a union of classes when the near product of e and any disjoint domain is annihilated by η. For each nonzero e, Di Paola–Heller 6.6 first supplies a total morphism into e from an arbitrary object. A separator would give a total morphism carrying each half into the opposite union of classes. Its fixed-point equation forces both inverse images of the separator halves to be empty. The numerical application in §8.6 has separate proofs for the PA program relation and for arbitrary disjoint nonempty unions of its classes. [§1](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/01-introduction.tex#L175) · [Bernardi, §§2–3](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1986-0848889-9) · [Total morphism and fixed-point equation](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46DominicalPrecompleteness.lean) · [Class unions](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46DominicalClassUnions.lean) · [Expanded hypotheses](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46DominicalLiteratureChecks.lean) · [Numerical PA example](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46ProvableProgramEquality.lean).

Montagna's Remark 3.1 uses a single total binary productive morphism. In the unprimed PA category the product coding supplies this morphism. The two evaluation tests use the opposite projections of a coded pair. A section of their copairing divides their union into disjoint subdomains. Each enlarged domain has indices at every source object. If two enlargements are disjoint, evaluating them at the paired indices annihilates both. This proves the historical statement. Corollary 7.3 and the independent-sentence construction of Theorem 8.6 have their own proofs. [§1](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/01-introduction.tex#L179) · [Montagna, p. 114](https://doi.org/10.1305/ndjfl/1093634998) · [General reduction and productive morphism](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46EffectiveInseparability.lean) · [Unprimed PA statement](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46SyntacticEffectiveInseparabilityChecks.lean).

## Existence theorems named in the related work

Section 1 names Heller’s existence theorem and Lengyel’s absolute and relative existence theorems for recursion categories. The historical constructions require the iteration structures in their original formulations. The paper's three examples have direct evaluators. [Related work, §1](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/01-introduction.tex#L166).

- Heller's Theorem 9.2 takes a locally connected iteration category and a uniformly generated isotypical B-sharp subcategory. The conclusion gives its Turing completion the structure of a recursion category. A proof at that type requires the Turing completion of the given subcategory and its universal morphism. [Heller, §§8–9](https://doi.org/10.2307/2274486).
- Lengyel's Theorem 21 starts with a separated iteration category. Its absolute construction requires the Turing completion and its evaluator for the uniformly generated isotypical B-sharp subcategory. Applying the relative theorem to the identity functor would give the absolute result. [Lengyel, §4](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0168-0072(03)00060-5).
- Lengyel's Theorem 26 uses separation by an iteration functor, including faithfulness and the separated-target hypothesis, for a uniformly generated isotypical B-sharp subcategory. At the original types the missing construction is the Turing completion and the universal computation under this relative separation hypothesis. The corrected simulation also preserves partial domains. [Lengyel, §4](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0168-0072(03)00060-5) · [Corrections, version 10](https://github.com/flengyel/errata/blob/5555e4fa1da7b8e3a0836dd1fbf1ca385cacd03b/lengyel2004/lengyel2004_repairs_v10.tex#L619) · [Primary-source comparison](../V80_ASSERTIONS/primary01.md).
