| [§4, after Corollary 4.3](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/03a-internal-theories.tex#L237), Visser, Lemma 23 | In any first-order language with encoded sentences, let a computable code map send one side of a recursively inseparable pair to the theorems of `T` and the other to its refutations. For every consistent `U` containing the theorems of `T`, its set of theorem codes is undecidable. Pulling back a decider along the given map produces a recursive separator. This covers different axiom presentations and also applies to inclusion of axiom sets. [Production theorem](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46EssentialUndecidability.lean) · [Axiom inclusion](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46EssentialUndecidabilityChecks.lean) · [Visser, Lemma 23](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00153-024-00911-y) | The paper compares two methods here. | [§4, same paragraph](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/03a-internal-theories.tex#L240), Kirst and Peters, Theorem 20 | For a type of sentences with negation, a semidecision is a Boolean test indexed by a natural-number stage. Partial functions have deterministic, option-valued stages. Under the authors’ enumeration hypothesis EPF, let a map strongly separate the diagonal outputs 1 and 0. For each consistent extension with a semidecision of provability, code the reversed search for a proof of either sign. At its own index neither sign of the image sentence is provable. EPF is an explicit hypothesis of the implication, as in their Axiom 13. [Definitions and construction](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46SyntheticIndependence.lean) · [Expanded strong separation and extension](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46SyntheticIndependenceChecks.lean) · [Kirst and Peters, §§2–4](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.CSL.2023.30) | This is the external synthetic result cited for comparison with Corollary 4.3. | [§4, before Theorem 4.7](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/03b-internal-adjunctions.tex#L368), Voutsadakis, Theorem 7 | A hierarchy of symbols has one component for each finite set `N` of variable indices, with intersection given by `N ∩ M`. Component maps are coherent under inclusion. Formula formation uses indexed equality, symbols, negation, conjunction and existential binding. In the signature category a morphism assigns a target formula in each symbol's context. The free functor sends symbols to atoms, and the right adjoint substitutes through every constructor, extending the variable context under a binder. The unit is the inclusion of generators and the counit is the identity assignment on formulas. [Components and formulas](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46RelationalSignature.lean) · [Adjunction](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46RelationalSignatureAdjunction.lean) · [Unit, counit and binder](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46RelationalSignatureChecks.lean) · [Voutsadakis, Definitions 1–3 and Theorem 7](https://www.voutsadakis.com/RESEARCH/PUBLISHED/fol.pdf#page=12) | Theorem 4.7 constructs a different adjunction for signed first-order theories. | [§8.3, opening paragraph](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/08b-uniform-independent-sentences.tex#L4) | A complete consistent theory with decidable axiom codes has no independent sentence at the empty extension. ## Existence theorems named in the related work

Section 1 also names two existence theorems for recursion categories and gives their subject. The historical constructions require the iteration structures in their original formulations. The paper's three examples have direct evaluators. [Related work, §1](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/01-introduction.tex#L166).

- Heller's Theorem 9.2 takes a locally connected iteration category and a uniformly generated isotypical B-sharp subcategory. The conclusion gives its Turing completion the structure of a recursion category. A proof at that type requires the Turing completion of the given subcategory and its universal morphism. [Heller, §§8–9](https://doi.org/10.2307/2274486).
- Lengyel's Theorem 26 uses separation by an iteration functor, including faithfulness and the separated-target hypothesis, for a uniformly generated isotypical B-sharp subcategory. At the original types the missing construction is the Turing completion and the universal computation under this relative separation hypothesis. The corrected simulation also preserves partial domains. [Lengyel, §4](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0168-0072(03)00060-5) · [Corrections, version 10](https://github.com/flengyel/errata/blob/5555e4fa1da7b8e3a0836dd1fbf1ca385cacd03b/lengyel2004/lengyel2004_repairs_v10.tex#L619) · [Primary-source comparison](../V80_ASSERTIONS/primary01.md).
