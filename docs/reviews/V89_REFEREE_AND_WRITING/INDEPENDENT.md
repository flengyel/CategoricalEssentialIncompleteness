## Lemma A.7

Work in a split restriction category with ranges. In its regular total category let $f=me$, with $e$ a regular epimorphism and $m$ a monomorphism. The partial inverse $r$ of this particular $m$ supplies both $rm=1$ and $mr=\bar r$.

* Since $(mr)f=f$ and $mr$ is a restriction idempotent, the definition of range in §2 gives $\operatorname{ran}(f)\le mr$. The agreement law gives the same inequality by comparing $mr$ and $1_Y$ on $f$.
* If a restriction idempotent $a$ fixes $f$, write $a=i t$ with $t i=1$ and $i$ total. The morphism $i$ is monic among total morphisms by cancellation with $t$, even if $t$ is partial. From $i t f=f$ and totality of $i,f$ it follows that $tf$ is total. Hence the factorization $f=i(tf)$ is in the total category. The image property supplies $u$ with $iu=m$. Thus $am=m$ and $a(mr)=mr$, giving $mr\le a$. Taking $a=\operatorname{ran}(f)$ proves the second inequality.

The Lean range class packages reception and agreement. Its theorem `Ranges.least` compares a fixing idempotent with the identity on $f$. In `range_regularImage`, `hleast` gives the first inequality. For the reverse inequality Lean splits the range itself, derives totality of the factor, uses the regular-epi/mono diagonal in the total category, and applies antisymmetry. The statement check instantiates the result with an arbitrary displayed regular-image factorization $f=me$ and includes the equality $mr=\bar r$. The separate application in a restriction splitting expressly assumes the image inclusion has a partial inverse.
