## Abstract

Replace the first paragraph with the passage below. The second paragraph already states the slice theorem, the uniform arithmetic result and the two further applications.

> In a split restriction category satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem 4.7, a consistent first-order theory freely generated by a disjoint pair of domains is essentially incomplete if and only if the domains are inseparable. For a recursion category we take its restriction idempotent completion. The object on which the domains lie indexes the nullary predicates, the only nonlogical symbols. The axioms assert the predicates indexed by the first domain and negate those indexed by the second. The two domains are inseparable when no two complementary domains contain them respectively. Essential incompleteness means that in every consistent deductively closed extension in the same language the join of the theorem and refutation subobjects is a proper subobject of the sentence object. On pairs generating consistent theories, this construction is left adjoint to the functor assigning to a consistent theory its theorem and refutation subobjects.

## §1, opening paragraph

> Suppose a consistent theory proves the numeral instances of a predicate on one set and refutes them on another. Smullyan proved that if the two sets are recursively inseparable, then so are the sets of codes of the theory’s provable and refutable sentences (Chapter III, §17, Theorem 23(b)). Di Paola and Heller sought an algebraic account of generalized incompleteness theorems of this kind (pp. 594–596, 629). We ask when a disjoint pair of domains generates an internal first-order theory with no complete consistent deductively closed extension in its language. For the consistent theory freely generated by the corresponding positive and negative atomic axioms, this happens exactly when the two domains are inseparable (Corollary 4.8).
