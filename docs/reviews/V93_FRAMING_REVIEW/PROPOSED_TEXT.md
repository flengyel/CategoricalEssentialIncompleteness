## Abstract

> Smullyan showed that if $A$ and $B$ are recursively inseparable sets of natural numbers and a formula $F(x)$ of a consistent theory $T$ satisfies $T\vdash F(\bar n)$ for $n\in A$ and $T\vdash\neg F(\bar n)$ for $n\in B$, then the sets of codes of provable and refutable sentences of $T$ are recursively inseparable. We replace classical computability by an arbitrary recursion category and characterize the disjoint pairs of domains in its restriction idempotent completion that generate essentially incomplete first-order theories. The only nonlogical symbols are nullary predicates indexed by the object on which the two domains $a,b$ lie, and the axioms are the atomic sentences indexed by $a$ and the negated atomic sentences indexed by $b$. A consistent theory so generated has a complete consistent deductively closed extension in its language if and only if two complementary domains contain $a$ and $b$ respectively. On pairs generating consistent theories, this construction is left adjoint to the functor assigning to a consistent theory its theorem and refutation subobjects.

> The total category must be regular with disjoint pullback-stable finite coproducts, and its monomorphisms must have partial inverses in the completion. For a decidable variable object, we assume initial syntax and variable-list algebras in every parameter slice and an initial derivation algebra for each axiom subobject. The construction requires no topos and uses neither exponentials nor a subobject classifier.

> Under the standard interpretation in $\mathbb{N}$, one total morphism sends each index for a computably enumerable set of additional axioms consistent with Peano arithmetic to the code of a sentence independent of the resulting theory. We also treat real closed ordered fields with constants for the entries of a Miller sequence and propositional theories from cosheaves on a three-point space.

## §1, opening paragraph

> Let $A,B\subseteq\mathbb{N}$ be recursively inseparable and let $T$ be a consistent arithmetical theory. A formula $F(x)$ strongly separates $A$ from $B$ in $T$ if $T\vdash F(\bar n)$ for every $n\in A$ and $T\vdash\neg F(\bar n)$ for every $n\in B$. Under this hypothesis the sets of codes of the provable and refutable sentences of $T$ are recursively inseparable (Smullyan, Chapter III, §17, Theorem 23(b), pp. 59–60). We give a categorical form of this argument and recover the conclusion of Di Paola and Heller’s statement (3) for every consistent computably enumerable extension of Peano arithmetic (Corollary 8.4). For the consistent first-order theory freely generated by the positive and negative atomic axioms of two disjoint domains, inseparability is also necessary for essential incompleteness. A separator of the domains determines a Boolean valuation whose true sentences form a complete consistent extension in the same language (Corollary 4.8).

## §1, related work

Add the following after the existing sentence on Heller's definition of prodominical categories.

> For a consistent c.e. extension $T$ of PA, the syntactic recursion category $S_T$ is prodominical, has ranges and admits a partial section for every morphism, although it fails dominicality (Di Paola–Montagna, pp. 645–646). Ranges require a separate hypothesis. In the recursion category of Heller's Example 5, a diagonal morphism has no range (§10, p. 1267).

Place the following paragraph before the existing account of Di Paola–Heller's Theorem 8.15.

> Di Paola and Heller proposed to obtain dominical categories, and in some cases recursion categories, from polyadic algebras (p. 595). They intended to use their results on creativity and inseparability in an algebraic treatment of generalized incompleteness (p. 629). The announced sequel did not appear. Here we start with a recursion category and use the initial algebras for syntax, lists and derivations assumed in §4 to construct theories in the total category of its restriction idempotent completion. The construction uses neither exponentials nor a subobject classifier.

## §8.1, immediately before Corollary 8.4

Replace the paragraph beginning “Di Paola and Heller record”. The pullback calculation stays in the proof of the corollary.

> Di Paola and Heller cite Smullyan for their statement (3) (p. 595). The corresponding result is Chapter III, §16, Theorem 22 of *Theory of Formal Systems* (p. 59). If every recursive set is definable in a consistent arithmetical theory $T$, the sets of codes of its provable and refutable sentences are recursively inseparable. Here $C\subseteq\mathbb{N}$ is definable in $T$ if there is a formula $F_C(x)$ such that $T\vdash F_C(\bar n)$ for $n\in C$ and $T\vdash\neg F_C(\bar n)$ for $n\notin C$. For a consistent c.e. extension $T$ of PA, Lemma 8.1 gives strong separation of $D_0$ from $D_1$ by $\alpha(x)$, as in Smullyan’s §17, Theorem 23(b) (p. 60). The computable function $\Theta(n)=\ulcorner\alpha(\bar n)\urcorner$ sends $D_0$ into the theorem set and $D_1$ into the refutation set.
