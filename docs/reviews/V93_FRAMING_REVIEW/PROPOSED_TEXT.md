## Abstract

> Smullyan showed that if a consistent theory proves a predicate on the numerals of one set and refutes it on those of another, recursive inseparability of the two sets passes to its provable and refutable sentence codes. We vary the recursion category of partial computations and characterize the disjoint pairs of domains in its restriction idempotent completion that generate essentially incomplete first-order theories. The common object indexes the nullary predicates, the only nonlogical symbols, which the axioms assert on the first domain and negate on the second. If the generated theory is consistent, it has a complete consistent deductively closed extension in its language exactly when two complementary domains contain the given domains respectively. On pairs generating consistent theories, this construction is left adjoint to taking theorem and refutation subobjects.

> In a split restriction category we require the total category to be regular with disjoint pullback-stable finite coproducts, and each of its monomorphisms to have a partial inverse in the split category. Given a decidable variable object, initial syntax and variable-list algebras in every parameter slice, and an initial derivation algebra for each axiom subobject, the construction needs no ambient topos. If initial derivation algebras exist for all parameterized axiom subobjects and every consistent theory contains a fixed logical theorem, a consistent theory is essentially incomplete exactly when its constant family over its theorem object is. A single total morphism, interpreted numerically, sends each index for a computably enumerable set of axioms consistent with Peano arithmetic to the code of a sentence independent of the resulting theory. Other applications treat real closed ordered fields with constants from a Miller sequence and propositional theories from cosheaves on a three-point space.

## §1, opening paragraph

> Suppose a consistent theory proves the numeral instances of a predicate on one set and refutes them on another. Smullyan proved that if the two sets are recursively inseparable, then so are the sets of codes of the theory’s provable and refutable sentences (Chapter III, §17, Theorem 23(b), pp. 59–60). We algebraize this single-pair argument in a recursion category and recover the conclusion of Di Paola and Heller’s statement (3) for every consistent computably enumerable extension of Peano arithmetic (Corollary 8.4). We also prove a converse. A consistent first-order theory freely generated by positive and negative atomic axioms on two disjoint domains is essentially incomplete exactly when the domains are inseparable (Corollary 4.8).

## §1, related work

Add the following after the existing sentence on Heller's definition of prodominical categories.

> For a consistent c.e. extension $T$ of PA, the syntactic recursion category $S_T$ is prodominical, has ranges and admits a partial section for every morphism, although it fails dominicality (Di Paola–Montagna, pp. 645–646). Ranges require a separate hypothesis. In the recursion category of Heller's Example 5, a diagonal morphism has no range (§10, p. 1267).

Place the following paragraph before the existing account of Di Paola–Heller's Theorem 8.15.

> Di Paola and Heller proposed to obtain dominical categories, and in some cases recursion categories, from polyadic algebras (p. 595). They intended to use their results on creativity and inseparability in an algebraic treatment of generalized incompleteness (p. 629). We have found no published joint sequel on this question. Here we start with a recursion category and use the initial algebras for syntax, lists and derivations assumed in §4 to construct theories in the total category of its restriction idempotent completion. The construction uses neither exponentials nor a subobject classifier.
