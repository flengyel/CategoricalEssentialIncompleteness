**Recommendation.** Open with Smullyan’s single-pair criterion, then explain what varies and what is proved. The recursion category supplies the partial computations. The paper characterizes essential incompleteness for the theory freely generated by a disjoint pair of domains. State the hypotheses in the abstract itself, including the additional assumptions for the slice equivalence. ## Reading the abstract on its own

The v93 abstract sends readers to Theorems 4.7 and 5.3 for two different sets of assumptions. The first proposal kept the former reference and retained the existing second paragraph. The corrected proposal supplies both paragraphs. Its positive formulation of the main result explains what a separator does. A complete consistent closed extension exists precisely when two complementary domains contain the given domains respectively. Smullyan’s statement concerns codes of provable and refutable sentences. The converse concerns the freely generated internal theory. ## Referee findings

**1, 3. What Smullyan proved.** In Chapter III, §16, Theorem 22 (p. 59), definability of every recursive set implies recursive inseparability of the sets of codes of provable and refutable sentences. Di Paola and Heller's (3) cites the book as [39]. Theorem 22 is identified by comparing the two printed statements. In §17, Theorem 23(b) (pp. 59–60, part (b) on p. 60), a consistent system obtains the same conclusion if it *strongly separates* one recursively inseparable pair. There must be one predicate whose numeral instances the system proves on the first set and refutes on the second. Any recursive separator of the resulting sets of sentence codes would pull back under the computable instance map to a separator of the given pair. A complete consistent c.e. extension would give such a separator by enumerating proofs of each sentence and its negation. [Smullyan, Chapter III](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt1b7x7ww.6) · [Searchable book text, printed pp. 59–60](https://dokumen.pub/theory-of-formal-systems-am-47-volume-47-9781400882007.html) · [Di Paola–Heller, pp. 594–595 and ref. 39](https://doi.org/10.2307/2274352)

Use *suffices*. A necessity claim for arbitrary theories has a counterexample. Ehrenfeucht constructed an essentially undecidable theory whose theorem and refutation sets admit a recursive separator. A predicate strongly separating any recursively inseparable pair in that theory would contradict the inverse-image argument. The manuscript's converse has a specific reason to hold. For the free theory of Corollary 4.8, a separator of the indexing domains gives a Boolean valuation of all the generating predicates and hence a complete consistent extension. [Visser, Remark 11 and ref. 13](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00153-024-00911-y) · [Corollary 4.8](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/03b-internal-adjunctions.tex#L478) · [Lean statement with the full hypotheses](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46SignedFirstOrderIncompleteness.lean#L124)

**1, 4. What the paper proves.** Corollary 4.3 takes an inseparable pair as input. Under its inverse-image hypotheses, a total morphism sending the pair into the theorem and refutation domains makes a consistent theory essentially incomplete. Corollary 4.8 proves the equivalence for the consistent theory generated by the two families of atomic axioms under Theorem 4.7. It applies in a split restriction category and assumes no Turing morphism. The separate comparison in §3 uses a Turing morphism and a lift of a partial conditional. Proposition C.1 gives a recursion category with ranges and binary restriction coproducts in which the specified diagonal pair is separable. This disproves a guarantee for that pair. Existence of other inseparable pairs in that example is a different question. [Corollary 4.3](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/03a-internal-theories.tex) · [Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.3](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/03-sections-and-inseparability.tex) · [Proposition C.1](../../../manuscript/latex/appendices/c-section-lifting.tex) · [Lean separation witness](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46MachineOracleTuring.lean#L147)

**4, 5. What Di Paola and Heller proposed.** Corollary 8.4 recovers the conclusion of (3) for consistent c.e. extensions of PA using a particular computable sentence map. Their (1) and (2) concern essential undecidability under other definability hypotheses. Essential undecidability also follows in the paper's arithmetic instances, since the theorem set of a consistent decidable extension would separate the original two sets. Their (4) concerns creativity of the theorem set, assuming representability of all r.e. sets and recursive enumerability of the theorem set (p. 595, sentence following (5)). Their (5) concerns effective inseparability for Rosser theories. Neither stronger conclusion follows from the ordinary inseparability proved here. [Corollary 8.4 and its proof](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/08-essential-incompleteness-of-arithmetic.tex#L239) · [Di Paola–Heller, pp. 594–595](https://doi.org/10.2307/2274352)

On p. 595 the proposed direction is **from polyadic algebras to dominical categories**, and in some cases to recursion categories. On p. 629 the authors name Theorems 8.5, 8.7, 8.15 and 8.18 in connection with future work. Theorem 8.5 gives creativity of a diagonal halting domain, and Theorem 8.18 gives effectively inseparable domains. A corresponding result about the theorem and refutation sets of a theory would require an argument transferring the effective data. Heller’s posthumous MathSciNet bibliography records the 1987 paper and no subsequent joint paper with Di Paola. Heller’s 1988 and 1990 papers concern the existence of recursion categories. In 1991 Di Paola and Montagna described the 1987 work as a first step. This supports “We have found no published joint sequel on this question.” The bibliography cannot exclude an unpublished manuscript or a publication outside its coverage. [Di Paola–Heller, pp. 595, 622, 625–626, 629](https://doi.org/10.2307/2274352) · [MathSciNet bibliography for Heller, pp. 1–2](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/files/Alex-Heller-pub.pdf) · [Di Paola–Montagna, p. 643](https://doi.org/10.2307/2274707)

**6. Which topos assumptions can be dispensed with.** Theorem 4.7 specifies a regular total category with disjoint pullback-stable finite coproducts, partial inverses for its monomorphisms, a decidable variable object, syntax and variable-list algebras initial in every parameter slice, and initial derivation algebras for the prescribed axiom subobjects. It assumes neither exponentials nor a subobject classifier. Regularity already provides quotients of kernel pairs. Saying that the proof uses “no exact quotients” would obscure that use. [Theorem 4.7](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/03b-internal-adjunctions.tex#L376) · [Construction by regular image, Appendix A](../../../manuscript/latex/appendices/a-syntax-and-theories.tex)

In a Boolean subobject lattice, disjoint subobjects $a,b$ satisfy $b\leq\neg a$, so $a,\neg a$ separate them. Booleanity matters. In the functor topos on $0\to1,\ 0\to2$, consider the two subobjects of the terminal functor supported on the individual endpoints. They are disjoint. In any complementary partition, the part containing the point at 0 contains both endpoints, so the two subobjects are inseparable. The paper's Appendix D shows how a *faithful* interpretation into a category of partial maps can introduce separators. Conversely, for a fully faithful functor between finitely complete extensive categories preserving finite limits and finite coproducts, a target separator determines a decision morphism $FX\to F(1+1)$. Fullness lifts it, faithfulness reflects its two prescribed values, and the summand pullbacks give a source separator. A blanket assertion about embeddings into toposes would lose these distinctions. [Faithful interpretation, Appendix D](../../../manuscript/latex/appendices/d-faithful-family.tex)

**7. Why prodominicality and ranges occur separately.** For a consistent c.e. extension $T$ of PA, Di Paola–Montagna's $S_T$ has ranges, satisfies the axiom that each morphism has a partial section, and fails dominicality. The weaker zero-product law admits this example. In Heller's Example 5 the diagonal of the indicated object has no range in the generated recursion category. This explains the separate range hypothesis wherever the comparison uses ranges. Di Paola–Heller's Theorem 8.15 has the stronger $c^+$-dominical hypotheses. [Di Paola–Montagna, pp. 645–646](https://doi.org/10.2307/2274707) · [Heller, §10, Example 5, p. 1267](https://doi.org/10.2307/2274486) · [Di Paola–Heller, pp. 625–626](https://doi.org/10.2307/2274352)

## Mathematical writing

| Proposal | Editorial decision |
| --- | --- |
| 1, 3–5. Abstract and historical framing | State the equivalence once in the abstract. Open with Smullyan’s single-pair argument and explain it in §1 using numeral instances. Put the two theorem numbers next to the arithmetic comparison in §8. Retain the introduction's attribution to Murwanashyaka, Pakhomov and Visser for their numerical equivalence. The existing §8 already states (3) and constructs the sentence map. |
| 2. Name the completion | Use §2's *restriction idempotent completion* for the category, *split the restriction idempotents* for the operation, and $\operatorname{Split}(\mathcal C)$ where the category is already named. In v93 the long name occurs twice and *restriction splitting(s)* five times, including the line break in Proposition B.8. The §6 heading denotes the **total category** of the completion. |
| 6, 7. Foundational framing | Keep the hypotheses in the existing §1 paragraph. Add the two examples that explain the choice of hypotheses in related work. State in the abstract that the construction needs no ambient topos and identify exponentials and the subobject classifier in the introduction. The Boolean and embedding examples belong to this report. |
| 8. Attribution and Karoubi envelope | Accept the withdrawal. Cockett–Lack §2.3.3, Propositions 2.26–2.27 give the restriction idempotent completion. The full Karoubi envelope in the introduction's account of §10 is justified by Corollary B.9. |
