# Primary-source literature review of manuscript v57

Reviewed manuscript: *Logical consequence and essential incompleteness in recursion categories*, version 57, at repository commit `dfe6d3b9b948875d9d268ef8bbd332d65c6b53e5`. Scope: the introduction’s principal comparisons with earlier categorical recursion theory, the corresponding definitions in §2, and the cited arithmetic and Miller prerequisites. No manuscript edits were made.

## Finding

No incorrect attribution was confirmed among the primary-source claims checked below. The citations to Di Paola–Heller, Di Paola–Montagna, Heller, Montagna, Bernardi, Miller, Cockett–Hofstra, Cockett’s computability notes, and Bauer–Hanson support the stated comparisons. This is a source comparison, not a proof of the manuscript’s new results. One minor pinpoint improvement is identified for Bernardi below.

## Claims and source comparison

| `sections/01-introduction.tex:7–9` | Di Paola–Heller seek intrinsic categorical recursion theory as a route to generalized incompleteness. | Di Paola–Heller (1987), §1, printed pp. 594–596; supplied original PDF pp. 2–4 of 43. | Confirmed. The introduction identifies generalized incompleteness statements, the required creative/inseparable-set theory, and the desired intrinsic algebraic development. |
| `sections/01-introduction.tex:153–155` | Di Paola–Heller prove categorical recursive inseparability under section and coproduct hypotheses. | Di Paola–Heller, Theorem 8.15, printed pp. 625–626; Definition 6.2, p. 612; §7, pp. 615 onward. | Confirmed. Theorem 8.15 assumes a `c+`-dominical category and the specified Turing morphism. The `c` condition supplies sections for all morphisms; `+` includes compatible binary coproducts. The manuscript’s summary does not claim the weaker weak-choice hypothesis suffices for their theorem. |
| `sections/01-introduction.tex:162–166` | The same paper discusses formalized Löb, diagonalizable algebras, GL, and obstacles involving numerals and quantification. | Di Paola–Heller, printed pp. 595–596. | Confirmed. The source contrasts modal/algebraic fixed-point methods with the intrinsic recursion-theoretic approach. |
| `sections/01-introduction.tex:141–151`; §2 terminology | Heller adopts P-categories, introduces prodominical categories, and constructs recursion categories using local connectedness. | Heller (1990), printed p. 1253, §5 pp. 1260–1261, §8 p. 1264, Theorem 9.2 p. 1265; supplied PDF includes a cover sheet. | Confirmed. Theorem 9.2 assumes a locally connected iteration category and a uniformly generated isotypical B# subcategory. The manuscript does not reduce the theorem to local connectedness alone. |
| `appendices/b-three-point-cosheaves.tex`, frame terminology | A frame is established terminology in the cited construction. | Heller, §3, printed p. 1256; used again in Theorem 9.2 on p. 1265. | Confirmed. Heller defines a frame at an isotypical object using the product, coproduct, and formally free-semigroup isomorphisms and their inverses. The term is not invented by this manuscript. |
| `sections/01-introduction.tex:149–151` | Di Paola–Montagna’s syntactic examples motivate extending Heller beyond locally connected categories. | Di Paola–Montagna (1991), Introduction, printed p. 644; §4, especially Corollary 4.4 p. 657 and Theorem 4.5 p. 658. | Confirmed. The introduction explicitly makes that proposal. The source distinguishes the unprimed categories, which are not locally connected, from the primed construction’s conditional behavior. The manuscript does not rely here on validity of the primed construction. |
| `sections/01-introduction.tex:155–158` | Bernardi proves categorical fixed-point and inseparability results from precomplete equivalence relations, including PA-provable equality of partial recursive functions among the examples. | Bernardi (1986), §2 p. 125, §3 Corollary 1 p. 127 and Theorem 2 p. 128; the example is recalled on p. 127. | Confirmed. Minor pinpoint improvement: cite §§2–3, pp. 125–128 rather than §3 alone, because the PA example is first defined in §2. The inseparability theorem has additional `c+` hypotheses, which the manuscript does not deny. |
| `sections/01-introduction.tex:159–160` | Montagna obtains effectively inseparable pairs in his syntactic category. | Montagna (1989), Remark 3.1, printed p. 114; original PDF p. 10 of 12. | Confirmed. The remark derives the existence of such pairs from Proposition 1.1 and Di Paola–Heller Theorem 8.18. It also discusses whether pathological examples exist, which does not negate the existence assertion. |
| `sections/05-syntactic-recursion-category.tex:38,50,86`; Lemma 6.1 | Montagna supplies the arithmetic coding convention and uses formalized parameterization. | Montagna, printed p. 108 and Note 1, p. 115; original PDF pp. 4 and 11. Di Paola–Montagna p. 644 likewise specifies PR extensions and expanded formulas. | Confirmed at the stated source level. Page 108 identifies the universal evaluation morphism and invokes the formalized s-m-n theorem. Note 1 fixes the Kleene T and output predicates and expands the partial-function notation. These passages support the coding convention; every exact identity in Lemma 6.1 still requires the manuscript’s own proof or its indicated arithmetic argument. |
| `sections/02-categorical-preliminaries.tex:122–124`; introduction p-category comparison | Binary restriction products characterize P-category structures. | Cockett–Lack, *Restriction categories III*, Proposition 4.5; accessible author preprint pp. 20–21. | Confirmed. The proposition states the asserted equivalence. |
| `sections/02-categorical-preliminaries.tex:188–191` | A universal retract object with one self-application Turing morphism characterizes Turing categories. | Cockett–Hofstra (2008), Theorem 3.4, printed p. 189; journal PDF p. 7 of 27. | Confirmed. The theorem and preceding construction use total embedding/retraction pairs and the application morphism to recover the full Turing structure. |
| `sections/01-introduction.tex:168–170` | Cockett–Hofstra relate Turing categories and PCAs through idempotent splitting. | Cockett–Hofstra, Theorem 4.9, printed p. 200; journal PDF p. 18 of 27. | Confirmed. A PCA generates its computable-map Turing category; a Turing category is recovered by splitting a class of idempotents in the corresponding computable-map category. The cited theorem concerns an internal PCA; external-base classification is the later Theorem 4.12. The manuscript does not confuse these assertions. |
| `sections/01-introduction.tex:170–177` | Cockett’s incompleteness theorem assumes a predicate for program restriction order and concludes a nontrivial terminal-object restriction idempotent. | Cockett, *Categories and Computability* (12 August 2014), §7.5, printed pp. 92–94, Theorem 7.14 on p. 93. | Confirmed. The theorem also explicitly assumes a nontrivial Turing category and joins, or minimally a restriction zero. The introduction’s contrast with generated theories and all consistent extensions is accurate. |
| `sections/04-miller-sequences.tex:3–6,31–37` | Miller constructs a non-diagonalizable sequence and the stronger same-index property appears immediately after Theorem 6.3. | Miller (2004), Theorem 6.3 and following paragraph; author-hosted preprint pp. 12–13. | Confirmed. Miller uses metric-space representations; his binary representations are treated separately below that paragraph. The manuscript properly credits Bauer–Hanson for the binary-oracle formulation, rather than silently identifying the two representation systems. |
| `sections/04-miller-sequences.tex:3–6,31–37`; introduction `:179–182` | Bauer–Hanson use Miller’s non-diagonalizable sequence and binary representations; their topos has countable Dedekind reals. | Bauer–Hanson, *The countable reals*, arXiv:2404.01256v3 (1 April 2026), §2.1, Theorem 2.2, and Theorem 6.4. | Confirmed against exactly v3. Section 2.1 gives binary coding of reals and sequences and the uniform-index convention. Theorem 2.2 gives the Miller property; Theorem 6.4 gives an epimorphism from natural numbers to Dedekind reals. |

## Limits of the review

No scoped primary-source claim remains unverified. The Bernardi original was read through its author-uploaded full-text rendering, rather than a downloaded PDF. The Miller page references are to the author-hosted preprint, rather than the journal pagination.

No exhaustive novelty search, all-reference bibliography audit, or verification of the imported authors’ proofs was undertaken. In particular, the review does not convert a correct attribution into a claim that an old theorem proves a new manuscript result.
