### Continuous reading of the revised front matter

I read the assembled candidate preface and all of §1, including its four related-work groups and Table 1, continuously in the TeX source. The preface answers why the work was undertaken. Di Paola and Heller supplied inseparable computations; deriving incompleteness still required internal theories of which sentences could be provable or refutable. Heller's reason for seeking categories of different kinds explains why the book goes beyond arithmetic. The acknowledgments are identified separately.

The opening of §1 now starts with a mathematical question. A reader can run the proof search without any categorical vocabulary: each of the two sign requirements constrains an atom; consistency gives at most one sign, completeness at least one, and computable enumeration detects which. The categorical pair, internal theory and relation to the ordinary example follow. The subsections divide free generation, diagonal pairs and the associated category; the three applications and the table can be located without reading the whole introduction. Smullyan's abstract system no longer appears as an unexplained prerequisite. The literature comparison describes what its predicates, sentence instances, proof and refutation designations do before using either theorem. It distinguishes their hypotheses and effective coding.

Four local points were returned to the coordinator. ## First reports on v104

### Independent mathematical referee, first filing

**Scope and decision.** *Logical consequence and essential incompleteness in recursion categories*, v104, commit `a4a3a56e769139a1ec0d787d29647f0cfff72d65`. I read the complete preface (printed pp. 4–5), introduction and Table 1 (pp. 6–10), and the body passages identified below. I recommend major expository revision before release. The front matter fails to explain the route from computation to theories before it asks the reader to follow it. I found no counterexample to the main mathematical claim as stated. My report precedes receipt of the writing referee’s findings.

#### A. Hard blockers for the front matter

**M1. The opening assumes its only use of “representation system.”** On p. 6, introduction lines 3–16, the first sentence defines what it means for a predicate to strongly separate two sets, and the next defines “definable.” Neither supplies the meaning of the surrounding system. A search of the complete TeX finds the expression “representation system” only here. The first reference to an instance’s effective coding is at lines 10–13. The monograph’s own question begins at line 17. The arithmetical specialization in §8.1 shows how to make the relevant data concrete: a formula with one free variable, a numeral, its sentence instance, and proofs of that sentence or its negation. In the general historical setting the class of predicates, formation of instances, designations of provability/refutability, and coding assumption must be distinguished. Defining strong separation alone tells the reader none of that. **Failure:** missing definition, absence in text, major and repairable.

**Repair.** Start with the question about generating a theory from computation. Explain why a separator would matter by a small case in arithmetic or in propositional logic, before writing the categorical theorem. For example:

> Given two disjoint computably enumerable sets $A,B$, take propositional atoms $p_n$ and assert $p_n$ for $n\in A$ and $\neg p_n$ for $n\in B$. In a complete consistent extension whose theorems can be enumerated, search for $p_n$ and $\neg p_n$ until one appears. This would decide a set containing $A$ and disjoint from $B$. What takes the place of that separation when the starting point is a recursion category?

This is a motivating example, independent of historical attribution. The recursive set is exactly the set of $n$ for which $p_n$ occurs in the enumeration. Explain next how the paper constructs a first-order theory internally and how complementary subobjects express the corresponding separation. Move the two historical statements together, keeping their different hypotheses and attributions. If the general expression “representation system” survives there, first introduce the precise data Smullyan uses from the book. The arithmetic example suffices for motivation if those data cannot be verified. Do not replace the generality of Theorem 22 by an implicit claim that it assumes arithmetic.

#### B. Major weaknesses

**M2. Several paragraphs of the preface do the introduction’s or the proofs’ work.** The preface, pp. 4–5, lines 17–39, 41–55 and 74–77, gives the full assignment property, both directions of the free-theory criterion, an arithmetic proof including the map to a coproduct and a lifted conditional, and the two extension adjunctions. Compare introduction lines 50–122, 168–205, and §8.1, Lemma 8.1 and Corollary 8.4. The reader twice receives the formal setup before learning where its details will be needed. The extended numerical reconstruction was a repair to an underexplained argument, and should not be shortened into a false assertion that the comparison theorem alone gives numerical inseparability. The preferable cut removes the proof from the preface; §8 already gives its ingredients. Keep the historical aim, what the construction adds, why Heller wanted examples apart from ordinary partial recursion, and the requested credit; a brief mathematically accurate sentence can retain the initiality or its result. Give the registered-source detail in Appendix E or together with the existing pointer to it. **Evidence:** structural argument and paired passages, major expository overlap. The number of pages by itself establishes no defect.

**M3. Main results, proofs and prerequisites have no visible hierarchy.** In §1, the first internal-theory criterion at lines 17–21 precedes the ambient hypotheses (23–35, 50–56) and the definition of internal inseparability (36–38). Lines 124–166 then introduce the symbols of three comparison results consecutively; the reader meets $\varepsilon, h,P,N,\chi,s,\ell,g,\widehat F,d,\theta$ without first being told the mathematical problem solved by lifting a decision. In print all this falls between the unheaded “Introduction” and “Related work.” Put the problem and meaning of a separator first, distinguish the construction from one source of examples, and group the results by question. Keep the comparison hypotheses in §1 as previously required. A named or clearly signposted paragraph can contain the full conditions after one sentence explaining why existence of a Turing morphism alone does not imply inseparability. Appendix C supplies the counterexample. **Failure:** ambiguous logical dependencies, structural argument, major editorial rather than proof failure.

#### C. Minor issues

**M4. “There is one morphism” can suggest uniqueness.** Introduction p. 8, lines 197–202. Theorem 8.6 states that a total $q$ supplies an independent sentence for every code $e$ of a consistent extension. It asserts existence uniformly in $e$. In fact the composite of $q$ with sentence negation has the same independence property: a classical theory proves neither $\varphi$ nor $\neg\varphi$ if and only if it proves neither $\neg\varphi$ nor $\neg\neg\varphi$. The numeral codes of a formula and its negation are distinct. Thus the alternative uniqueness reading is false; the intended reading is correct. Write “There is a morphism $q$ … such that, for every $e$ with $\Gamma_e$ consistent, …”. **Evidence:** derivation. Minor local clarification.

**M5. Related work offers no navigational distinction between four questions.** At pp. 9–10, lines 298–430, contributions to the construction of recursion categories, categorical diagonal pairs, effective signed theories, and other categorical incompleteness theorems all occupy a single subsection. The passage at lines 312–338 even proves the nontriviality condition in an antecedent. Subdivide by the question each group of authors answers. Place that proof with the diagonal comparison in §§2–3, preserving the assumptions including $1_X\ne0_X$, and leave the historical statement and cross-reference here. Keep separate the precomplete-relation results of Bernardi and the internal theory produced by this paper. The comparison with arithmetic universes, guarded fixed points, and categorical provability concerns genuinely different conclusions, not interchangeable versions of Corollary 4.8. **Evidence:** source locations and statement comparison. This change assists navigation; there is no evidence that one heading per author is necessary.

## Exchange of objections

### Mathematical referee’s challenge to the writing report

**v104, after exchange of the independent filings.** The writing report correctly identifies the first and only use of “representation system.” Its proposed order retains the standing hypotheses, free theory, direction of transfer, conditional comparison theorem, and all three different application statements. It introduces no counterexample to the mathematics.

**The proposed first paragraph needs one more connection.** After defining recursive inseparability for two c.e. sets, it immediately switches to internal theories. A first-time reader can now tell what a separator is, but still cannot tell why a *completion of a theory* would produce one. Insert the finite explanation from the arithmetic or propositional example: use an atom $p_n$ for each natural number, assert or refute it on the respective sets, and observe that in a complete consistent extension with enumerable theorems, enumerating proofs decides the sign of every $p_n$. The positive signs therefore give a recursive separator. Consistency gives uniqueness of the sign; completeness guarantees termination; membership on the two original sets is forced by the chosen axioms. In the internal conclusion replace this argument by the pullback of complementary theorem and refutation subobjects. This is the motivation requested, and it concerns generation of theories rather than an abstract system that is used only in the historical comparison.

**A restriction on moving the Di Paola–Heller proof.** Related work can carry the author's old assumptions and the theorem, with a pointer to the proof. If their proof moves to §3, clearly state its *own* hypotheses there. In particular, their displayed use of a section for every partial morphism cannot simply be inherited from this paper's weaker comparison-theorem data. The explicit qualification $1_X\ne0_X$ remains necessary. The assignment on all of $X$ should survive every draft of the universal property.

**Historical link and headings.** The report's option of retaining the original generality while illustrating only the arithmetic case is consistent. It requires distinguishing an arithmetical illustration from the exact definition of Smullyan's general ambient structure. Headings for construction of recursion categories, syntactic examples, diagonal inseparability, and theories prescribed by two signs can be ordered to keep neighboring authors' precise contributions visible. A syntactic choice between four headings and five has no bearing on validity.

**Minor wording objection independently confirmed.** The opening paragraph's “disjoint pair of restriction idempotents specifies which propositional atoms to assert and which to refute” uses $X$ implicitly: on a first reading the association of a pair with a *common* object of generators needs to be said once. This need not introduce $\theta_X$ before the example. In one phrase, “on the object of propositional atoms” makes the association intelligible when the construction is described. A total assignment on the whole object then becomes natural.

### Writing referee's challenge to the mathematical first filing

**M1 survives.** §1:3–7 explains a predicate property; the container in “In a representation system” remains unexplained. The mathematical referee's example does something my sample paragraph could do better. The parallel searches for opposite signs demonstrate why an inseparable pair has anything to do with an incomplete theory. This is a sound motivation precisely because the extension's theorems are enumerable and the extension is both complete and consistent. Name one atom for *each natural number*, and state that the extension is of the displayed signed theory in its language. This gives a usable example without naming an abstract representation system in the opening. It does not on its own identify all general representation systems with effective arithmetic theories.

**M2–M3 survive.** I checked the two long passages on p. 4 against §§1 and 8.1. Removing the preface's arithmetic proof preserves each mathematical ingredient elsewhere. Retain an explanation of why the two examples beyond arithmetic differ from computation with partial recursive functions, as requested by the author. State the internal graph of implications in the order in which it is used: free construction, transferred inseparability of every consistent extension, Boolean converse for the free theory, and then sufficient conditions supplying a diagonal pair. A reference to Appendix C can explain the need for the lifting condition; the entire counterexample need not be retold in §1.

**M4 survives as a genuine reading ambiguity.** Theorem 8.6 claims the existence of a total morphism. In a classical arithmetic theory the negation of any sentence independent of the theory is independent as well. “There is one morphism” is therefore better expressed as “There is a total morphism $q$ … for every $e$ …”. “A single morphism works for every $e$” would also be intelligible, but the explicitly quantified form costs little.

**M5 survives with an editorial qualification.** Group connected authors, and let the question guide the division; the author did not require a separate heading for every individual. A body relocation of the historical diagonal argument should keep the explicit conditions visible in the related-work summary, including nontriviality. Retain the qualification on the last novelty statement.

**Mathematical clarity check.** The distinction between a proper internal join and an undecided global point survives the suggested motivating example. Restrict the dovetailing narrative to the external enumerable example; distinguish the categorical criterion and the explicit endpoint statement from that construction. I have no challenge to the examples or the selected theorem statements within the writing scope.
