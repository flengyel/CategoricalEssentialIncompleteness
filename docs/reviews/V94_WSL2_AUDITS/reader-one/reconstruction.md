## Argument and notation in reading order

The abstract and §1 ask which signed disjoint pairs produce essentially incomplete internal theories. Essential incompleteness quantifies over consistent closed extensions in the same language. The equivalence assumes consistency of the generated theory. The forward reduction for an extension only asks for a total sentence assignment with two bounds. A Boolean separator gives the reverse construction for the free language. The three realizations have different languages, notions of effectiveness and means of establishing the existence of witnesses.

Sections 1–2 place these claims in the restriction idempotent completion of a recursion category. Read composition from right to left. Inverse image takes a domain to the restriction of its composite with the map. It preserves meets, zeros and, for total maps, top. For images one also assumes agreement on the range. The range of a copairing computes the join of domains. A range functor here preserves zero and binary restriction coproducts, in addition to restriction and range. The doubling functor on partial functions demonstrates that this does not imply product preservation. Split and Karoubi objects have different totality equations for general idempotents. Section 1.1 gives historical results on computation and incompleteness to compare with the conditional and unconditional implications proved here. I read its original DPH argument, including its hypotheses on sections, coproducts of total maps and distributivity, as a separate attribution requiring source passage checking.

Sections 3–4 move from a lifted conditional for a Turing diagonal to interpreted and source incompleteness, then move from the initial proof algebra through closure, translation, and the free adjunction. A proper theorem/refutation join in a general category need not omit a *global* element, as the fixed-point-free summand of a group action shows. Section 5 realizes the equivalent partial-map presentation by monic spans; a graph embedding supplies the slice retraction into a constant Turing family. Its pullback adjunctions are in the ordinary partial categories. The unit of the right-hand adjunction may be partial. The numerical example constructs an actual computable bijection between an infinite c.e. carrier and ℕ via the first halting stage.

Section 6 uses PA-provable uniform graph equivalence for the category S_T. Formal products and tagged coproducts have unique set presentations with the specified set conventions; primed pointwise quotients are separate and have a cited failure of composition. The numerical graph conventions give restrictions, ranges, congruence for composition and the Turing factorization. The finite-membership result scopes the color predicate by a finite syntactic disjunction. Syntax, lists, substitution and derivations use different folds. Section 7 distinguishes an arithmetic code model from the arbitrary model of the axiom set used to validate standard *decoded* derivations. An ultrapower transfers the validity subobject via a natural comparison in Set, rather than interpreting a satisfaction predicate for all nonstandard formula codes.

Section 8 proceeds through a fixed syntactic category, a theory-indexed axiom and derivation family, one independent-sentence morphism, the equivalent numerical total/partial categories and the comparison of proof-code carriers. The counit of this numerical equivalence involves actual domain and value equality, including isomorphism of span middles. Relabelling every rule instance with a computable inverse identifies the numerical and interpreted arithmetic derivations. Finite trace verification and extension of axioms give the sign bounds. The final signed theory of the section has only nullary nonlogical predicates, unlike the earlier arithmetic theory. Its internal consistency is proved using one finite support for both derivations.

Section 9 first requires a sequence a at whose index d lies the real computed uniformly by d from all names of a. Unanimity including definedness gives the Turing morphism in the uniform representation category. A single first-digit program gives a name-dependent separator. The cut reduction obstructs a single decidable set uniformly separating the cuts. Strict rational bounds give an actual consistent field theory witnessed by the expanded real field, with the required semidecision and slice behavior. Section 10 distinguishes pointwise interpretation at the two endpoints from interpretation at the central fork object. Inseparability at endpoints is reproved there from their Turing morphisms. The conservative extension uses a global atom to erase equality and quantification, giving both internal and endpoint conclusions.

Appendix A fixes the two truth-value injections, variable comparison, occurrences, binding, admissibility, initial algebras, finite-arity rule endofunctor, formula closure and its sentence pullback. A zero unary rule imposes a vacuous closure operation even in the general regular/finite-coproduct propositional setting. Appendix B constructs the three-point example, including pushout of a fork at the entire space, the failure of products/monos at that value, the connected probes and natural specialized evaluator. Appendix C explicitly disproves the earlier lifting implication and gives disjoint morphisms without a common upper bound. Appendix D treats the joint family of countable models, extension quotients, failure of the family evaluator to be Turing by anti-diagonalization, standard-numeral observations and the unprimed true-arithmetic category. Appendix E explains seven groups of deductions: the two inverse translation assignments, Boolean separators, joint finite support, lifted conditional, slice evaluation, uniform choice and the identification of Miller finite proofs with the theorem range for arbitrary axiom monos. I treated its printed names as manuscript content and did not follow its code or registry links before sealing.

The displayed definitions introduce composition, idempotent order, totality, range and a Turing morphism in §2. Sentence/refutation domains are introduced at the start of §4. The named-variable convention is defined in A.1–A.3 before it is used to derive the seven rule schemes in A.5. Key checks of logical direction include the second summand for a *positive* conditional, the inverse image of theorem under syntactic negation for refutability, regular image after restriction to sentence conclusions, and the distinct stages of the universal quantifier's capture test. A blanket negative historical statement in the abstract deserves a bibliographical check distinct from the mathematical assertions.
