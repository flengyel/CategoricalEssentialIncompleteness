## Abstract and reading order

The problem is to compare internal logical consequence with a disjoint pair of partial identities. A theory is a subobject of sentences closed under finite deductions, consistency means disjointness from the inverse image under negation, and essential incompleteness ranges over every larger consistent closed subobject in the same language. For the free signed theory the equivalence requires consistency of the generated theory. Its universal property is a natural bijection between signed assignments and theory translations. A range functor comparison requires a lift of a reversed conditional. The examples supply actual categorical data for arithmetic, Miller sequences and a finite fork, then pass to partial slices over theorems.

1. **Introduction and related work.** In a split restriction category with regular extensive total part and partial inverses for its monos, a subobject corresponds to its split partial identity. Signed atomic axioms are the join of two images, one after negation. A source reduction pulls back any proposed target complementary separator. A separating partition produces an ordinary Boolean valuation in the category. Taking a constant family over the theorem object and evaluating at a global logical theorem relates arbitrarily parameter-dependent extensions to extensions in the base. The target of a range functor need not preserve products. The target of an arithmetic interpretation of codes and the separate model K of the axioms play different roles. The uniform sentence selector is a single morphism independent of the extension index. At a Miller dyadic equality the individual oracle may choose its own separator. At the fork an endpoint is an actual numerical example, and the ambient recursion category differs from Rec. Di Paola–Heller’s original argument also uses distribution, total sums and sections. From g* c=g*d=0, agreement with range of g and the range of [c,d] gives g=0, contradicting totality if id≠0. The bibliography distinguishes this result, Smullyan’s two numerical criteria, precompleteness, provability logic, arithmetic universes, nullary theories, existence of recursion categories, and Turing categories from the argument here.
