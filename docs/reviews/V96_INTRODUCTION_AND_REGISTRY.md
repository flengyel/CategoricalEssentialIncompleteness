# Review of the Smullyan attribution, the free theory, and Appendix E

The v95 review correctly identified the missing conclusion of Corollary 4.3. It overlooked the scope of Smullyan’s Theorem 22 and overstated the objection to “translation” in the abstract. Its discussion of Appendix E also missed a citation to an unregistered version. The manuscript corrections are in §1, §4 and Appendix E of v96. [Earlier review](V95_ABSTRACT_AND_INTRODUCTION.md) · [Introduction](../../manuscript/latex/sections/01-introduction.tex) · [Appendix E](../../manuscript/latex/appendices/e-reading-lean-proofs.tex)

### What Smullyan proved

Di Paola and Heller introduce a consistent arithmetical system on p. 594 and give statement (3) on p. 595. That statement specializes Smullyan’s Theorem 22. His setting is a representation system. Chapter III, §8 assumes that the code of a predicate instance is computable from the predicate code and its argument. In §16 the printed hypothesis of Theorem 22 is that every recursive set is definable. Under that hypothesis no recursive set separates the provable sentence codes from the refutable sentence codes. In a consistent system these sets are disjoint. Theorem 23(b) explicitly assumes consistency and strong separation of one recursively inseparable pair by a predicate. Its instances are provable on the first set and refutable on the second. [Di Paola and Heller, pp. 594–595](https://doi.org/10.2307/2274352) · [Smullyan, definition on p. 48](https://archive.org/details/theoryofformalsy0000smul/page/48/mode/1up) · [Coding on p. 52](https://archive.org/details/theoryofformalsy0000smul/page/52/mode/1up) · [Theorems 22 and 23(b), pp. 59–60](https://archive.org/details/theoryofformalsy0000raym/page/59/mode/2up)

### What “translation” means in the abstract

The abstract says “translations from $T_{a,b}$ into a theory $T$” and states the two signed bounds in that sentence. The v95 referee’s example of a logical translation that fails to preserve an axiom distinguishes two notions defined in the body. It gives no counterexample to the abstract’s usage for a translation between theories. Section 4 now names this convention where it defines a morphism $(P,T)\to(Q,U)$. The approved arXiv text has 1,917 characters. Replacing both occurrences by “theory morphism(s)” would give 1,925. [Abstract](../../manuscript/arxiv/abstract.txt) · [Definition and Theorem 4.7](../../manuscript/latex/sections/03b-internal-adjunctions.tex)

Section 1 states that every total assignment $f:X\to S_Q$ extends uniquely to a logical translation out of $P_X$. It is a morphism from the generated theory to a consistent theory $U$ precisely when $a\le f^*U$ and $b\le f^*\nu_Q^*U$. If the source pair is inseparable and $H\ge U$ is any consistent closed extension, any complementary separator of $H,\nu_Q^*H$ would pull back to one of $a,b$. Thus $H,\nu_Q^*H$ are inseparable. The converse for the free theory uses a Boolean valuation. [Corollary 4.3](../../manuscript/latex/sections/03a-internal-theories.tex#L213-L235) · [Free theory and Boolean valuation](../../manuscript/latex/sections/03b-internal-adjunctions.tex)

The arithmetic summary states consistency of PA when it introduces $t_\pm$ and cites Lemma 8.9. The cosheaf summary gives an undecided propositional atom for every consistent c.e.-axiomatized extension at either endpoint. Section 1.1 cites Shoenfield’s Theorem 2 for the signed construction and the separator obtained from a decidable extension. It cites Murwanashyaka, Pakhomov and Visser for the equivalence with essential undecidability. In their construction a set-theoretic separator gives a complete consistent extension, which is c.e. exactly when that separator is recursive. [Shoenfield, pp. 390–391](https://doi.org/10.2307/2964013) · [MPV, §4.4, p. 1169](https://research-portal.uu.nl/ws/files/240671111/exad005.pdf#page=12)
