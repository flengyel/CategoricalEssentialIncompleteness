# Sentence and definition review of manuscript v78

The review found an incorrect substitution case, a missing comparison of slice algebras, and an ambiguous reference to a category. The oracle-computability criterion in §1 belongs to the recursion category of a Miller sequence. The substitution argument in Theorem 4.7 distinguishes nullary predicates from equality atoms. The proofs below identify what each correction requires.

## A. Effect on the results

**Theorem 4.7, v77 p. 18.** The proof said that substitution fixes the interpretation of an atom. In the free syntax take distinct variables $x,y$. Substitution of $y$ for $x$ sends the equality atom $x=x$ to $y=y$. These are distinct formulas of the syntax. The absent-variable identity does apply to the *sentence* interpreting a nullary predicate. At an equality atom the interpretation instead commutes with substitution on each of its two terms. These are the two induction cases required by the free first-order adjunction. [Previous proof](https://github.com/flengyel/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/blob/9b07c34a32ec47757c8ba3bd5c7446d01ee907fb/manuscript/latex/sections/03b-internal-adjunctions.tex#L408) · [Revised proof](../../manuscript/latex/sections/03b-internal-adjunctions.tex)

**Corollary 8.10, v77 p. 59.** The proof attributed derivation algebras in every parameter slice to the construction of §6. An axiom subobject $A\hookrightarrow P\times S^{\pm}$ can vary with $P$. Pulling back the algebra for one axiom object over the terminal object only handles constant families. Write $F=P\times\mathsf{Fm}^{\pm}$. To give an object over $F\to P$ in the slice is to give $q:Q\to F$, since its map to $P$ is forced. If $q d_j=p_{nj}b$ for every premise of a rule, composition with $F\to P$ shows that all the premises have the parameter of the same rule label $b$. For arity zero both constructions give the rule object itself, including the arbitrary axioms $A$. These identifications give the same inference functor, up to natural isomorphism, so Lemma 6.8 supplies the required initial algebra. The existing Lean declaration also quantifies over the rule family and target algebra. ## C. Economy of the proofs

In §1 of *The Maximality of Cartesian Categories*, Došen and Petrić give the types of the projections and of pairing before using their equations. In §5 they close the maximality argument by applying those equations to two arbitrary parallel morphisms. Two comparable edits here are the formula $f+g=[i_0 f,i_1 g]$ before the summand idempotents in §3 and the two substitution cases in Theorem 4.7. Knuth, Larrabee and Roberts, §1, items 10–11 and 17, discuss connecting formulas with prose, identifying variables on introduction, and avoiding ambiguous readings. An example of useful economy in Appendix A is the removal of a second Boolean table while retaining the joint-epimorphy argument that proves the equations. [Došen–Petrić, §§1 and 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9911059) · [Knuth–Larrabee–Roberts, §1](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/papers/cs1193.pdf)

Appendix E contained Lean tactic transcripts. For instance, the line about `rw [h] at hq` in E.3 described an edit to a local hypothesis. The revised proof uses the mathematical content: the conclusions of the two derivations are $\varphi$ and $\neg\varphi$, and the common valuation cannot make both true. E.6 distinguishes choosing the single sentence-valued morphism from applying independence at a consistent axiom index. Named declarations, the seven subsections, and the proof and registry links remain available to readers. The source archive's README now contains the build and file guide without the old revision diary. ## D. Definition order across sections

The abstract introduces the internal theory $T$, the indexing morphism $\theta_X:X\to S_X$, and the meaning of essential incompleteness. Section 1 explains what it means for an index $x:W\to X$ to lie in a domain. Section 2 gives the order on restriction idempotents and the conditions for complementarity and inseparability. Section 4 defines deductive closure at the first quantification over closed extensions. Section 5 identifies the object of the slice and the equivalence relation on its partial-map spans. Sections 6–8 fix the arithmetic language before using its numeral codes. In §§9–10 the oracle representation and the endpoints of the cosheaf diagram each enter where the corresponding theory is constructed.
