**B.1–B.2.** A cover of a nonempty proper open contains that open. A cover of the whole space that omits the whole space contains the two maximal proper opens. Consequently, its compatible cocones are exactly the pairs agreeing on their intersection. The pushout supplies the value of the extended diagram on the whole space, and uniqueness supplies functoriality. Finite paths in the enumerated symmetric generating relation give the equivalence relation on the two tagged carriers. For the diagram with empty centre and two singleton endpoints, the pushout has two elements, including after the diagram is squared. Squaring its whole-space value gives four. Its monomorphism to the terminal diagram induces the fold from two elements to one. For regularity, componentwise kernel-pair descent is natural because the quotient at the centre is surjective. Component images are c.e. and stable under transitions. The three finite test diagrams detect injections, and inverse images of the two summands decompose each morphism to a coproduct. The numerical diagram category itself has the regularity and extensivity instances, and the component image factorization is constructed in its equivalent total-fork presentation. **B.3–B.4.** A partial morphism is determined by its c.e. domain subfunctor and its component functions there. Pullback composition gives the stated intersection with the inverse image of the second domain. The image subfunctor is the least restriction idempotent fixing the morphism. Testing two composites on a preimage proves agreement of both domains and values on the image. Taking inverse images distributes over unions and intersections, and taking images commutes with products. For weak choice, two test morphisms through the domain with the same component image agree after composition with the partial monomorphism at every component, by naturality. Partial monicity then identifies the test morphisms, so each component map is injective on its domain. Preimage search on its image gives the inverse, which commutes with the transitions by injectivity. A singleton at the centre together with its two images is connected. Those subfunctors and the endpoint singletons detect both the domain and all values of an arbitrary partial morphism. **B.5–B.7.** At the centre the embedding records the centre code and the codes of its two images. Projecting to either endpoint recovers its embedding there. Coordinatewise pairing commutes with projection to an endpoint. For the coproduct frame the shared first coordinate determines the parity tag. For the word frame it determines the word length and the sequence of first coordinates. Each remaining coordinate is decoded using that same length. For the evaluator, halting and agreement of all three centre computations imply halting at both endpoints and naturality on that domain. Given an arbitrary partial span, specialization is applied to computations whose domains are exactly its three component domains. On the centre domain, naturality of its value morphism makes both comparisons succeed. Conversely, the centre computation can halt only there. At each endpoint equation (33) ignores the first coordinate of the program parameter and gives equality of partial domains as well as values. In Lean the two graph equivalences quantify over every input and output, and the parameter theorem quantifies over every numerical diagram and every such partial span. **B.8–B.10.** The frame gives isotypy by induction, and fullness retains the parameter morphisms and the same ranges. Restriction idempotents split through their domains. For an arbitrary partial idempotent, equality of the domains of its square and itself puts every image point back in its domain, and equality of values fixes every image point. The image therefore supplies its ordinary splitting. For a morphism supported by idempotents on its source and target, the Karoubi restriction is $\bar f e$, with $e$ the source idempotent; on the source image this yields the stated restriction of the realized morphism. Dominance gives essential surjectivity. The terminal fork has four subobjects with empty centre and one with inhabited centre; the numerical terminal singleton has two. These counts distinguish the restriction categories after passing to splittings. Two distinct centre points differing only in the coordinate forgotten by the transitions disprove a natural classifier of the diagonal: they coincide at an endpoint, and the transition on truth values is the identity.
