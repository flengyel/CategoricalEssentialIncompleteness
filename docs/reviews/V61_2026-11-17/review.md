# Review of the revised literature comparisons, abstract and introduction

*Logical consequence and essential incompleteness in recursion categories*, version 61, 17 November 2026. Page numbers refer to the [compiled manuscript](../../../manuscript/logical_consequence_and_essential_incompleteness_v61.pdf).

## (A) Kill-shots / hard blockers

The revised passages support the stated contributions. The opening identifies the theory generated by a disjoint pair and the effect of passage to its constant family. The arithmetic, Miller and cosheaf paragraphs specify which extensions they cover. No claim of historical priority is needed.

## (B) Major weaknesses

The [v60 report](../V60_2026-11-17/literature-comparisons.md) identified missing antecedents in §§4 and 8. The revised comparisons resolve those objections.

| Location | Comparison checked against the sources and the manuscript |
|---|---|
| §1.1, p. 3 | Murwanashyaka, Pakhomov and Visser use sentences asserting the existence of equivalence classes of prescribed sizes. Section 4.6 constructs the internal theory on nullary predicates and proves its universal property. Cockett attributes his Theorem 7.8 to Lengyel. His Theorem 7.14 produces a nontrivial restriction idempotent on the terminal object, whereas Theorem 5.3 concerns extensions of a theory on a specified sentence object in a slice. |
| Corollary 4.3, p. 10 | Visser’s Lemma 23 yields essential undecidability from a recursive reduction. Kirst and Peters obtain an independent sentence for each consistent extension with semidecidable provability. In Corollary 4.3 the theorem and refutation domains remain inseparable. Their join is proper. Properness alone need not produce a global element outside the join. |

| Theorem 8.6, p. 41 | Pour-El’s effective extensibility concerns finding an independent sentence from an index for additional axioms of a consistent extension. In the manuscript the Turing morphism gives one total morphism whose standard interpretation computes those sentences. |

## (C) Minor issues

The editorial check began from the [section outline](../V60_2026-11-17/outline.md) and compared the opening with the proofs it announces. The abstract now specifies the strict rational bounds that axiomatize the Miller field theory. Adjoining constant symbols alone would leave their values unconstrained. The introduction defines inseparability, consistency and completeness before explaining the two directions of Corollary 4.8. The Miller application and its attribution appear together. Removing the closing list of sections eliminates a second account of the same results.

## (D) Cross-section consistency failures

Three checks determine the scope of the revised opening. Theorem 5.3 quantifies over *all* consistent closed extensions of the constant family. In Corollary 9.4 the separator computed from a fixed representation may depend on that representation. The endpoint assertion in Corollary 10.6 allows any consistent c.e.-axiomatized extension in the language of the chosen endpoint. The abstract and introduction preserve these distinctions.

## (E) Acceptance conditions, non-negotiable

The four local comparisons identify the predecessor and the difference in hypotheses or conclusion. The abstract identifies both categorical results and the hypotheses relevant to the three applications. The 77 numbered statements, their 77 proofs and the three numbered definitions agree byte for byte with the v60 source archive. The revised TeX compiles with all citations and cross-references resolved.
