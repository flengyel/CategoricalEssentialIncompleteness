## The right unit for an arbitrary slice object

At `manuscript/latex/sections/04-theory-extensions.tex:192–199,219–226`, let $i:T\hookrightarrow U$ and $q:Y\to U$ in the total category $D$. Write $P=Y\times_U T$, with projections $p:P\to Y$ and $k:P\to T$; thus $qp=ik$. These factors are reversed relative to the order of the two factors in the printed pullback, in agreement with the Lean source. Postcomposing the $T$-family $k$ gives $ik:P\to U$. Write $m:(P,ik)\to(Y,q)$ for $p$ as a morphism in $D/U$. The component at $q$ of the unit of $i^*\dashv\Sigma_i$ is the partial arrow $[(Y,q)\leftarrow^m(P,ik)\to^1(P,ik)]$. The asserted equivalence applies to each $q$, including ones which are neither terminal nor themselves monomorphisms.

1. A monic span $[X\leftarrow^l A\to^h Y']$ is total if and only if $l$ is invertible. Totality gives $[X\leftarrow^l A\to^l X]=[X\leftarrow^1 X\to^1 X]$. Equality of these spans in the quotient supplies an isomorphism of apices and its first leg equation identifies that isomorphism with $l$. Conversely an inverse for $l$ identifies the original span with the total span for $hl^{-1}$. This works in any category with pullbacks, in particular $D/U$. Apply it with $l=m,h=1$.
2. If $m$ is invertible over $U$, its underlying arrow $p$ is invertible. The required factorization is $r=kp^{-1}:Y\to T$. Then $ir=ikp^{-1}=qpp^{-1}=q$.
3. Conversely suppose $r:Y\to T$ satisfies $ir=q$. The pullback produces $j=\langle1_Y,r\rangle:Y\to P$, and $pj=1_Y$. As $p$ is the pullback of the mono $i$, it is monic, hence $jp=1_P$. In particular $j$ commutes with the structure maps to $U$ and is the inverse of $m$ in the slice. When $q=1_U$, this gives the familiar section of $i$; the proof works for arbitrary $q$.

## Appendix A.6 without extensivity

At `manuscript/latex/appendices/a-syntax-and-theories.tex:599–623`, assume $D$ regular with finite coproducts and a fixed initial algebra for $G(Z)=V+Z+Z^2$, with carrier $S$ and constructors $\theta,\nu,I$. Fix $i_A:A\hookrightarrow S$. There is a second, logically distinct initiality assumption on the inference endofunctor. Neither is an existence theorem for every regular category. There is no need for complemented subobjects, disjoint sums, a strict initial object, or pullback-stable sums in this subsection.

**The exact rule instance.** Write $L=(S^2+S^3)+S^2$ and let $\lambda:L\to S$ copair the three printed schemes. Given the finite products and three scheme morphisms, finite coproducts alone form this map. Form a `RulePresentation S 2` at `V46Inference.lean:124–127` with the following operations. The first coordinate of each row below is the actual arity $n$.

| $n$ | Instance object | Premise map | Conclusion map |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | $L+A$ | $!:L+A\to1$ | $[\lambda,i_A]$ |
| 1 | $0$ | $!:0\to S$ | the same $!:0\to S$ |
| 2 | $S\times S$ | $\langle\pi_1,I\rangle:S^2\to S^2$ | $\pi_2:S^2\to S$ |

In code, the nullary arrow is `coprod.desc (coprod.desc (coprod.desc (ErasureLogicalSchemes.one I) (ErasureLogicalSchemes.two I)) (ErasureLogicalSchemes.three ν I)) i_A`. The three scheme morphisms are already given by explicit pairings in `V46ErasureLogicalSchemes.lean:22–36`. Independent evidence that the individual expressions can be used without an extensive instance is `V46PropositionalSchemeTransport.lean:20–22,44–60`: its statements apply those three declarations over categories required only to be regular, with $K$ preserving finite products. No transport functor is needed to construct the three morphisms in the first place. The three rows above can be packaged by the same `Fin.cases` expressions as `V46ForkDerivationInference.lean:31–36`, using the supplied `[HasFiniteCoproducts D]` to form the initial object and coproducts. The specialized `ForkInference.rules` and `PropositionalRuleComparison.rules` are written in a section using `[FinitaryExtensive D]`; invoking their prepackaged form is distinct from applying the generic theorem with these weak data.

**Closure, including the unary projection.** For $m:M\hookrightarrow S$, let $B_n(M)=R_n\times_{S^n}M^n$, with first and second projections $a_n,b_n$. By the universal property of the coproduct, an algebra over the conclusion inclusion amounts to arrows $u_n:B_n(M)\to M$ with $mu_n=q_na_n$. Each is unique if it exists because $m$ is monic.

- For the nullary component, $B_0(M)\cong L+A$, since both zero powers are terminal. Thus a component is exactly a factorization of $[\lambda,i_A]$ through $m$, or equivalently separate factorizations of $\lambda$ and $i_A$. Lean's `R.nullaryRule (Over.mk m)` and its first-projection identity are in `V46Inference.lean:152–157`; `R.closed_contains_nullary m α` at lines 238–244 extracts the factor from any algebra arrow $α$.
- For the unary component define $u_1=b_1$. Then $mb_1=p_1a_1=q_1a_1$. In Lean, with `Q := Over.mk m`, this is `(pullback.condition (f := initial.to S) (g := m)).symm`, typed `pullback.snd (initial.to S) m ≫ m = pullback.fst (initial.to S) m ≫ initial.to S`. The proof applies to every object $q:Q\to S$, including nonmonos. There is no reason for $B_1(Q)$ itself to be initial.
- The binary component is precisely closure under modus ponens: on the matching premise pullback the two proofs conclude $\varphi$ and $I(\varphi,\psi)$; the result must conclude $\psi$, the projected second formula of the rule instance. This is the factorization of $\pi_2\circ a_2$. The typed morphism is `pullback.fst (prod.lift prod.fst I) (prod.map m m) ≫ prod.snd`. Given a modus-ponens operation and the nullary factor, `Sigma.desc` of these and the unary projection forms the required algebra arrow. Conversely restrictions of any such arrow to the nullary and binary summands recover both properties.

**Initial conclusion image.** For the inferred rule record $R$, `R.inference` is defined under `[Regular D] [HasFiniteCoproducts D]` (`V46Inference.lean:206–229`). Given `P : Endofunctor.Algebra R.inference` and `hP : IsInitial P`, the exact application is
`R.least_theorem_subobject P hP` (`V46Inference.lean:247–252`). It yields `ClosedUnder R.inference (image.ι P.a.hom)` and a factor of that mono through every mono $m$ satisfying `ClosedUnder R.inference m`; the preceding equivalence identifies it with containment of the axioms and closure under modus ponens. Preservation of regular epis is proved for the generic rule functor: finite power maps preserve them by stable pullbacks and composition (lines 69–101), the premise maps between rule objects are base changes (178–204), and finite coproducts preserve regular epis by componentwise diagonal lifting and the universal property of the coproduct (103–120). Applying these steps at $N=2$ uses no strictness of 0. The regular-epi/mono diagonal in `V46RegularImage.lean:48–63` equips the image mono with an algebra; initiality yields the factorization into every closed mono at lines 77–90. This gives the full image-closure argument without a set-theoretic union or any exactness assumption on coproduct injections.

**Signed axioms and the partial identity.** Let $g=[\theta i_+,\nu\theta i_-]:A_++A_-\to S$. Factor $g=m e$ by the regular image and take $i_A=m$. For any $x:Z\to A_-$, the actual axiom witness in $\operatorname{im}g$ is $e\,\iota_- x$. Its value under $m$ equals $g\iota_-x=\nu\theta i_-x$. In Lean composition order, `x ≫ coprod.inr ≫ factorThruImage g` has the required codomain, and `image.fac g` followed by `coprod.inr_desc` computes its image. This construction works regardless of whether the signed images have common parts, and does not require a set-theoretic interpretation of them. The partial identity of the generated theory is the mono span $[S\gets M\to S]$ with both legs its image mono; `V46PartialMaps.lean:73,102–104,255–264` identifies it as the restriction of that mono's canonical partial inverse.

**Test of the strictly stronger shortcut.** In the regular category of pointed sets, finite coproducts are wedges and the initial object is a one-point set. Let $V=S=*$. Then $G(*)=*$ and the unique map from $*$ into an algebra is an algebra map, since all constructor operations preserve the basepoint. Thus the printed syntax initiality is realized in this example. Let $A=*$ and $q:Q\to *$ for a two-point pointed set $Q$. Then $B_1(Q)=*\times_*Q\cong Q$, which is not initial. The projection $b_1$ is the identity up to this canonical isomorphism and gives exactly the required unary operation. More strongly $B_0(Q)=*$ and $B_2(Q)\cong Q^2$. The three-branch inference object has five points $(1+(2-1)+(4-1))$, while the shortened object using just the nullary and binary summands has four. The three-branch functor has initial algebra $*$ here, by the same unique basepoint map argument. Hence the manuscript's own hypotheses do not imply that the unary pullback is initial, or even that the three-branch inference functor is isomorphic to the shortened one. They *do* give the desired closure implication for monos. The code at `V46ForkDerivationInference.lean:104–112` invokes `IsInitial.ofStrict` and legitimately makes that stronger identification in its own extensive setting. It cannot serve as the proof at the weaker printed hypotheses. There is no counterexample to the printed theorem here.

**Conclusion on A.6.** The printed argument survives non-strict initial objects; all uses of finite coproducts, coproduct images and the generic closure theorem are valid with the given hypotheses. An exact weak-hypothesis assembly still needs the three-row record and its unary projection application, or equivalent source-level evidence at that type. Later extensive examples are unaffected.
