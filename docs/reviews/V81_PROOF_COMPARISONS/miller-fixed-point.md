# Miller existence in v81. The fixed-point step

Once each coordinate correspondence has a closed graph and nonempty interval values, ordinary finite-cube Brouwer and compactness suffice. The following proof uses neither a continuous selection of the original correspondence nor the topology of the manuscript’s weighted metric. All mathematical choices are classical. It produces an existential witness without an effectiveness claim.

**Finite-cube lemma.** Let $K=[0,1]^m$, and let $B(x)$ be a nonempty closed box in $K$ for each $x$, with closed graph. For $\epsilon>0$, set
$$
 T_\epsilon(x)=\operatorname{conv}\bigcup\{B(u):u\in K,\ \|u-x\|_\infty<\epsilon\}.
$$
It is convex and contained in $K$. Each $z\in B(x)$ is a constant selection into $T_\epsilon(y)$ for every $y$ within $\epsilon$ of $x$. Consequently there is a continuous $g_\epsilon:K\to K$ with $g_\epsilon(x)\in T_\epsilon(x)$. Explicitly take a finite $\epsilon/2$-net $V$, choose $z_v\in B(v)$, and give $z_v$ weight proportional to $\max(0,\epsilon-\|x-v\|_\infty)$. The denominator is strictly positive and every positive-weight vertex lies within $\epsilon$ of $x$. This construction needs no continuity of the choices at the vertices.

Brouwer gives $x_n=g_{1/(n+1)}(x_n)$. Pass to a convergent subsequence with limit $x$. If $x_i>\max B(x)_i$ for some coordinate (the maximum of its coordinate projection), choose $c$ strictly between them. The set of $u$ for which some $z\in B(u)$ has $z_i\ge c$ is closed: it is the first projection of the intersection of the closed graph with $K\times\{z\in K:z_i\ge c\}$, and $K$ is compact. Thus a neighborhood of $x$ excludes all such $u$. Eventually the entire $1/(n+1)$-ball around the subsequential $x_n$, relative to $K$, is in this neighborhood. The closed halfspace $z_i\le c$ contains the union defining $T_{1/(n+1)}(x_n)$ and hence its convex hull. Fixedness gives $(x_n)_i\le c$ and passage to the limit contradicts $x_i>c$. Reverse the inequalities for $x_i<\min B(x)_i$. Since $B(x)$ is a box, all the coordinate conclusions give $x\in B(x)$. Dimension zero is the singleton cube. No uniform upper-continuity estimate for the moving fibers is used. For arbitrary closed convex fibers, replace the violated-coordinate test by a strict finite-dimensional separating hyperplane. The box case needs no separation theorem.

**Passage to all program coordinates.** Write $Q=[0,1]^{\mathbb N}$ and $J_d(a)$ for the interval in step 3 of the prior manuscript-side proof. For $u\in K_m$, let $e_m u$ extend $u$ by zeros and define $B_m(u)=\prod_{d<m} J_d(e_m u)$. It is a nonempty closed box. Its graph in $K_m\times K_m$ is the finite intersection of the inverse images of the coordinate graphs under $(u,v)\mapsto(e_m u,v_d)$. Apply the finite lemma and lift its fixed point by $e_m$. This shows that
$$
 D_m=\{a\in Q:\forall d<m,\ a(d)\in J_d(a)\}
$$
is nonempty. Each $D_m$ is closed by the maps $a\mapsto(a,a(d))$, and $D_{m+1}\subseteq D_m$. Compactness of the product cube gives $a\in\bigcap_m D_m$. For each $d$ use $m=d+1$. When all names of $a$ compute $r$ at index $d$ with the prescribed strict bounds, step 3 gives $J_d(a)=\{r\}$, hence $a(d)=r$. This directly yields the type in `V46MillerRealComputation.lean:25–32` once the certificate and interval constructions are formalized. The fixed-point lemma does not require constructing a compact graph in $Q\times Q$.
