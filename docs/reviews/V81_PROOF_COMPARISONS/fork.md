### 1. An undecided atom at each endpoint

**Manuscript.** Section 10, lines 561–607, fixes an endpoint $i$, a compatible c.e. closed extension $T$, and its atom map $\theta_i$. Write $P=\{x\mid\theta_i(x)\in T_i\}$ and $N=\{x\mid\neg\theta_i(x)\in T_i\}$, subsets of $\mathbb N^2$. These disjoint c.e. sets extend the signed inseparable pair. If every $x$ belonged to $P$ or $N$, simultaneous enumeration would terminate on every $x$ and decide $P$. Therefore some $x$ belongs to neither. The undecided formula is $\theta_i(x)$, with negation $\nu_i(\theta_i(x))$. Existence of an undecided *arbitrary* sentence alone would not justify this assertion.

There is a shorter categorical proof using the actual compiled atomic idempotents. For fixed `i : Bool`, `t : ForkSyntax.Theory`, put `p := observedPositive i t.val`, `n := observedNegative i t.val`, both in `Idem natCarrier`. The exact instance
`h := observed_inseparable i t.val t.property.1 t.property.2.1`
has type `Inseparable p n` (`ForkObservedIncompleteness.lean:72`). If `hc : p ⊔ n = ⊤`, the expression

```lean
h.no_separator ⟨p, n, ⟨h.disjoint, hc⟩, le_rfl, le_rfl⟩
```

has target `False` by the definitions of `Complementary` and `Inseparable` in `RestrictionIdempotents.lean:198–212`. In fact the end of `domain_incompleteness` there uses precisely this implication on theorem/refutation domains. Here the argument instantiates it on the **atom** domains.

If every numerical $k$ were in the support of $p$ or $n$, `EnumerableCarriers.support_sup p n` and `support_top natCarrier` would give `support (p ⊔ n) = support (⊤ : Idem natCarrier)` by set extensionality: the former support is the union and the latter is all of $\mathbb N$. Applying `support_injective` would give the forbidden equality `hc`. Classical negation of universal coverage thus gives $k$ with $k\notin\operatorname{supp} p$ and $k\notin\operatorname{supp} n$. The lemmas are at `EnumerableCarriers.lean:166,176,181`, with full support at `EffectiveForkUniversal.lean:25`. Take the tuple $x$ returned by `Nat.unpair k`. Its paired code equals $k$ by `Nat.pair_unpair`, so `ForkObservationFunctor.lean:85–88` identifies $x$ as outside both tuple sets. Applying the two equivalences `observedPositive_mem i t.val k` and `observedNegative_mem i t.val k` (`ForkObservedIncompleteness.lean:87–94`) gives the two required failures of membership for `atomCode k` and `negCode (atomCode k)`. This derives the stronger claim from the existing atomic declarations without selecting a separator or invoking any additional computability theorem.

### 2. The topological gluing

**Manuscript.** Section 10, lines 27–35, concerns topological spaces, before any cosheaf is applied. Put $D=\{c\}$, $L=\{a,c\}$, $R=\{b,c\}$. By `ForkTopology.lean:27`, a subset $U$ of $X$ is open precisely if $a\in U\Rightarrow c\in U$ and $b\in U\Rightarrow c\in U$. The five-element presentation of this rule is identified with the *actual* open lattice by `Open.toActual`, `Open.toActual_surjective`, and `Open.actualOrderIso` (`ForkTopology.lean:108–149`).

A subset $W\subseteq L$ is relatively open iff $a\in W\Rightarrow c\in W$. In the forward direction take the intersection with an open of $X$. Conversely $W$ itself, regarded as a subset of $X$, satisfies both implication conditions, the condition at $b$ being vacuous. Thus its opens are exactly $\varnothing$, $\{c\}$ and $L$. The identical argument at $R$ replaces $a$ by $b$. For the two-element space with opens $\varnothing$, $\{1\}$, $\{0,1\}$, the two maps $0\mapsto a,\ 1\mapsto c$ and $0\mapsto b,\ 1\mapsto c$ are homeomorphisms onto $L$ and $R$ respectively: under either bijection the three opens correspond. Both identify the open point with $c$. On $D$ the induced topology is the singleton topology.

The actual inclusions $D\hookrightarrow L,\ D\hookrightarrow R$ and $L\hookrightarrow X,\ R\hookrightarrow X$ give the gluing in topological spaces. For any space $Y$ and continuous maps $f:L\to Y$, $g:R\to Y$ agreeing at $c$, define $h(a)=f(a)$, $h(b)=g(b)$, $h(c)=f(c)=g(c)$. For an open $O\subseteq Y$, the traces of $h^{-1}(O)$ on $L$ and $R$ are $f^{-1}(O)$ and $g^{-1}(O)$. Relative openness of the first supplies the implication at $a$, and relative openness of the second supplies the implication at $b$. The defining topology on $X$ therefore gives continuity. Values on the three points force uniqueness. This proves the pushout universal property, including the topology and the leg maps. The set-valued cosheaf pushout of B.1 is a different universal property.

**Comparison status.** The finite open presentation proves every set-membership fact used by this argument. No project declaration using these inclusions as a topological pushout, or exhibiting the two homeomorphisms, was found. `ForkCovers.lean:42,48` proves the cover classification, and `ForkActualCosheaves.lean:102` proves the cosheaf equivalence directly. Neither needs this auxiliary gluing claim. This is a missing formal application of definitions, not an extra topological hypothesis or a defect in B.1.

### 3. B.10 at its categorical pullback type

**The exact generic comparison is already present.** Let $\mathcal E=\mathcal C_{\mathrm{rec}}^J$, $2=1+1$, let $\mathsf{true}:1\to 2$ be the second coproduct injection, and suppose $d:V\times V\to 2$ has the pullback square of Appendix A, lines 85–96. For *any* object $P$ of $\mathcal E$ and any $f,g:P\to V$,

$$
d\circ\langle f,g\rangle=\mathsf{true}\circ !_{P}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f=g.
$$

Indeed, the equality on the left gives a unique lift $\ell:P\to V$ with $\Delta_V\circ\ell=\langle f,g\rangle$, so the two projections give $\ell=f=g$. The converse follows by composing the commutative pullback square with $f$. This is exactly `DecidableVariables.test_true_iff`, `V46CategoricalListLaws.lean:251–267`, on the structure `DecidableVariables V` at `V46CategoricalSyntax.lean:155–158`. The regular and finitarily extensive category assumptions of the Lean lemma are available on the literal diagram category (`V46NumericalForkPartialMaps.lean:34–38`). In particular the generic bridge from a categorical pullback to equality tests on *all generalized elements* is already formalized.

**Realizing the component functions.** Use the numerical presentations $H_0=(1\leftarrow 1\to 1)$, $H_1=(1\leftarrow 0\to 0)$, $H_2=(0\leftarrow 0\to 1)$. There are canonical bijections, where the right side denotes the underlying set at vertex $j$.

$$
\mathcal E(H_j,Y)\cong \lvert Y_j\rvert.
$$

For $j=0$, a total natural map is forced by its centre value $y$. Its endpoint entries are the values of the two transitions on $y$. For $j\in\{1,2\}$, only the chosen endpoint has a singleton component, and each admissible endpoint element gives a map. All constants are computable; the empty component has a unique map. The coded representatives and totality proofs are `EffectiveForkProbes.lean:30–35,60–85`; endpoint bijections are explicit in `ForkProbeObservations.lean:121`. Componentwise equality of numerical maps is `V46NumericalTuples.lean:150`, `map_eq_iff`; `EffectiveForkTotal.lean:100` and `V46NumericalForkPartialMaps.lean:26` compare the diagram and coded presentations.

Write $r_i^Y:Y_0\to Y_i$ for the transition to endpoint $i$. Let $P_y:H_j\to Y$ denote the representative of $y\in Y_j$. The inclusion $s_i:H_i\to H_0$, for $i\in\{1,2\}$, is the identity on the singleton at endpoint $i$ and the unique map from an empty set at both remaining components. Then $P_y\circ s_i=P_{r_i^Y(y)}$ for $y\in Y_0$: the equality follows at $i$ from the definition and holds uniquely on the other components. This is contravariance in the representing object. In the **object** $Y$ its component transition is covariant, in the direction $Y_0\to Y_i$. In particular the correct probe arrow is $H_i\to H_0$. There is no map $H_0\to H_i$ in the total category because its centre component would be a function $1\to\varnothing$.

Identify the terminal object with the constant singleton diagram. Via the pointwise coproduct, $2$ identifies with the constant two-element diagram and its true inclusion with the same tag at every component. The bijection above applied to $Y=2$ sends $\mathsf{true}\circ !_{H_j}$ to true; the function on these two Boolean values induced by precomposition with $s_i$ is the identity. Define $d_j(x,y)$ to be the Boolean corresponding to $d\circ\langle P_x,P_y\rangle:H_j\to 2$. Injectivity of the probe bijection on maps into 2 and `test_true_iff` yield $d_j(x,y)=\mathsf{true}\iff P_x=P_y\iff x=y$. Naturality of the product pairing and $P_x\circ s_i=P_{r_i^V(x)}$ give

$$
d_0(x,y)=d_i\big(r_i^V(x),r_i^V(y)\big).
$$

This constructs all three functions, the two naturality equations, and all three equivalences demanded by `Fork.EqualityDecision` (`ForkObstruction.lean:35–43`). The absence of such a triple, even allowing arbitrary set functions, is already checked for the actual encoded numerical projection maps in `EffectiveForkUniversal.lean:86–100` and for the literal product coordinates in `ForkObstruction.lean:25–54`.

**A direct typed contradiction without the intermediate structure.** Take $u=(0,0,0,0)$, $v=(0,1,0,0)\in V_0$. Their left images coincide, but their centre coordinates differ. Set $f=P_u$, $g=P_v:H_0\to V$, and $s=s_1:H_1\to H_0$. Then $f\ne g$, while $f\circ s=g\circ s$. Put $h=d\circ\langle f,g\rangle:H_0\to 2$. Applying `test_true_iff` with $P=H_1$ gives $h\circ s=\mathsf{true}\circ !_{H_1}$. Since precomposition with $s$ on maps from $H_0$ to 2 is injective, $h=\mathsf{true}\circ !_{H_0}$. Applying the same lemma with $P=H_0$ now gives $f=g$, a contradiction. The injectivity invoked here holds **on maps into 2** because its legs are identities in the constant presentation. There is no assertion that $s$ is epic; $f$ and $g$ themselves disprove that.

**Global elements and bookkeeping.** $H_0$ is terminal and its maps into $Y$ are the centre elements. A fork with empty centre and nonempty endpoint can have no global elements. The proof does not require all endpoint elements of all diagrams to extend globally. In this specific contradiction $u$ and $v$ do yield global points of $V$, and comparing their restrictions at the non-global probe $H_1$ is legitimate. The isomorphisms with the chosen terminal, product and Boolean coproduct objects, if the chosen objects are not definitionally the displayed constant diagrams, must be inserted before claiming equality of raw component codes. `V46DiagramDecidableVariables.lean:27–68` constructs the general comparison isomorphisms and transports a diagonal pullback along a finite-limit/finite-coproduct-preserving functor. The later declaration `fork` there is a *positive* test for the constant number diagram and does not supply a test on $V$. Also `ForkProbeObservations.lean:67–68` uses false for the left endpoint, whereas `ForkObservedIncompleteness.lean:24–26` uses true for the left endpoint in `endpointTheory`. Use the named endpoint objects or reverse the Boolean when relating the two parametrizations; both endpoints are universally quantified.
