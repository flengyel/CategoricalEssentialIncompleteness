## Appendix A. Total maps in a monic-span category

**Reconstruction from the manuscript.** `manuscript/latex/appendices/a-syntax-and-theories.tex:570–581` assumes a regular category D. Its monic-span category and its subcategory of total arrows keep the objects of D. The forward functor J sends a:X→Y to [X ←id X →a Y]. For a total [X ←m U →v Y], the restriction idempotent [m,m] equals the identity span. Equality of those quotient spans supplies an apex isomorphism U ≅ X whose underlying map is m. Define P on the given span by m⁻¹ ≫ v. If the span is replaced by an isomorphic representative, this arrow is unchanged, since both legs commute with the apex isomorphism. PJ(a)=a directly. The isomorphism m between the apexes exhibits the identity-legged span for P([m,v]) as the same *quotient arrow* as [m,v]. Thus JP is also identity on homs. Applying the faithful J proves P preserves identities and composition. The object maps are identity, so these two hom equalities are equalities of functors. Pullbacks suffice for this part. The regularity hypothesis also serves the separate range argument.

**Existing formal source and the strict inverse.** `V46PartialMaps.lean:30–36,57–59` wraps an object in the sole field `Par.obj` and quotients span homs. `V46PartialMapTotals.lean:31–51` obtains invertibility of the left leg of a total representative and its apex isomorphism; lines 54–72 implement P on maps by `totalValue` and prove `ofTotal_totalValue` and `totalValue_ofTotal`. Lines 86–109 use the wide subcategory, define `toTotal` and already verify the object equality by cases on the two wrappers. Lines 111–115 return `totalEquivalence : D ≌ Tot D`.

Precisely, take P.obj W = W.obj.obj and P.map u = `totalValue u.hom u.property`. Then P(J(X))=X and J(P(W))=W by unwrapping and rewrapping; the latter can be proved by cases on W and its contained Par object as in lines 104–109. For P.map_id and P.map_comp, apply `ofTotal_injective` and rewrite by `ofTotal_id`, `ofTotal_comp` and `ofTotal_totalValue` (`V46PartialMaps.lean:228–243`). For J ⋙ P = 𝟭 D use `totalValue_ofTotal`. Both are componentwise proofs for functor extensionality, with any transports reduced by the object equalities above. The two equations also force uniqueness of P on maps, despite the implementation's use of a chosen span representative. There is no multiplicity of object copies.
