# §9. The weighted distance and the product topology

Let $I=[0,1]$, $Q=I^{\mathbb N}$, $a_n=2^{-n-1}$ and $d(z,w)=\sum_n a_n|z_n-w_n|$. These are the point space and distance in the [manuscript](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/04-miller-sequences.tex#L239-L255). The formalization defines this exact real-valued function on the product space. Its continuity for the given topology and continuity of each coordinate for the metric topology follow by separate estimates.

1. Each summand belongs to $[0,a_n]$ and $\sum_n a_n=1$, so the series converges with $0\le d\le1$. Symmetry and $d(z,z)=0$ hold term by term. The triangle inequality follows by summing the triangle inequalities and using absolute convergence. Since $a_k|z_k-w_k|\le d(z,w)$ and $a_k>0$ for every $k$, $d(z,w)=0$ implies $z=w$.
2. For every $k$, $|z_k-w_k|\le 2^{k+1}d(z,w)$. Each coordinate of the identity from the metric space to the product is therefore continuous. Equivalently, given a basic product neighborhood with coordinate radii $r_k>0$ on a finite nonempty set $F$, the ball of radius $\min_{k\in F}a_kr_k$ is contained in that neighborhood. If $F=\varnothing$ the neighborhood is all of $Q$.
3. Given $z$ and $\epsilon>0$, choose $N\ge1$ with $2^{-N}<\epsilon/2$. If $|z_k-w_k|<\epsilon/2$ for $k<N$, the first $N$ terms sum to at most $\epsilon/2$ and the tail to at most $\sum_{k\ge N}a_k=2^{-N}$. This product neighborhood lies in the $d$-ball of radius $\epsilon$. The two topologies are equal.
4. For the existing Lean definition there is also a direct proof of continuity of $(z,w)\mapsto d(z,w)$ in the product topology. Apply `Series.value_continuous` with parameter type $Q\times Q$ and coordinate function $(z,w)\mapsto|z_k-w_k|$. This function is continuous by evaluation in the product followed by subtraction and absolute value. Its bounds are 0 and 1. The resulting weighted sum is definitionally the displayed `productDistance`. Continuity in the fixed first argument makes every metric ball product-open; step 2 supplies the converse topology inclusion.

The existing proofs give the weights, their exact tail, uniform convergence of the weighted sums, and the nearest point and lower-bound inequalities for each nonempty closed box. Their proofs of continuity of box distances use the existing product topology directly. The remaining WSL2 application is to exhibit the metric axioms and prove that its topology equals the existing product topology on these points. A proof of metrizability of a countable product without the displayed distance would omit its specification.
