## Full Karoubi envelope

**Type and source.** At `manuscript/latex/sections/02-categorical-preliminaries.tex:90–97`, $P=(X,e)$ and $Q=(Y,d)$ are objects in the envelope of **all** idempotents, and $f:P\to Q$ has underlying morphism satisfying $dfe=f$. The asserted equivalence is
$$
   \mathrm{isTotal}_{\mathrm{Kar}}(f)\ \longleftrightarrow\
       \bar f\,e=e
$$
in manuscript order. In particular the right-hand side is an equality of morphisms on $X$. It is not an assertion that $\bar f=e$, which would in general imply that $e$ itself was a restriction idempotent.

**Proof application.** Import `CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness.V46KaroubiRestriction`. For the full Mathlib type `CategoryTheory.Idempotents.Karoubi C`, the restriction is `KaroubiRestriction.restriction` at lines 35–41; the four axioms are proved at 55–77 and `KaroubiRestriction.rst_f` is its underlying component at 79–80. Unfold `CategoryTheory.RestrictionCategory.isTotal` from `Lean/PCats/Restriction.lean:84–86`. Forward, apply `congrArg (fun k : P ⟶ P => k.f)` to the equality `rst f = 𝟙 P`. Reverse, apply `Karoubi.hom_ext` to the component equality. The components are definitionally `P.p ≫ rst f.f` and `P.p`. The source commutation at `V46KaroubiRestriction.lean:27–31` would allow the opposite order also. The Karoubi identity uses $e$ even when its restriction differs from $e$. This disposes of both the totality assertion and its composition convention at the general printed type. The original Cockett–Lack notation/proposition comparison is bibliographic.

## Doubling partial functions

**Type and source.** At `manuscript/latex/sections/02-categorical-preliminaries.tex:162–168`, $F$ is an endofunctor of all sets and partial functions. It preserves restrictions, ranges, zeros and binary restriction coproducts. The canonical product comparison for nonempty $X,Y$ is a total map with formula $((x,y),a)\mapsto((x,a),(y,a))$. It is not surjective.

**Proof application.** Import `CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness.DoublingRangeFunctor`. Lines 23–45 define exactly `X × Bool` and transport a partial result while retaining its Boolean coordinate. Lines 58–84 identify the actual coproduct comparison and its inverse. Lines 86–111 instantiate all four required fields of `RangeFunctor`. Lines 113–127 compute the pairing of the images of the two actual projections; the concrete restriction-product data using the lifted projections and `PFun.prodLift` are `V46ModelFamilyDiagonal.lean:29–43`. Lines 129–137 give the nonsurjectivity theorem with both `Nonempty` hypotheses by exhibiting $((x,\mathrm{false}),(y,\mathrm{true}))$. If this comparison were an isomorphism, its inverse evaluated at that mixed-tag pair would have to produce a preimage, by the inverse/identity equation in partial functions. Hence the displayed cone fails to be a restriction product. The implication from nonsurjectivity requires no principle concerning the cardinalities of infinite sets. The paper claims neither nonsurjectivity for empty factors nor nonisomorphism of the two abstract carriers. No additional defect found here.

## Cockett–Hofstra recognition

The external characterization can be confirmed independently; the authors give their argument in the [Turing structure section of these lecture slides](https://pages.cpsc.ucalgary.ca/~robin/talks/estonia-winter-2010/estonia-slides-4.pdf). Here is the two-direction argument in the manuscript's notation.

Suppose $m_A:A\to U$, $r_A:U\rightharpoonup A$ have $r_A m_A=1_A$ and $m_A$ is total for each $A$. Let $\tau:U\times U\rightharpoonup U$ be Turing. For every $A,B$, define
$$
  \tau_{A,B}= r_B\,\tau\,(1_U\times m_A):U\times A\rightharpoonup B .
$$
For $f:Z\times A\rightharpoonup B$, apply the single evaluator to $m_B f(1_Z\times r_A):Z\times U\rightharpoonup U$. It gives a **total** parameter $g:Z\to U$. Precomposition by $1_Z\times m_A$ and postcomposition by $r_B$, followed by $r_A m_A=1_A$ and $r_B m_B=1_B$, show $\tau_{A,B}(g\times 1_A)=f$. No equation $m_A r_A=\bar r_A$ enters.

Conversely, suppose $U$ supplies all typed Turing evaluators $\tau_{A,B}:U\times A\rightharpoonup B$. Take $A=B=U$ for the special evaluator. Parameterize the total projection $X\times 1\to X$ under $\tau_{1,X}$ to obtain a total $m_X:X\to U$. Put $r_X=\tau_{1,X}\langle 1_U,!_U\rangle:U\rightharpoonup X$. Evaluating the parameterization at $\langle1_X,!_X\rangle$ gives $r_Xm_X=1_X$.

**Exact statement of the cited characterization for the correspondence.** For an arbitrary cartesian restriction category and a fixed object $U$, the existence of total-parameter evaluators $U\times A\rightharpoonup B$ for all $A,B$ is equivalent to the existence of a Turing endoevaluator and ordinary total-section retractions onto every object. `Lean/PCats/Recursion.lean:51–55` supplies the evaluator predicate. `V46SplitTuring.lean:134–163` constructs analogous data for a specific split completion; `V46CompatibleSplitSlices.lean:70–115` supplies the stronger slice witnesses. Neither declares the above biconditional in arbitrary cartesian restriction categories. No checked import or declaration for that exact general biconditional was located. This is an unassembled general correspondence for a true citation, not a missing hypothesis or a dependence of the paper's slice examples.
