## External results used in proofs

**Łoś’s theorem, §7.4, pp. 42–43.** For $M=\mathbb N^I/\mathcal U$, each PA axiom holds in every factor and hence in $M$. Lemma 7.8 also needs transfer for the unary formula $C(x)$ to identify $C(M)$ with the ultrapower of $C(\mathbb N)$. A representative in $C$ on a large set can be repaired elsewhere with the value at one coordinate of that set. The set is nonempty because the ultrafilter is proper. For the binary graph $\Gamma(x,y)$, transfer applied to $(c_i,\Gamma_{\mathbb N}(c_i))$ and functionality in $M$ prove naturality. In Lean `eval_representative` handles parameters, `sentence_iff` and `modelsPA` the sentence instance, `predicateEquiv` the predicate and `predicateComparison` its graph. The natural isomorphism extends to the total restriction splitting and its chosen domain interpretation. [Keisler, Theorem 3.1](https://people.math.wisc.edu/~hkeisler/ultraproducts-web-final.pdf#page=4) · [Formula transfer](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46ArithmeticUltrapower.lean#L72) · [Sentence instance](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46ArithmeticUltrapower.lean#L175) · [Predicate, graph and comparison](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46UltrapowerComparison.lean#L30)

**Miller’s sequence, §9, pp. 61, 63–64.** The paragraph following Miller’s Theorem 6.3 identifies the *coordinate* of the value with the fixed program index. Bauer–Hanson specify the binary representation and strict rational approximation criterion. CEI requires one $a$ such that for every $d,r$, computation of $r$ to precision $2^{-k}$ at every $k$ by program $d$ from every binary name of $a$ implies $a(d)=r$. Lean proves existence by finite-dimensional fixed points and nested closed subsets of the compact cube. In Lemma 9.3 the single index $h(e)$ computes $r_e(a)$ from every name of $a$, whence $a(h(e))=r_e(a)$. This is the inference needed for both inverse-image equalities and then the field application. The witness, expanded statement and application are present in the v81 receipt. [Miller, Theorem 6.3 and following paragraph](https://people.math.wisc.edu/~jsmiller8/Papers/continuous.pdf) · [Bauer–Hanson, §2 and Theorem 2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.01256v3#S2) · [Existence](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46MillerFixedPoint.lean#L125) · [Expanded type](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46MillerFixedPointChecks.lean#L45) · [Cut reduction](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46MillerCutReduction.lean#L48)

**Completeness and downward Löwenheim–Skolem, Appendix D, p. 96.** The arithmetic language is countable and every model extending PA is infinite. Apply completeness first to consistent $T$ and then to $T+\neg E$ when $T$ fails to prove the universal graph equality $E$. A countable elementary submodel and transport along a bijection with $\mathbb N$ give, respectively, an index for the model family and a member in which the graphs differ. No recursive model presentation is required. In Lean the countable-carrier construction feeds `complete_nat_models`, `modelIndex_nonempty` and `joint_faithful_nat`. [Both uses](../../../manuscript/latex/appendices/d-faithful-family.tex#L3) · [Construction and application](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/CountableArithmeticModels.lean#L73) · [References for the model-theoretic inputs](../V80_ASSERTIONS/primary6.md)

The comparison in Appendix C involves a different theorem of Lengyel. Both readers inspected the author’s 2006 source, including the statement and proof of Theorem 4.1 on p. 11. It assumes a range functor into a locally connected split target satisfying weak choice, a weak Turing morphism in the source, the stated coproduct isomorphism and a nonzero image of the identity. The proof passes to the identity-functor case and chooses an index for $b^{-1}f$, where $f$ is the chosen section. In CEI an endomorphism $u$ with $F(u)=F(b)^{-1}s_A$ would yield $F(bu)=s_A$, which C.1 excludes. The general functor statement therefore fails under its original hypotheses.
