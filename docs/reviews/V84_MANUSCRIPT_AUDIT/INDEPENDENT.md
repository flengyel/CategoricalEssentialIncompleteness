## C. Minor issues

1. **Unused hypothesis in the adjunction formulation.** Page 18, Theorem 4.7, `sections/03b-internal-adjunctions.tex:375–396`. The adjunction is between total-category subobject/translation categories. Images, Boolean truth values, term/formula/list and derivation initiality, and inverse images suffice for the proof at lines 399–474. The proof does not use partial inverses of every mono or a split restriction ambient. Their role is to identify separation by subobjects with separation by restriction idempotents in Corollary 4.8. Theorem 4.7 remains valid. A sentence locating the stronger hypothesis at the restriction interpretation would clarify the scope.
2. **Abstract hypotheses require two forward lookups.** Page 1, `abstract.tex:7–9,17–19`. The clauses refer only to theorem numbers for their assumptions. Page 4 spells out regularity, stable disjoint coproducts, the monomorphism condition and initialities. Pages 21–24 supply parameterized derivations and a theorem point for the second equivalence. Adding the decisive assumptions to the abstract would let it stand alone, especially the availability of a theorem point for evaluation. This is editorial. Knuth, Larrabee and Roberts, Stanford report §1, point 5 (printed p. 2), discuss self-contained theorem statements. Applying the same aim to an abstract is this reader’s recommendation.
3. **Hypotheses of a sufficient criterion are stronger than the calculation.** Page 11, Proposition 3.4, `sections/03-sections-and-inseparability.tex:158–174`. Once a common upper bound of the two tagged restrictions exists, the restriction order forces its restrictions to equal both pieces, and dominance of their complementing domains makes it total. A least upper bound is convenient but unnecessary for existence of this conditional. The proposition is true as written and does not assert necessity.
