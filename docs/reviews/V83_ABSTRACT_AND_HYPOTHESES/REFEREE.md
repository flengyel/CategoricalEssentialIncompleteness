## (A) Kill-shots / hard blockers

No counterexample was found to the revised claims. The classification in the abstract concerns consistent theories generated by disjoint domains in the specified nullary-predicate language. The quantifier is over closed extensions in that language. Completeness is an equality in the subobject lattice of the sentence object. This qualification matters even in categories admitting finite limits and coproducts. For a nontrivial group $G$, the proper inclusion $1\hookrightarrow 1+G$ of $G$-sets contains every global element, since left multiplication on $G$ has no fixed point. Consequently properness alone cannot supply an undecided global sentence. The abstract states that the join is proper. The introduction gives the equality for completeness, the inequality for incompleteness and the forward reference to their definitions. ## (C) Minor issues

The three requested body corrections pass the following tests.

1. **Di Paola–Heller 8.15.** Their definitions require sections for all partial morphisms and compatible binary coproducts. The manuscript spells out both compatibility conditions. For the sum-valued evaluator, each $p:X\times X\rightharpoonup X+X$ admits a total $k:X\to X$ such that $p=\tau(k\times1_X)$. This is the instance of Definition 4.1 needed here. The published proof passes from empty inverse images of a proposed separator $c,d$ to the inverse image of their join using preservation of the join (p. 626). For the last step under the weaker coproduct hypotheses, a total $g:X\to X$ has $g^*c=g^*d=0_X$. Then $cg=dg=0_X$. Range agreement and commutation of restriction idempotents give $\operatorname{ran}(g)[c,d]=0$. Since $\operatorname{ran}[c,d]=c\vee d=1_X$, a second application gives $\operatorname{ran}(g)=0_X$. Hence $g=0_X$ and $1_X=0_X$. In the one-morphism category the two equal domains are separable, verifying the need for the nonzero hypothesis. These are both clauses of the representation hypothesis for the cited implication. The consistency of $H$ makes the theorem and refutation supports disjoint. The corollary for a consistent c.e. extension of PA has its own reduction. The inverse image $\Theta^{-1}R$ would separate $D_0,D_1$ for any recursive separator $R$ of the two code sets.
