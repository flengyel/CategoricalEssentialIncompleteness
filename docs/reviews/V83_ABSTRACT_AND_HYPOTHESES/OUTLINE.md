## Abstract, p. 1

Under the hypotheses of Theorem 4.7, the [abstract](../../../manuscript/latex/abstract.tex) characterizes essential incompleteness of the consistent first-order theory generated by disjoint domains. The axioms assert the atoms indexed by the first domain and negate those indexed by the second. Every consistent closed extension in this language has a proper join of its theorem and refutation subobjects if and only if no complementary pair contains the two given domains respectively. The construction is a left adjoint on pairs generating consistent theories. Theorem 5.3 gives the corresponding equivalence for the constant family over the theorem object. The abstract states the uniform conclusion for PA and identifies the Miller-sequence and three-point-space applications.

## 1. Introduction, p. 4

The introduction gives the types and hypotheses for the two equivalences. In the total category $\mathcal D$ of the restriction idempotent completion, take disjoint subobjects $i_a:A\hookrightarrow X$ and $i_b:B\hookrightarrow X$. Let $\theta_X:X\to S_X$ be the nullary atomic sentence constructor and $\nu_X:S_X\to S_X$ negation. The axiom subobject is $\operatorname{im}(\theta_X i_a)\vee\operatorname{im}(\nu_X\theta_X i_b)$. If its deductive closure $T_{a,b}$ is consistent, essential incompleteness says that for every closed extension $H\ge T_{a,b}$, the condition $H\wedge\nu_X^*H=\bot$ implies $H\vee\nu_X^*H\ne\top$. Sections 2.1, 4.1 and 4.6 define the domain and subobject conventions, closure and the free theory. For a consistent theory $u:U\hookrightarrow S$, §5.3 proves the equivalence for $1_U\times u$ in $\mathcal D/U$, quantifying over extensions that may vary with the parameter. The introduction also states the lifting hypotheses for diagonal comparison and explains how the arithmetic, Miller and cosheaf applications use different classes of extensions.

- [**1.1 (Related work, p. 6)**](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/01-introduction.tex#L179) places these results in the literature. Di Paola–Heller 8.15 assumes a dominical category in which every morphism has a partial section, coproducts preserve pairs of total morphisms and the canonical distributivity maps are invertible. For the diagonal argument let $\tau:X\times X\rightharpoonup X+X$ satisfy $p=\tau(k\times1_X)$ for some total $k:X\to X$ for each $p$ of the same type. With binary coproducts and the stated sections, a separator of the two summand domains of $\tau\Delta_X$ forces $1_X=0_X$. Thus $1_X\ne0_X$ gives inseparability. Theorem 3.1 specifies the source lift for a diagonal comparison between categories, and Appendix C exhibits its necessity in the comparison with Lengyel’s 2006 theorem. Bernardi uses precomplete equivalence relations. Montagna and Di Paola–Montagna supply syntactic precedents for §6. Murwanashyaka–Pakhomov–Visser encode disjoint c.e. sets by finite equivalence-class sizes and characterize essential undecidability by recursive inseparability. Section 4 constructs a free theory on internal subobjects. Heller and Lengyel concern existence of recursion categories. Van Dijk–Gietelink Oldenziel describe Joyal's arithmetic-universe proofs. Cockett–Hofstra relate Turing categories to partial combinatory algebras by splitting idempotents. Cockett's results on inseparable domains and provability predicates supply further comparisons.

## 3. Diagonal comparison and complementary conditionals, p. 9

- [**3.1 (The comparison theorem, p. 9)**](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/03-sections-and-inseparability.tex#L19) defines $h=b\tau\Delta_U:U\rightharpoonup U+U$ and its two summand domains $a_0,a_1$. For the functor $F$ of Theorem 3.1, let disjoint domains $P,N$ on $FU$ contain $F(a_0),F(a_1)$ respectively. A partial conditional with summand inverse images $N,P$ and a lift in the source yield a total $g:U\to U$ with $(Fg)^*P=(Fg)^*N=0$. If $F(1_U)\ne0$ and this inverse image preserves $P\vee N$, then $P\vee N\ne1_{FU}$.

- [**3.2 (Complementary conditionals, p. 10)**](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/03-sections-and-inseparability.tex#L108) supplies source conditionals from joins of disjoint morphisms or from specified sections. Appendix C shows why the lift matters.

## 4. Internal theories and essential incompleteness, p. 11

- [**4.1 (Theories and their interpretation, p. 11)**](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/03a-internal-theories.tex#L3) identifies the theorem subobject $t\hookrightarrow S$ as the image of finite sentence derivations and its refutation subobject as $\nu^*t$.

- [**4.2 (Incompleteness from semantic consistency, p. 13)**](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/03a-internal-theories.tex#L149) applies §3 to the interpreted theorem and refutation domains under the disjointness, lifting and nonzero hypotheses.

- [**4.3 (Inseparability in the source, p. 14)**](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/03a-internal-theories.tex#L209) proves that a total $f:X\to S$ carrying inseparable domains into the theorem and refutation domains rules out completeness of any consistent closed extension.

- [**4.4 (Generation and logical translations, p. 14)**](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/03b-internal-adjunctions.tex#L1) gives the least deductive closure and the adjunction between generation after direct image and inverse image under a translation.

- [**4.5 (Change of the ambient category, p. 16)**](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/03b-internal-adjunctions.tex#L188) compares deduction under a change of ambient category.

- [**4.6 (Free first-order theories and inseparability, p. 18)**](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/03b-internal-adjunctions.tex#L323) proves the free first-order adjunction (Theorem 4.7) and the classification in the abstract (Corollary 4.8). A complementary separator gives a Boolean valuation and hence a complete consistent extension. Conversely, pulling back the theorem and refutation subobjects of such an extension along $\theta_X$ gives a separator.

## 5. Theory extensions and recursion categories, p. 20

- [**5.1 (Categories associated with internal theories, p. 20)**](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/04-theory-extensions.tex#L12) identifies $\mathscr C$ with $\operatorname{Par}(\mathcal D,\operatorname{Mono})$ under the partial-inverse hypothesis and equips $\mathscr C[U]=\operatorname{Par}(\mathcal D/U,\operatorname{Mono})$ with a Turing structure.

- [**5.2 (Functoriality under theory extension, p. 22)**](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/04-theory-extensions.tex#L131) sends a theory inclusion $i:T\hookrightarrow U$ to a fully faithful postcomposition functor with adjunctions $\Sigma_i\dashv i^*\dashv\Sigma_i$.

- [**5.3 (Essential incompleteness in the associated category, p. 23)**](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/04-theory-extensions.tex#L228) assumes initial derivation algebras in every parameter slice and a logical theorem $\lambda:1\to S$ factoring through every consistent theory. For consistent $u:U\hookrightarrow S$, the constant family $1_U\times u$ over $U$ is essentially incomplete exactly when $u$ is. Evaluation at the chosen factorization $1\to U$ recovers $u$. A total Boolean extension of the partial theorem/refutation map exists exactly when its two domains are separable.

## 6. The syntactic recursion category, p. 25

## 8. Essential incompleteness of arithmetic, p. 44

- [**8.1 (Comparison in the fixed syntactic category, p. 44)**](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/08-essential-incompleteness-of-arithmetic.tex#L3) applies diagonal comparison in the fixed syntactic category of PA. Di Paola–Heller, p. 595, item (3), obtain recursively inseparable theorem and refutation codes for a consistent arithmetical theory strongly representing every recursive set by positive and negative numeral instances. Corollary 8.4 deduces recursive inseparability for each consistent c.e. extension of PA by pulling a putative separator back along its sentence-code function.

- [**8.2 (An indexed family of arithmetic theories, p. 47)**](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/08a-uniform-theories.tex#L1) constructs an indexed family with fiber $\Gamma_e$ consisting of the PA axioms and the arithmetic sentences on whose codes program $e$ halts.

- [**8.3 (Uniform independent sentences, p. 50)**](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/08b-uniform-independent-sentences.tex#L1) fixes a c.e. base axiom set and a uniformly c.e. family of finitary rules in Pour-El’s presentation of effective extensibility. One total recursive function must supply an independent sentence for every c.e. addition preserving consistency. For PA the subsection constructs one total morphism $q:E\to S$ whose standard interpretation at every index $e$ with consistent $\Gamma_e$ codes a sentence independent of $\Gamma_e$ (Theorem 8.6).

- [**8.4 (The numerical realization, p. 51)**](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/08c-numerical-realization.tex#L1) realizes syntax and finite derivations numerically. With recursive supports for each symbol class, term and formula membership are decidable on *every* numerical word code (Proposition 8.8).

- [**8.5 (Numerical incompleteness, p. 57)**](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/08d-numerical-incompleteness.tex#L1) applies §4 to the ordinary numerical diagonal pair and the theorem domain of a consistent c.e. presentation containing the PA axioms.

- [**8.6 (Numeral instances and theory extension, p. 58)**](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/08-essential-incompleteness-of-arithmetic.tex#L292) sends each consistent c.e. extension $T$ of PA to the disjoint sets of numerals at which $T$ proves $\alpha$ and $\neg\alpha$, respectively. Theory inclusions induce the identity function on numeral indices.

- [**8.7 (The associated recursion categories, p. 59)**](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/08-essential-incompleteness-of-arithmetic.tex#L333) assumes PA consistent and applies §5 to the internal theory $t_{\pm}$ generated by the diagonal pair in the splitting of $S_{\mathrm{PA}}$ (Corollary 8.10).

## 9. Miller sequences and essential incompleteness, p. 61

Miller's existence theorem supplies a sequence $a$ such that, if program $d$ computes the same real $r$ to every requested precision from every representation of $a$, then $a(d)=r$. Bauer–Hanson use a Miller sequence to construct a parameterized realizability topos with countable Dedekind reals (their Theorem 6.4). Section 9 uses the sequence to construct a theory of ordered fields.

- [**9.1 (Computation independent of the binary representation, p. 61)**](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/04-miller-sequences.tex#L48) defines computations that give the same output from every binary representation of $a$.

- [**9.2 (The index sets below and above $1/2$, p. 63)**](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/04-miller-sequences.tex#L209) reduces the diagonal pair to the index sets $L_a=\{n:a_n<1/2\}$ and $R_a=\{n:a_n>1/2\}$. Their domains are inseparable in the category $\mathcal E_a$, although each individual representation computes a separator of the underlying sets.

- [**9.3 (A theory of real closed fields, p. 65)**](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/04-miller-sequences.tex#L328) adds constants $c_n$ and their strict rational bounds to real closed ordered fields. Every consistent extension in this language admitting a fixed axiom set semidecided by one oracle program from every representation has inseparable theorem and refutation domains (Theorem 9.7).

- [**9.4 (The associated recursion categories, p. 68)**](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/04-miller-sequences.tex#L654) proves essential incompleteness of the constant family for each consistent internal extension in its associated category (Corollary 9.8).

## Appendix C. Necessity of section lifting for inseparability, p. 93

Morphisms have ordinary partial-recursive extensions and domains c.e. relative to the halting set $K$. A faithful range functor into partial functions satisfies the preservation conditions of Lengyel's 2006 Theorem 4.1, yet a complementary pair separates the two diagonal domains. Disjoint parallel morphisms in this category can lack a common upper bound. The construction distinguishes the source-lifting hypothesis of Theorem 3.1 from preservation of products and reflection of zero.

## Appendix D. Faithful model interpretations and theory extension, p. 95

Taking every model of $T$ on the carrier $\mathbb N$ gives a faithful product interpretation of $S_T$ for consistent $T\supseteq\mathrm{PA}$.

- [**D.1 (Inseparable pairs under theory extension, p. 96)**](../../../manuscript/latex/appendices/d-faithful-family.tex#L91) follows inseparable diagonal domains under theory extension.

- [**D.2 (Provability of numeral instances, p. 97)**](../../../manuscript/latex/appendices/d-faithful-family.tex#L148) assumes that $T$ is c.e. and identifies $T\vdash C(\bar n)$ with $[C]_T\kappa_n=\kappa_n$ for the constant morphism $\kappa_n$, and with the corresponding equality under faithful interpretation.

- [**D.3 (The theory of the standard model, p. 97)**](../../../manuscript/latex/appendices/d-faithful-family.tex#L181) identifies $S_{\operatorname{Th}(\mathbb N)}$ with the numerical recursion category. The former has inseparable domains even though true arithmetic is complete.
