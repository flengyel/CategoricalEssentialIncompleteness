**Keep the nine-sentence abstract.** It states which theory a pair of domains generates, gives the criterion for essential incompleteness and explains its internal meaning without requiring the reader to learn notation for atoms, negation or the derivation algebra. ## Structure and source

The mathematical question is when the first-order theory generated by two disjoint domains, if consistent, admits a complete consistent extension. Sections 2–3 give the domain and diagonal constructions, §4 gives the adjunction and the criterion, and §5 proves the slice equivalence. Sections 6–7 construct and interpret the arithmetic category. Sections 8–10 give the arithmetic, Miller-sequence and three-point applications. Appendices A–E supply internal syntax and deduction, the cosheaf construction, a section-lifting counterexample, faithful model interpretations and a guide to selected proofs. The [v83 outline](OUTLINE.md) follows the recursive LaTeX inputs, with the titles and opening pages of all 60 subsections.

In §35 of Knuth, Larrabee and Roberts, Mary-Claire van Leunen explains that further compression can require changing the structure and omitting detail while preserving the meaning. In §36 her précis exercise asks for the content of an article or book in ten sentences or fewer. The limit belongs to the exercise. In §27 Herb Wilf asks that a prospective reader encounter the mathematical result early, in language that survives a quick reading. Section 1, items 5 and 13, distinguishes the need for self-contained theorem statements from readability when formulas are skimmed. The comments in §§4–5 allow formal definitions to follow motivation and place the symbols close to their use. ## Historical comparisons

- Di Paola and Heller’s section of $f:Y\rightharpoonup Z$ is $s:Z\rightharpoonup Y$ with $fs=\bar s$ and $fsf=f$. In the revised comparison the output of $\tau$ is $X+X$, and the universal property quantifies over $p$ of that same type. The conclusion explicitly requires $1_X\ne0_X$. Stating the cited coproduct assumptions separately makes the scope of the historical attribution legible. [§1.1](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/01-introduction.tex)
- Before Corollary 8.4, both implications $n\in B\Rightarrow H\vdash\beta_B(\bar n)$ and $n\notin B\Rightarrow H\vdash\neg\beta_B(\bar n)$ specify the meaning of definability. The next sentence identifies the distinct reduction used in the corollary. [§8.1](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/08-essential-incompleteness-of-arithmetic.tex#L239)
- Section 8.3 fixes a c.e. axiom set and a uniformly c.e. family of finitary rules before introducing $g$. Both nonderivability assertions use those rules and the same enlarged axiom set. The ensuing PA theorem gives a single total morphism. [Pour-El comparison and Theorem 8.6](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/08b-uniform-independent-sentences.tex#L4)
