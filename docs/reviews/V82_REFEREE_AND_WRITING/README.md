## (A) Kill-shots / hard blockers

No counterexample was found to a numbered result in the passages reviewed. There is a counterexample to reading internal incompleteness as the existence of an undecided global sentence solely because a subobject is proper. In the category of $G$-sets for a nontrivial group $G$, let $G$ act on itself by left multiplication. The summand inclusion $1\hookrightarrow 1+G$ is proper. Every equivariant map $1\to1+G$ lands in the first summand, since the regular action has no fixed point. The body already gives this example. It refutes the inference from properness to a point outside the subobject. Corollary 4.8 requires only properness. [§4.3](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/03a-internal-theories.tex#L244) · [Corollary 4.8](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/03b-internal-adjunctions.tex#L477)

## (B) Major weaknesses

1. **Abstract, first two paragraphs.** State the comparison on $\operatorname{Sub}(S_X)$. With $T$ consistent, its essential incompleteness means that for every closed $U$ in the fixed language with $T\le U$ and $U\wedge\nu_X^*U=\bot$, one has $U\vee\nu_X^*U\ne\top$. Pullback of a complete consistent extension along $\theta_X$ gives complementary subobjects separating $a,b$. Conversely, a separator supplies a total truth assignment on $X$ whose Boolean extension to the sentence object has a complete consistent truth subobject containing $T$. Neither implication requires that global points detect inclusions. Fix a common variable object for the adjunction and quantify over all objects of predicate symbols. [Abstract](../../../manuscript/latex/abstract.tex#L4) · [Proof of both implications](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46SignedSubobjectIncompleteness.lean#L43)
2. **Abstract, slice paragraph.** State which extensions are quantified over. For each consistent closed $u:U\hookrightarrow S$, the assertion is an equivalence between essential incompleteness of $u$ and essential incompleteness of $1_U\times u$ over $U$. A consistent complete extension $W\hookrightarrow U\times S$ can vary with the parameter. A factorization $u\lambda_U=\lambda$ gives an extension in the base by taking the inverse image of $W$ along $\langle\lambda_U!_S,1_S\rangle:S\to U\times S$. Restricting to constant $W$ would omit this part of the theorem. [Theorem 5.3](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/04-theory-extensions.tex#L260) · [Quantifiers in Lean](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46AssociatedIncompletenessChecks.lean#L29)
3. **§1, hypotheses of the historical diagonal argument.** The paper’s §2 defines application $U\times U\rightharpoonup U$. The cited passage needs $\tau:X\times X\rightharpoonup X+X$ with $\forall p:X\times X\rightharpoonup X+X,\ \exists k:X\to X\text{ total},\ p=\tau(k\times1_X)$. Di Paola–Heller’s Definition 4.1 accommodates this codomain. Their Theorem 8.15 assumes compatible coproducts in the sense of Definition 7.1. The Lean proof explicitly assumes the displayed factorization and takes the coproduct equations used in the proof. Giving it at first use removes the apparent type conflict and specifies which property of $\tau$ the diagonal argument uses. Retain $1_X\ne0_{X,X}$. In the one-morphism category the only idempotent is its own complement and separates the two diagonal domains. [§1](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/01-introduction.tex#L173) · [§2](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/02-categorical-preliminaries.tex#L192) · [Proof](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46DominicalLiteratureChecks.lean#L24) · [Degenerate example](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46TrivialDominicalCategoryChecks.lean)
