The defining inequality $U\vee\nu_X^*U\ne\top$ makes sense whether or not $\operatorname{Sub}(S_X)$ is detected by global points. An assertion that there exists a global sentence $s:1\to S_X$ undecided by $U$ would require a further argument. Consistency belongs among the hypotheses on $T$. ### Proposed abstract

In the setting of Di Paola and Heller's work on incompleteness, we characterize the disjoint domains that generate an essentially incomplete first-order theory. Let $\mathscr C$ be the restriction idempotent completion of a recursion category. Assume its total category $\mathcal D$ is regular with disjoint, pullback-stable finite coproducts and that each monomorphism in $\mathcal D$ has a partial inverse in $\mathscr C$. Fix a variable object with decidable equality. For each $X\in\mathcal D$, take the language whose only nonlogical symbols are nullary predicates indexed by $X$. Assume its syntax and variable-list algebras are initial in every parameter slice. In each presentation under consideration, assume an initial derivation algebra for every axiom subobject.

Write $\theta_X:X\to S_X$ for atomic sentence formation and $\nu_X:S_X\to S_X$ for negation. An index $x:W\to X$ gives the sentence $\theta_Xx:W\to S_X$. Given disjoint subobjects $a,b$ with inclusions $i_a:A\hookrightarrow X$ and $i_b:B\hookrightarrow X$, let $T$ be the deductive closure of $\operatorname{im}(\theta_Xi_a)\vee\operatorname{im}(\nu_X\theta_Xi_b)$. Suppose $T$ is consistent. Then $T$ is essentially incomplete if and only if no complementary subobjects of $X$ contain $a$ and $b$, respectively. Here essential incompleteness means that every deductively closed extension $U$ in this language satisfying $U\wedge\nu_X^*U=\bot$ satisfies $U\vee\nu_X^*U\ne\top$. On pairs generating consistent theories, the construction is left adjoint to the functor sending a consistent theory $H\hookrightarrow S$ with negation $\nu:S\to S$ to $(S,H,\nu^*H)$.

For a consistent closed theory $u:U\hookrightarrow S$, partial maps in $\mathcal D/U$ form a Turing category. Suppose the parameterized rules admit an initial derivation algebra for each axiom subobject of $P\times S\to P$ in every $\mathcal D/P$, and a fixed logical theorem $\lambda:1\to S$ factors through every consistent theory. Then $u$ is essentially incomplete if and only if its constant family $1_U\times u:U\times U\hookrightarrow U\times S$ is essentially incomplete with respect to all consistent closed extensions over $U$.

For Peano arithmetic (PA), let $\Gamma_e$ consist of the PA axioms and the sentences on whose codes program $e$ halts. In the completed syntactic category for PA there is a total morphism $q:E\to S$ from the numerical object to the sentence object. Its numerical interpretation sends every $e$ with $\Gamma_e$ consistent to the code of a sentence independent of $\Gamma_e$. For a Miller sequence $a$, form $T_a$ by adjoining constants $c_n$ to real closed ordered fields, with axioms $r<c_n$ for $r<a(n)$ and $c_n<r$ for $a(n)<r$, where $r\in\mathbb Q$. Every consistent extension of $T_a$ in this language is incomplete if it has an axiom set that one oracle program semidecides from every binary representation of $a$. In a cosheaf on a three-point space, each endpoint carries a consistent propositional theory. Every consistent extension of either endpoint theory with a computably enumerable axiom set in its language is incomplete.

### Proposed body wording

**§1, related work.** Di Paola and Heller’s Theorem 8.15 concerns a dominical category in which every partial morphism $f$ has a section $s$ with $fs=\bar s$ and $fsf=f$. Their coproduct $f+g$ is total whenever $f$ and $g$ are total, and the canonical maps $(X\times Y)+(X\times Z)\to X\times(Y+Z)$ are isomorphisms. Let $\tau:X\times X\rightharpoonup X+X$ have the following property. For each $p:X\times X\rightharpoonup X+X$, some total $k:X\to X$ satisfies $p=\tau(k\times1_X)$. Put $h=\tau\Delta_X$. With binary coproducts and the stated sections, the diagonal argument shows that a complementary separator of $h^*(1_X+0_X)$ and $h^*(0_X+1_X)$ forces $1_X=0_X$. Thus the two domains are inseparable when $1_X\ne0_X$. [Historical hypotheses and formal comparison](CORRESPONDENCE.md)

**Before Corollary 8.4.** Di Paola and Heller record the following implication for a consistent arithmetical theory $H$ (p. 595, item (3)). Suppose every recursive set $B\subseteq\mathbb N$ has a representing formula $\beta_B(x)$ such that $H\vdash\beta_B(\bar n)$ for $n\in B$ and $H\vdash\neg\beta_B(\bar n)$ for $n\notin B$. Then the theorem and refutation sets of $H$ are recursively inseparable. For a c.e. extension of PA, the function $\Theta$ sends $D_0$ into its theorem set and $D_1$ into its refutation set by Lemma 8.1.

**§8.3.** Fix a presentation of a consistent theory with c.e. axioms $A$ and a uniformly c.e. family $R$ of finitary inference rules. In Pour-El’s terminology the presentation is effectively extensible if there is a total recursive $g:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N$ with the following property. If $e$ indexes a c.e. set $W_e$ of sentences in the same language and $A\cup W_e$ is consistent under $R$, then $g(e)$ codes a sentence $\varphi_e$ for which $A\cup W_e\nvdash_R\varphi_e$ and $A\cup W_e\nvdash_R\neg\varphi_e$. For the chosen PA presentation we obtain the assignment from one morphism in the completed syntactic category.
