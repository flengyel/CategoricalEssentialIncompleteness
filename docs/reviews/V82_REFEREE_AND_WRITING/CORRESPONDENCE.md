### Corollary 4.8 and the binary joins of domains

The subobject formulation has a direct declaration at the printed hypotheses. The domain formulation in Lean takes an explicit instance of `BinaryJoins C`. In the abstract's setting every monomorphism in $\mathcal D$ has a partial inverse in $\mathscr C$. For each $X$ the proved order isomorphism $\Phi_X:\operatorname{Sub}(X)\cong\operatorname{Idem}(X)$ therefore transports the subobject join to the least upper bound of $e,f$:
$$e\vee f=\Phi_X\bigl(\Phi_X^{-1}(e)\vee\Phi_X^{-1}(f)\bigr).$$
The two inclusions and leastness follow by applying $\Phi_X^{-1}$ to the corresponding inequalities. Restriction zeros are inherited by the splitting. Under these identifications, complementarity and absence of a separator agree on the two sides. [Order isomorphism](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46RestrictionSubobjects.lean#L124) · [Restriction zeros](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/RestrictionSplitting.lean#L77) · [Transport of inseparability](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46RestrictionSubobjectInseparability.lean#L49)

**Exact WSL2 check.** For $\mathscr C=\operatorname{Split}(\mathscr R)$ with the printed regularity, finite extensivity and partial inverses, instantiate the domain formulation using the induced joins, without a separate input asserting their existence. Identify the declaration or assemble the instance by the displayed formula and strictly elaborate the resulting implication in both directions for $F,a,b$, disjointness and consistency. This review located the order isomorphism and the subobject and domain theorems individually. The [instance on the splitting](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/RestrictionSplitting.lean#L139) already assumes joins in the base, and the [instance from ranges](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/RestrictionRanges.lean#L97) assumes ranges and coproducts. ### §1.1. Di Paola–Heller

Definition 4.1 (p. 601) specializes to the following property of $\tau:X\times X\rightharpoonup X+X$. For each $p$ of the same type, some total $k:X\to X$ satisfies $p=\tau(k\times1_X)$. Theorem 8.15 (pp. 625–626) assumes a $c^+$-dominical category. Every $f:Y\rightharpoonup Z$ has a partial section $s:Z\rightharpoonup Y$ with $fs=\bar s$ and $fsf=f$. Binary coproducts send pairs of total morphisms to total morphisms, and the canonical distributivity maps $(X\times Y)+(X\times Z)\to X\times(Y+Z)$ are isomorphisms (Definitions 6.1–6.2, p. 612 and 7.1, p. 615). With $h=\tau\Delta_X$, the two domains $h^*(1_X+0_X)$ and $h^*(0_X+1_X)$ have meet zero. A complementary separator gives $1_X=0_X$, so $1_X\ne0_X$ excludes it. The published statement omits the nonzero condition. [Original article](https://doi.org/10.2307/2274352) · [Manuscript](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/01-introduction.tex#L173)

Lean's `nonzero_turing_object` quantifies over the fixed $\tau$ and its total parameter morphisms. Its coproduct class requires bifunctoriality, injection, codiagonal and naturality equations. The declaration does not take the cited distributivity isomorphism as an input. Distinguish the historical hypotheses from this proof of the implication at the paper’s hypotheses. In the one-object, one-morphism category the sole idempotent is both top and bottom, hence separates the two equal domains. ### §8.1. Before Corollary 8.4

On p. 595, item (3), the cited implication starts from a consistent arithmetical theory $H$. For each recursive $B\subseteq\mathbb N$ require a formula $\beta_B(x)$ such that $n\in B\Rightarrow H\vdash\beta_B(\bar n)$ and $n\notin B\Rightarrow H\vdash\neg\beta_B(\bar n)$. Its conclusion is recursive inseparability of the theorem and refutation codes of $H$. The Lean proof uses both clauses of strong numeralwise representation. The corollary’s proof instead takes a recursive separator $R$ of the theorem and refutation codes of a consistent c.e. extension of PA and obtains $\Theta^{-1}R$ separating $D_0,D_1$. Thus the broad historical implication and the numerical reduction for the paper's corollary have distinct hypotheses and proofs. ### §8.3. Effective extensibility

For a fixed presentation $(A,R)$, take one total recursive $g:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N$. For every $e$ with $W_e$ a set of sentences and $A\cup W_e$ consistent under $R$, $g(e)$ must code a sentence and neither it nor its negation is derivable under $R$ from $A\cup W_e$. Pour-El specifies a c.e. axiom set and an effectively enumerable sequence of c.e. finitary inference relations. A theory is effectively extensible if it admits a presentation with the property. The same predicate makes sense on more general coded presentations. Fixing $(A,R)$ corrects the original quantifier. Taking the empty extension of a complete consistent theory shows that consistency alone cannot produce $g$. The PA-specific morphism and its two nonderivability clauses are compared above. [§8.3](../../../manuscript/latex/sections/08b-uniform-independent-sentences.tex#L4) · [Complete consistent case](../../../Lean/CategoricalEssentialIncompleteness/V46EffectiveExtensibilityChecks.lean#L20) · [Pour-El, 1968](https://doi.org/10.2307/2270052) · [Pour-El, 1970, p. 274](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~yytran/PourEl_recursion_axiomatizable_theories.pdf#page=9)

**Proposition 8.8, proof.** For a fixed finite arity bound $d$, suppose every function- and relation-symbol support through arity $d$ has a recursive membership predicate. Then there exist total computable functions $t,f:\mathbb N\to\{0,1\}$ such that, for every numerical word code $w$, $t(w)=1$ precisely for the term carrier and $f(w)=1$ precisely for the formula carrier. Both equivalences address all input codes, including ill-formed words. A procedure requiring a prior proof of well-formedness would not establish this assertion. The strict check specializes the two numerical predicates to the actual postfix carriers. ### Appendix C. Specializing an oracle-program index

The ordinary total recursive $s:\mathbb N^2\to\mathbb N$ has, for every oracle $B\subseteq\mathbb N$ and every $e,z,x$, the defining equation $x\in W^B_{s(e,z)}\iff\langle z,x\rangle\in W^B_e$. For a partial map $v$ in the constructed category, take its ordinary partial recursive extension $v_0$ and one index $c\in\mathbb N$ for its entire coded domain relative to $K$. Let $t(z)$ specialize $v_0$ at $z$. Then $r(z)=\langle t(z),s(c,z)\rangle$ is total recursive. At $(z,x)$ the specialized oracle domain agrees with $D_v$, and on that domain the ordinary computation agrees with $v(z,x)$. Hence $v=\tau_K(r\times1_{\mathbb N})$ as partial morphisms. The diagonal $d=\tau_K\Delta_{\mathbb N}:\mathbb N\rightharpoonup\mathbb N$ used later has a different type from $c$.
